Part IThe Cosmic Frame (Ādi Parva)

Mahābhārata 1.1.27–29, 36–38

निष्प्रभेऽस्मिन्निरालोके सर्वतस्तमसावृते ।niṣprabhe 'smin nirāloke sarvatas tamasāvṛte बृहदण्डमभूदेकं प्रजानां बीजमक्षयम् ॥ २७ ॥bṛhad aṇḍam abhūd ekaṃ prajānāṃ bījam akṣayam युगस्यादौ निमित्तं तन्महद्दिव्यं प्रचक्षते ।yugasyādau nimittaṃ tan mahad divyaṃ pracakṣate यस्मिंस्तच्छ्रूयते सत्यं ज्योतिर्ब्रह्म सनातनम् ॥ २८ ॥yasmiṃs tac chrūyate satyaṃ jyotir brahma sanātanam अद्भुतं चाप्यचिन्त्यं च सर्वत्र समतां गतम् ।adbhutaṃ cāpy acintyaṃ ca sarvatra samatāṃ gatam अव्यक्तं कारणं सूक्ष्मं यत्तत्सदसदात्मकम् ॥ २९ ॥avyaktaṃ kāraṇaṃ sūkṣmaṃ yat tat sadasadātmakam … यदिदं दृश्यते किं चिद्भूतं स्थावरजङ्गमम् ।yad idaṃ dṛśyate kiṃ cid bhūtaṃ sthāvarajaṅgamam पुनः संक्षिप्यते सर्वं जगत्प्राप्ते युगक्षये ॥ ३६ ॥punaḥ saṃkṣipyate sarvaṃ jagat prāpte yugakṣaye यथर्तावृतुलिङ्गानि नानारूपाणि पर्यये ।yathartāv ṛtuliṅgāni nānārūpāṇi paryaye दृश्यन्ते तानि तान्येव तथा भावा युगादिषु ॥ ३७ ॥dṛśyante tāni tāny eva tathā bhāvā yugādiṣu एवमेतदनाद्यन्तं भूतसंहारकारकम् ।evam etad anādyantaṃ bhūtasaṃhārakārakam अनादिनिधनं लोके चक्रं संपरिवर्तते ॥ ३८ ॥anādinidhanaṃ loke cakraṃ saṃparivartate

When this world lay without radiance, without light, shrouded on every side in darkness, there came to be one great egg — the inexhaustible seed of all creatures. They call it the great and divine cause at the beginning of the yuga, and within it, we are told, was the true light, the eternal brahman: wondrous and unthinkable, spread evenly through everything, the unmanifest and subtle cause whose nature is both what is and what is not. … Whatever is seen here — any being at all, standing still or moving — the whole world is drawn back together again when the yuga comes to its ruin; and as the marks of the seasons, various in shape, appear again with each turning — those very same marks — so do the states of being appear again at the start of each yuga. Thus this wheel, without beginning or end, maker of the gathering-in of all beings, without origin and without extinction, turns and turns again in the world.

Out of that beginningless turning the poem draws its largest claim: that it holds, within itself, everything worth holding.

Mahābhārata 1.56.33

धर्मे चार्थे च कामे च मोक्षे च भरतर्षभ ।dharme cārthe ca kāme ca mokṣe ca bharatarṣabha यदिहास्ति तदन्यत्र यन्नेहास्ति न तत्क्व चित् ॥ ३३ ॥yad ihāsti tad anyatra yan nehāsti na tat kva cit

In dharma, and in wealth, and in desire, and in liberation, O bull of the Bharatas — what is here is found elsewhere; what is not here is nowhere at all.

If the poem inventories dharma, it must also show how dharma acts. The sage Śamīka gives its mechanism, rebuking his son Śṛṅgin, who has cursed the king to die for hanging a dead snake on his silent father’s neck.

Mahābhārata 1.37.20cd–24

न मे प्रियं कृतं तात नैष धर्मस्तपस्विनाम् ॥ २० ॥na me priyaṃ kṛtaṃ tāta naiṣa dharmas tapasvinām वयं तस्य नरेन्द्रस्य विषये निवसामहे ।vayaṃ tasya narendrasya viṣaye nivasāmahe न्यायतो रक्षितास्तेन तस्य पापं न रोचये ॥ २१ ॥nyāyato rakṣitās tena tasya pāpaṃ na rocaye सर्वथा वर्तमानस्य राज्ञो ह्यस्मद्विधैः सदा ।sarvathā vartamānasya rājño hy asmadvidhaiḥ sadā क्षन्तव्यं पुत्र धर्मो हि हतो हन्ति न संशयः ॥ २२ ॥kṣantavyaṃ putra dharmo hi hato hanti na saṃśayaḥ यदि राजा न रक्षेत पीडा वै नः परा भवेत् ।yadi rājā na rakṣeta pīḍā vai naḥ parā bhavet न शक्नुयाम चरितुं धर्मं पुत्र यथासुखम् ॥ २३ ॥na śaknuyāma carituṃ dharmaṃ putra yathāsukham रक्ष्यमाणा वयं तात राजभिः शास्त्रदृष्टिभिः ।rakṣyamāṇā vayaṃ tāta rājabhiḥ śāstradṛṣṭibhiḥ चरामो विपुलं धर्मं तेषां चांशोऽस्ति धर्मतः ॥ २४ ॥carāmo vipulaṃ dharmaṃ teṣāṃ cāṃśo 'sti dharmataḥ

“You have not done what pleases me, child; this is not the dharma of ascetics. We dwell within that king’s domain; protected by him in right order, I do not approve of evil done to him. Whatever a king may do, such as we must always forgive him, son — for dharma, struck down, strikes down: there is no doubt of it. If the king did not protect us, great affliction would be ours; we could not practice dharma at our ease, son. Protected by kings whose eyes are the treatises, we practice dharma in abundance — and of that dharma, by dharma, a share belongs to them.”

Dharmo hi hato hanti — “dharma, struck down, strikes down” — states the recoil as a plain physical fact, not a judgment handed down from anywhere: the violated order strikes back of itself.

Note. Tasya pāpaṃ can mean evil done to the king or the king’s own offence. The next verse, that a king must be forgiven whatever he does, favours the first reading; the second (“I take no pleasure in his offence, yet he must be forgiven”) is also possible.

That recoil operates at the largest scale of all, because the war that will one day fill this poem was arranged in heaven before its fighters were born. The earth herself asked for it.

Mahābhārata 1.58.29, 36–37, 44, 46, 47ef

जातैरिह महीपाल जायमानैश्च तैर्मही ।jātair iha mahīpāla jāyamānaiś ca tair mahī न शशाकात्मनात्मानमियं धारयितुं धरा ॥ २९ ॥na śaśākātmanātmānam iyaṃ dhārayituṃ dharā … न हीमां पवनो राजन्न नागा न नगा महीम् ।na hīmāṃ pavano rājan na nāgā na nagā mahīm तदा धारयितुं शेकुराक्रान्तां दानवैर्बलात् ॥ ३६ ॥tadā dhārayituṃ śekur ākrāntāṃ dānavair balāt ततो मही महीपाल भारार्ता भयपीडिता ।tato mahī mahīpāla bhārārtā bhayapīḍitā जगाम शरणं देवं सर्वभूतपितामहम् ॥ ३७ ॥jagāma śaraṇaṃ devaṃ sarvabhūtapitāmaham … यदर्थमसि संप्राप्ता मत्सकाशं वसुंधरे ।yadartham asi saṃprāptā matsakāśaṃ vasuṃdhare तदर्थं संनियोक्ष्यामि सर्वानेव दिवौकसः ॥ ४४ ॥tadarthaṃ saṃniyokṣyāmi sarvān eva divaukasaḥ … अस्या भूमेर्निरसितुं भारं भागैः पृथक्पृथक् ।asyā bhūmer nirasituṃ bhāraṃ bhāgaiḥ pṛthak pṛthak अस्यामेव प्रसूयध्वं विरोधायेति चाब्रवीत् ॥ ४६ ॥asyām eva prasūyadhvaṃ virodhāyeti cābravīt … स्वैरंशैः संप्रसूयध्वं यथेष्टं मानुषेष्विति ॥ ४७ ॥svair aṃśaiḥ saṃprasūyadhvaṃ yatheṣṭaṃ mānuṣeṣv iti

With those demons born here, and still being born, O protector of the earth, this earth — the Bearer — was no longer able by herself to bear herself. … Neither wind, O king, nor serpents, nor mountains could then bear up this earth, overrun by force by the Dānavas. Then the earth, O protector of the earth, oppressed by the burden and afflicted with fear, went for refuge to the god who is grandfather of all beings. … “For the very purpose that has brought you into my presence, Earth, bearer of treasure — for that same purpose I will set all the dwellers of heaven to their task.” … “To cast off this earth’s burden, each by his own portion — be born upon her, for the sake of the coming conflict,” he said. … “By your own portions, be born among mortals as you please.”

The earth’s own name, dharā, “the Bearer,” shares its root with dhārayitum, “to bear,” and with dharma itself — the same root, √dhṛ. What fails here is not her virtue but her capacity to hold; the remedy is aṃśāvataraṇa, the gods’ descent “by portions” into human birth, undertaken expressly virodhāya, “for conflict.” The war to come is not an accident that better choices might have averted — it is the purpose for which its participants were born.

Circular time in its exhaustion phase; dharma as automatic recoil; a world made too heavy to stand; and gods walking down into flesh to bring the weight off. The frame is set. What follows built upon it — the dice, the hall, the eighteen days — belongs to another telling.

IThe Weight of the World

Ādi Parva 1.58

Janamejaya, at whose snake-sacrifice the epic is being recited, asks his teacher why the heroes he has been hearing about were born at all.

1.58.2

यदर्थमिह संभूता देवकल्पा महारथाः ।yadartham iha saṃbhūtā devakalpā mahārathāḥ भुवि तन्मे महाभाग सम्यगाख्यातुमर्हसि ॥ २ ॥bhuvi tan me mahābhāga samyag ākhyātum arhasi

“For what purpose were these godlike great chariot-warriors brought into being on the earth? Tell me that, fortunate one, and tell it rightly.”

Vaiśaṃpāyana answers that the thing being asked about is a secret belonging to the gods, which he will tell only after bowing to the Self-Existent. He begins far back. Rāma son of Jamadagni had emptied the earth of its warrior class twenty-one times over and gone to Mount Mahendra to do austerities. With the kṣatriyas gone, kṣatriya women went to brahmins wanting children, and the brahmins came to them.

1.58.6

ताभिः सह समापेतुर्ब्राह्मणाः संशितव्रताः ।tābhiḥ saha samāpetur brāhmaṇāḥ saṃśitavratāḥ ऋतावृतौ नरव्याघ्र न कामान्नानृतौ तथा ॥ ६ ॥ṛtāv ṛtau naravyāghra na kāmān nānṛtau tathā

“Brahmins of severe vows came together with them season by fertile season, tiger among men — not out of desire, and not out of season.”

Thousands of warrior-caste women conceived by them and bore sons and daughters, and the warrior class was restored — restored dharmeṇa, by dharma, and long-lived. Kings punished only the punishable, and by dharma. Indra rained sweetly, in the right place at the right time. Brahmins studied the Vedas and did not sell their learning. Farmers did not yoke thin oxen. Merchants did not weigh with false weights.

1.58.20cd–22

न कूटमानैर्वणिजः पण्यं विक्रीणते तदा ॥ २० ॥na kūṭamānair vaṇijaḥ paṇyaṃ vikrīṇate tadā कर्माणि च नरव्याघ्र धर्मोपेतानि मानवाः ।karmāṇi ca naravyāghra dharmopetāni mānavāḥ धर्ममेवानुपश्यन्तश्चक्रुर्धर्मपरायणाः ॥ २१ ॥dharmam evānupaśyantaś cakrur dharmaparāyaṇāḥ स्वकर्मनिरताश्चासन्सर्वे वर्णा नराधिप ।svakarmaniratāś cāsan sarve varṇā narādhipa एवं तदा नरव्याघ्र धर्मो न ह्रसते क्व चित् ॥ २२ ॥evaṃ tadā naravyāghra dharmo na hrasate kva cit

“Then the traders did not sell their goods by false measures. Human beings did their works joined to dharma; looking upon dharma alone, devoted to dharma, they acted. Every class delighted in its own proper work, O king — and so, tiger among men, dharma did not diminish anywhere.”

Cows calved on time and women bore on time; trees flowered and fruited in their seasons; nobody died a child.

1.58.24

एवं कृतयुगे सम्यग्वर्तमाने तदा नृप ।evaṃ kṛtayuge samyag vartamāne tadā nṛpa आपूर्यत मही कृत्स्ना प्राणिभिर्बहुभिर्भृशम् ॥ २४ ॥āpūryata mahī kṛtsnā prāṇibhir bahubhir bhṛśam

“Thus, while the Kṛta age was going correctly, the whole earth was filled — filled to bursting — with living beings.”

Into that saturated world the demons came, not by invasion but by birth. Defeated repeatedly by the Ādityas and thrown down from their sovereignty, the asuras began to be born in the wombs of kings.

1.58.25–26, 28

ततः समुदिते लोके मानुषे भरतर्षभ ।tataḥ samudite loke mānuṣe bharatarṣabha असुरा जज्ञिरे क्षेत्रे राज्ञां मनुजपुंगव ॥ २५ ॥asurā jajñire kṣetre rājñāṃ manujapuṃgava आदित्यैर्हि तदा दैत्या बहुशो निर्जिता युधि ।ādityair hi tadā daityā bahuśo nirjitā yudhi ऐश्वर्याद्भ्रंशिताश्चापि संबभूवुः क्षिताविह ॥ २६ ॥aiśvaryād bhraṃśitāś cāpi saṃbabhūvuḥ kṣitāv iha … गोष्वश्वेषु च राजेन्द्र खरोष्ट्रमहिषेषु च ।goṣv aśveṣu ca rājendra kharoṣṭramahiṣeṣu ca क्रव्यादेषु च भूतेषु गजेषु च मृगेषु च ॥ २८ ॥kravyādeṣu ca bhūteṣu gajeṣu ca mṛgeṣu ca

“Then, when the human world had risen to its height, bull of the Bharatas, the asuras were born in the field of kings. Beaten again and again in battle by the Ādityas, thrown down from their lordship, they came to be here on the earth. … Among cattle and horses, among asses and camels and buffalo, among the flesh-eating creatures, among elephants and among deer.”

1.58.29

जातैरिह महीपाल जायमानैश्च तैर्मही ।jātair iha mahīpāla jāyamānaiś ca tair mahī न शशाकात्मनात्मानमियं धारयितुं धरा ॥ २९ ॥na śaśākātmanātmānam iyaṃ dhārayituṃ dharā

“With those already born here and those still being born, O protector of the earth — this earth, the Bearer, was no longer able by herself to bear herself up.”

Dharā, “she who bears,” failing at dhārayitum, “to bear” — both from the root that gives dharma, “that which holds up.”

Proud, strong, wearing whatever forms they chose, the demons went over the sea-bounded earth crushing brahmin, kṣatriya, vaiśya and śūdra alike; they terrified and slaughtered and roamed by the hundred thousand; they violated the great seers in their hermitages, irreverent, “intoxicated with their valour, mad with the force of that intoxication” (abrahmaṇyā vīryamadā mattā madabalena ca, 1.58.34).

1.58.36–37

न हीमां पवनो राजन्न नागा न नगा महीम् ।na hīmāṃ pavano rājan na nāgā na nagā mahīm तदा धारयितुं शेकुराक्रान्तां दानवैर्बलात् ॥ ३६ ॥tadā dhārayituṃ śekur ākrāntāṃ dānavair balāt ततो मही महीपाल भारार्ता भयपीडिता ।tato mahī mahīpāla bhārārtā bhayapīḍitā जगाम शरणं देवं सर्वभूतपितामहम् ॥ ३७ ॥jagāma śaraṇaṃ devaṃ sarvabhūtapitāmaham

“The wind could not hold this earth up, king; the world-serpents could not; the mountains could not — overrun by the Dānavas, and overrun by force. Then the earth, pained by her burden and crushed by fear, went for refuge to the god who is grandfather of all beings.”

She found Brahmā surrounded by gods and seers, and bowed and made her petition.

1.58.41–42

तत्प्रधानात्मनस्तस्य भूमेः कृत्यं स्वयंभुवः ।tat pradhānātmanas tasya bhūmeḥ kṛtyaṃ svayaṃbhuvaḥ पूर्वमेवाभवद्राजन्विदितं परमेष्ठिनः ॥ ४१ ॥pūrvam evābhavad rājan viditaṃ parameṣṭhinaḥ स्रष्टा हि जगतः कस्मान्न संबुध्येत भारत ।sraṣṭā hi jagataḥ kasmān na saṃbudhyeta bhārata सुरासुराणां लोकानामशेषेण मनोगतम् ॥ ४२ ॥surāsurāṇāṃ lokānām aśeṣeṇa manogatam

“The earth’s business was already known beforehand to that Self-Existent one, whose nature is the first principle, the Highest-Standing. For why would the maker of the world not know, Bhārata — completely, and without remainder — what is in the minds of the gods, the asuras, and the worlds?”

Brahmā’s answer is short.

1.58.44

यदर्थमसि संप्राप्ता मत्सकाशं वसुंधरे ।yadartham asi saṃprāptā matsakāśaṃ vasuṃdhare तदर्थं संनियोक्ष्यामि सर्वानेव दिवौकसः ॥ ४४ ॥tadarthaṃ saṃniyokṣyāmi sarvān eva divaukasaḥ

“For the purpose for which you have come into my presence, treasure-bearing one — for that purpose I shall commission every last one of the dwellers in heaven.”

He dismissed the earth and gave the order.

Note. The order at 1.58.47 is addressed to the gandharvas and apsarases, gathered first (47a), and not only to the gods; the ellipsis before 47e hides the change of addressee.

1.58.46, 47ef

अस्या भूमेर्निरसितुं भारं भागैः पृथक्पृथक् ।asyā bhūmer nirasituṃ bhāraṃ bhāgaiḥ pṛthak pṛthak अस्यामेव प्रसूयध्वं विरोधायेति चाब्रवीत् ॥ ४६ ॥asyām eva prasūyadhvaṃ virodhāyeti cābravīt … स्वैरंशैः संप्रसूयध्वं यथेष्टं मानुषेष्विति ॥ ४७ ॥svair aṃśaiḥ saṃprasūyadhvaṃ yatheṣṭaṃ mānuṣeṣv iti

“To cast off the burden of this earth, each by his own separate portion — be born upon her, for conflict. So he said. … By your own portions be born among human beings, as you please.”

Virodhāya — for conflict, for collision. That is the stated purpose, given as an instruction, in advance. Śakra and the rest heard the word of the guru of the gods and took it up as true, as advantageous, and as wholesome, and went, ready to descend each by his own portion, to Nārāyaṇa, the slayer of foes, Vaikuṇṭha himself.

1.58.51

तं भुवः शोधनायेन्द्र उवाच पुरुषोत्तमम् ।taṃ bhuvaḥ śodhanāyendra uvāca puruṣottamam अंशेनावतरस्वेति तथेत्याह च तं हरिः ॥ ५१ ॥aṃśenāvatarasveti tathety āha ca taṃ hariḥ

“For the cleansing of the earth Indra said to the highest of persons, ‘Descend by a portion’ — and Hari said to him, ‘So be it.’”

The Descent by Portions

Ādi Parva 1.61, 1.113–115

The roster of who is whose portion, and then the scene of the actual births.

Janamejaya wants the list. Vaiśaṃpāyana gives it, and it runs for a hundred verses — this demon became that king, this Kāleya became that lord of the Niṣādas — before it reaches the people of the story.

Droṇa, teacher of both sides, is a portion of Bṛhaspati, begotten from no womb. His son comes of a stranger source.

“From Mahādeva and from Death, from Desire and from Anger — from those four gone into a single unity — was born the hero, the scorcher of foes: Aśvatthāman of great strength, the annihilator of the enemy’s side.”

The eight Vasus were born as Gaṅgā’s sons by Śaṃtanu, and the last-born of them is Bhīṣma. Kṛpa is out of the host of the Rudras. And then, with no comment at all:

“The king called Śakuni, the great chariot-warrior in the world — know him, O king, to have come into being as Dvāpara: the crusher of enemies.”

Dvāpara is the third age of the world, and also the next-to-worst throw of the dice; the losing throw is kali. His nephew is worse.

“From a portion of Kali was born on earth the king Duryodhana — ill-minded, ill-judging, the unmaker of the Kurus’ fame; hated by all the world, Kali in the shape of a man, the lowest of men, who had the whole earth slaughtered, by whom was kindled that great enmity that makes an end of beings.”

His hundred brothers, Duḥśāsana at their head, are rākṣasas of Pulastya’s line. Dhṛtarāṣṭra is a gandharva king born blind of his mother’s fault and a seer’s anger. Vidura is the son of Atri, wisest of the wise. And then the five:

“Know the king Yudhiṣṭhira to be the portion of Dharma; Bhīmasena of the Wind; Arjuna of the king of the gods. And the two portions of the Aśvins, unmatched on earth in their beauty — Nakula and Sahadeva, the delight of all the world.”

Karṇa is the portion of the Sun; Kṛṣṇa, of Nārāyaṇa himself; Dhṛṣṭadyumna, of Fire; Śikhaṇḍin, a rākṣasa, and strīpuṃsa — woman-and-man.

“A portion of Śrī — of Fortune herself — was born on the surface of the earth for the sake of delight: a blameless girl in Drupada’s line, out of the middle of the sacrificial altar. Not too short, not too tall, fragrant as the blue lotus, long-eyed as the lotus, fine-hipped, with long dark hair; endowed with every auspicious mark, like a beryl gem; the churner, in private, of the hearts of five lords of men.”

Siddhi and Dhṛti, Success and Steadfastness, were born as Kuntī and Mādrī; Mati, Judgment, was born as Subala’s daughter, Gāndhārī. Vaiśaṃpāyana closes: “Thus has the descent by portions been recited” — and adds that the wise man who hears it “does not sink down in hard times.”

By the time we reach 1.113, Pāṇḍu has been cursed to die if he ever approaches a woman in desire. He has no sons. He argues Kuntī toward a solution, raising joined hands to his head as he pleads (1.113.29).

“— especially a man greedy for a son, cut off himself from begetting, as I am, woman of flawless limbs, longing to look at a son. … At my appointment, then, fair-tressed one, by a twice-born man superior in austerity, you should bring forth sons endowed with good qualities. Kuntī answers with a secret she has carried since she was a girl in her father’s house.

“The one they know as Durvāsas, whose resolve in dharma is hidden — that man of honed self I satisfied with every effort. And the blessed one declared to me a boon joined with power of compulsion, and gave me a body of mantras, and said this to me: ‘Whichever god you summon with this mantra, willing or unwilling, that god will come into your power.’”

She asks Pāṇḍu’s permission and asks him to choose which god. He answers at once.

“This very day, prepare yourself according to the rule. Summon Dharma, auspicious one — for he is the one among the gods who holds the share of merit. In no way let our dharma be joined to adharma; and this world, too, will judge the thing to be dharma.”

The births. In the year when Gāndhārī’s own pregnancy is already a year old, Kuntī recites the mantra and joins with Dharma in his yoga-body.

“When Wolf-belly had been born, Pāṇḍu thought further: ‘How might I have a still better son, the best in the world?’ — For this world stands upon two things: on daiva, and on the effort of a man. Of these, daiva is obtained by ordinance joined with the right time.”

So he and Kuntī kept a year’s vow, and at length Indra spoke to him and promised a son famed in the three worlds; Kuntī summoned Indra, and Arjuna was born. The prophecy that follows names, among other things, the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest and the recovery of a vanished fortune: “and this bull among men will be the one who brings back the fortune that was utterly lost.”

The last two are Mādrī’s. She tells Pāṇḍu she cannot bear being childless while her co-wife has children; Pāṇḍu argues Kuntī into sharing the mantra once; Mādrī thinks of the Aśvins, both of them, and bears twins, Nakula and Sahadeva. When Pāṇḍu presses Kuntī to let her do it again, Kuntī refuses.

IWhat Duryodhana Saw

Sabhā Parva 2.43–45, 2.50–51

After the Rājasūya, in the assembly hall Maya built.

Between the last passage and this one lie the childhood rivalries, the house of lac, the exile, the winning of Draupadī, the partition of the kingdom, the burning of the Khāṇḍava forest, and Yudhiṣṭhira’s imperial consecration.

Duryodhana had been staying in the hall, walking through it slowly with Śakuni, seeing marvels he had never seen in Hāstinapura.

2.43.3–5

स कदा चित्सभामध्ये धार्तराष्ट्रो महीपतिः ।sa kadā cit sabhāmadhye dhārtarāṣṭro mahīpatiḥ स्फाटिकं तलमासाद्य जलमित्यभिशङ्कया ॥ ३ ॥sphāṭikaṃ talam āsādya jalam ity abhiśaṅkayā स्ववस्त्रोत्कर्षणं राजा कृतवान्बुद्धिमोहितः ।svavastrotkarṣaṇaṃ rājā kṛtavān buddhimohitaḥ दुर्मना विमुखश्चैव परिचक्राम तां सभाम् ॥ ४ ॥durmanā vimukhaś caiva paricakrāma tāṃ sabhām ततः स्फाटिकतोयां वै स्फाटिकाम्बुजशोभिताम् ।tataḥ sphāṭikatoyāṃ vai sphāṭikāmbujaśobhitām वापीं मत्वा स्थलमिति सवासाः प्रापतज्जले ॥ ५ ॥vāpīṃ matvā sthalam iti savāsāḥ prāpataj jale

“Once, in the middle of the hall, that lord of earth came upon a crystal floor and, suspecting it was water, hitched up his clothes — his mind deceived. Then, sick at heart and turning his face away, he went on through the hall. And then, taking a pool of crystal-clear water bright with crystal lotuses to be solid ground, he fell into it with his clothes on.”

The servants laughed at him and, on the king’s order, brought him fresh clothes. Bhīma laughed, and Arjuna, and both the twins. He kept his face composed, then lifted his clothes again to cross what was in fact dry floor, and they laughed again; then he struck his forehead on a door that looked open, and shied back from a doorway that was open, thinking it shut. Given leave by the Pāṇḍavas, he went back toward the City of the Elephant.

“As he went — scorched by the Pāṇḍavas’ fortune, worn out by brooding — an evil thought was born in king Duryodhana.”

Śakuni notices him sighing and asks the root of it.

“Having seen this whole earth gone under Yudhiṣṭhira’s control, conquered by the blaze of the great-souled White-Horsed one’s weapons; having seen that sacrifice of Pṛthā’s son, uncle — as Indra’s is among the gods, such was it — I am filled to the brim with a rage I cannot swallow, burning day and night. In the season when the hot months come I dry up like a little water.”

He runs through what he saw: Śiśupāla cut down by Kṛṣṇa in open court and not one man who would take a step for him; kings themselves burnt by the fire that rises off the Pāṇḍavas, enduring it; kings bringing jewels and standing before Kuntī’s son “like tradesmen paying tax.”

“Having seen a fortune of that kind, all but blazing, on the son of Pāṇḍu, I have fallen into the power of a rage I cannot bear, and I burn, unaccustomed to this. I will enter fire, or I will eat poison, or I will walk into water — I am not going to be able to live.”

2.43.29

सोऽहं न स्त्री न चाप्यस्त्री न पुमान्नापुमानपि ।so 'haṃ na strī na cāpy astrī na pumān nāpumān api योऽहं तां मर्षयाम्यद्य तादृशीं श्रियमागताम् ॥ २९ ॥yo 'haṃ tāṃ marṣayāmy adya tādṛśīṃ śriyam āgatām

“So I am not a woman, and not a non-woman; not a man, and not a non-man either — I, who today put up with a fortune of that sort having come to another.”

2.43.31–32ab, 33–34

अशक्तश्चैक एवाहं तामाहर्तुं नृपश्रियम् ।aśaktaś caika evāhaṃ tām āhartuṃ nṛpaśriyam सहायांश्च न पश्यामि तेन मृत्युं विचिन्तये ॥ ३१ ॥sahāyāṃś ca na paśyāmi tena mṛtyuṃ vicintaye दैवमेव परं मन्ये पौरुषं तु निरर्थकम् ।daivam eva paraṃ manye pauruṣaṃ tu nirarthakam … कृतो यत्नो मया पूर्वं विनाशे तस्य सौबल ।kṛto yatno mayā pūrvaṃ vināśe tasya saubala तच्च सर्वमतिक्रम्य स वृद्धोऽप्स्विव पङ्कजम् ॥ ३३ ॥tac ca sarvam atikramya sa vṛddho 'psv iva paṅkajam तेन दैवं परं मन्ये पौरुषं तु निरर्थकम् ।tena daivaṃ paraṃ manye pauruṣaṃ tu nirarthakam धार्तराष्ट्रा हि हीयन्ते पार्था वर्धन्ति नित्यशः ॥ ३४ ॥dhārtarāṣṭrā hi hīyante pārthā vardhanti nityaśaḥ

“Alone, I am not able to take that royal fortune, and I see no allies; therefore I am thinking about death. I hold daiva to be supreme, and manhood’s striving to be pointless. … Effort was made by me before, uncle, toward his destruction — and he passed clean through all of it and grew, like a lotus in water. So I hold daiva to be supreme, and manhood’s striving to be pointless: the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra dwindle, and Pṛthā’s sons increase perpetually.”

Śakuni’s reply is cool: don’t rage at Yudhiṣṭhira; the Pāṇḍavas are simply eating their own allotted portions, bhāgadheyāni hi svāni pāṇḍavā bhuñjate sadā. They won Draupadī and Drupada and his sons and Vāsudeva as an ally; Arjuna got Gāṇḍīva and two inexhaustible quivers by satisfying the Fire. As for allies, that is false — you have Droṇa and his son, Karṇa, Kṛpa, me and my brothers, Somadatta’s son.

Note. Pauruṣa is built on puruṣa, “man.” Duryodhana’s complaint begins with his sense of being unmanned (2.43.29) and ends with a definition of a real man (2.45.13). “Man” is kept in the English so the echo can be heard; the word also means human effort in general.

2.44.11cd

एतैस्त्वं सहितः सर्वैर्जय कृत्स्नां वसुंधराम् ॥ ११ ॥etais tvaṃ sahitaḥ sarvair jaya kṛtsnāṃ vasuṃdharām

“Joined with all of these, conquer the whole earth.”

Duryodhana takes it literally — let us go and beat them — and Śakuni answers with the sentence that produces the rest of the poem.

2.44.14–15ab, 18–19

“Arjuna, Vāsudeva, Bhīmasena, Yudhiṣṭhira, Nakula, Sahadeva, and Drupada with his sons — these are not to be beaten in battle by force, not even by the hosts of the gods. … But the son of Kuntī loves dicing and does not know how to play; and once he is challenged, that lord of kings will not be able to refuse. And I am skilled at the game, and I have no equal on earth or in the three worlds. Summon that son of Kuntī to dice.”

It has to be sold to the old king. Śakuni goes first and tells Dhṛtarāṣṭra that his eldest son has gone pale and wretched. The old king’s bewilderment is genuine.

2.45.8–9

ऐश्वर्यं हि महत्पुत्र त्वयि सर्वं समर्पितम् ।aiśvaryaṃ hi mahat putra tvayi sarvaṃ samarpitam भ्रातरः सुहृदश्चैव नाचरन्ति तवाप्रियम् ॥ ८ ॥bhrātaraḥ suhṛdaś caiva nācaranti tavāpriyam आच्छादयसि प्रावारानश्नासि पिशितौदनम् ।ācchādayasi prāvārān aśnāsi piśitaudanam आजानेया वहन्ति त्वां केनासि हरिणः कृशः ॥ ९ ॥ājāneyā vahanti tvāṃ kenāsi hariṇaḥ kṛśaḥ

“Great lordship, my son, has all been handed over to you. Your brothers and friends do nothing you dislike. You put on fine cloaks; you eat rice with meat; thoroughbreds carry you. Why are you sallow and thin?”

Duryodhana answers with a theory of kingship.

2.45.12–14ab

अश्नाम्याच्छादये चाहं यथा कुपुरुषस्तथा ।aśnāmy ācchādaye cāhaṃ yathā kupuruṣas tathā अमर्षं धारये चोग्रं तितिक्षन्कालपर्ययम् ॥ १२ ॥amarṣaṃ dhāraye cograṃ titikṣan kālaparyayam अमर्षणः स्वाः प्रकृतीरभिभूय परे स्थिताः ।amarṣaṇaḥ svāḥ prakṛtīr abhibhūya pare sthitāḥ क्लेशान्मुमुक्षुः परजान्स वै पुरुष उच्यते ॥ १३ ॥kleśān mumukṣuḥ parajān sa vai puruṣa ucyate संतोषो वै श्रियं हन्ति अभिमानश्च भारत ।saṃtoṣo vai śriyaṃ hanti abhimānaś ca bhārata …

“I eat and I dress myself like a sorry excuse for a man. I carry a fierce unbearable anger, waiting out the turning of time. The man who cannot bear it, when enemies have overpowered his own people and stand over him, who wants to be free of the sufferings the enemy causes — he is what is called a man. Contentment kills fortune, Bhārata, and so does self-satisfaction.”

Then the inventory: eighty-eight thousand householder-graduates whom Yudhiṣṭhira maintains, each with thirty maids; ten thousand more eating from gold plates in his house daily; Kāmboja sending antelope-skins, black, dark and red, and costly woolen cloaks; chariots, women, cattle and horses by hundreds and thousands, three thousand camels and mares walking about; brahmins and cattle-rich Vāṭadhānas, in bands of hundreds, kept waiting at the gate with a tribute of three kharvas, and admitted only once they had brought golden water-pots as their offering (2.45.17–27).

Note. The word prakṛti, in the plural, is a term of statecraft: the constituents of a realm: the king, ministers, territory with its people, fort, treasury, army and ally (the seven-element scheme of the Arthaśāstra). The construction is loose, and it is not settled whose prakṛtis are meant.

2.45.30–31

पूर्णे शतसहस्रे तु विप्राणां परिविष्यताम् ।pūrṇe śatasahasre tu viprāṇāṃ pariviṣyatām स्थापिता तत्र संज्ञाभूच्छङ्खो ध्मायति नित्यशः ॥ ३० ॥sthāpitā tatra saṃjñābhūc chaṅkho dhmāyati nityaśaḥ मुहुर्मुहुः प्रणदतस्तस्य शङ्खस्य भारत ।muhur muhuḥ praṇadatas tasya śaṅkhasya bhārata उत्तमं शब्दमश्रौषं ततो रोमाणि मेऽहृषन् ॥ ३१ ॥uttamaṃ śabdam aśrauṣaṃ tato romāṇi me 'hṛṣan

“Whenever a full hundred thousand brahmins had been served, a signal had been set there: a conch is blown. Again and again that conch sounded, and I heard that supreme sound — and then the hairs on my body stood up.”

He ends where he began, in fire: “Seeing that highest fortune of the son of Pāṇḍu, I do not come to any peace, with my mind burning.” Śakuni names the remedy again. Duryodhana asks his father’s permission, and the old king answers with the sane thing — he will meet with Vidura first, “for he keeps dharma in front and sees far.” Duryodhana answers with the only argument he ever needs.

2.45.43–44

निवर्तयिष्यति त्वासौ यदि क्षत्ता समेष्यति ।nivartayiṣyati tvāsau yadi kṣattā sameṣyati निवृत्ते त्वयि राजेन्द्र मरिष्येऽहमसंशयम् ॥ ४३ ॥nivṛtte tvayi rājendra mariṣye 'ham asaṃśayam स मयि त्वं मृते राजन्विदुरेण सुखी भव ।sa mayi tvaṃ mṛte rājan vidureṇa sukhī bhava भोक्ष्यसे पृथिवीं कृत्स्नां किं मया त्वं करिष्यसि ॥ ४४ ॥bhokṣyase pṛthivīṃ kṛtsnāṃ kiṃ mayā tvaṃ kariṣyasi

“That steward will turn you back if he comes. And if you turn back, lord of kings, I shall die — no doubt of it. When I am dead, king, be happy with Vidura; you will enjoy the whole earth. What use will you have for me?”

The father folds instantly and orders the builders: a great hall of a thousand pillars and a hundred doors, quickly made, strewn with jewels, dice laid out, and word brought to him quietly when it is done.

2.45.48ab, 49

दुर्योधनस्य शान्त्यर्थमिति निश्चित्य भूमिपः ।duryodhanasya śāntyartham iti niścitya bhūmipaḥ … अपृष्ट्वा विदुरं ह्यस्य नासीत्कश्चिद्विनिश्चयः ।apṛṣṭvā viduraṃ hy asya nāsīt kaś cid viniścayaḥ द्यूतदोषांश्च जानन्स पुत्रस्नेहादकृष्यत ॥ ४९ ॥dyūtadoṣāṃś ca jānan sa putrasnehād akṛṣyata

“Having decided that it was for Duryodhana’s peace of mind, the lord of the earth … — and he never made any decision without asking Vidura; and, knowing the evils of dicing, he was dragged along by love for his son.”

Vidura comes at a run, hearing that the door of Kali has opened, and bows to his elder brother’s feet.

2.45.52

नाभिनन्दामि ते राजन्व्यवसायमिमं प्रभो ।nābhinandāmi te rājan vyavasāyam imaṃ prabho पुत्रैर्भेदो यथा न स्याद्द्यूतहेतोस्तथा कुरु ॥ ५२ ॥putrair bhedo yathā na syād dyūtahetos tathā kuru

“I do not welcome this resolve of yours, my lord. Act so that there is no split among your sons on account of dicing.”

Dhṛtarāṣṭra answers with the sentence he will repeat, in some form, until Kurukṣetra is over.

“Steward, there will be no quarrel among my sons. The gods in heaven will be gracious to us; no doubt of it. Whether it is inauspicious or auspicious, whether it is good for us or bad for us — let this friendly game go forward. This is what has been appointed; no doubt of it. … My resolve is not to be obstructed, Vidura; I tell you this. I hold daiva to be supreme, since it is by daiva that this comes about.”

When Śakuni’s second, more open pitch comes — “I will take that fortune away by dicing; let the other man be summoned; without going into danger and without fighting at the head of an army, throwing dice, unwounded, I, knowing, beat the unknowing” — Duryodhana adds the argument dressed in scripture.

द्यूते पुराणैर्व्यवहारः प्रणीतस् तत्रात्ययो नास्ति न संप्रहारः ।dyūte purāṇair vyavahāraḥ praṇītas; tatrātyayo nāsti na saṃprahāraḥ … स्वर्गद्वारं दीव्यतां नो विशिष्टं तद्वर्तिनां चापि तथैव युक्तम् ।svargadvāraṃ dīvyatāṃ no viśiṣṭaṃ; tadvartināṃ cāpi tathaiva yuktam

“In dicing, the practice was laid down by the ancients; there is no transgression in it, and no armed clash. … The door of heaven is a distinguished one for those who play, and equally fitting for those who follow the practice.”

Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s last words before he gives the order are a prophecy he does nothing about.

2.51.14–15

वाक्यं न मे रोचते यत्त्वयोक्तं यत्ते प्रियं तत्क्रियतां नरेन्द्र ।vākyaṃ na me rocate yat tvayoktaṃ; yat te priyaṃ tat kriyatāṃ narendra पश्चात्तप्स्यसे तदुपाक्रम्य वाक्यं न हीदृशं भावि वचो हि धर्म्यम् ॥ १४ ॥paścāt tapsyase tad upākramya vākyaṃ; na hīdṛśaṃ bhāvi vaco hi dharmyam दृष्टं ह्येतद्विदुरेणैवमेव सर्वं पूर्वं बुद्धिविद्यानुगेन ।dṛṣṭaṃ hy etad vidureṇaivam eva; sarvaṃ pūrvaṃ buddhividyānugena तदेवैतदवशस्याभ्युपैति महद्भयं क्षत्रियबीजघाति ॥ १५ ॥tad evaitad avaśasyābhyupaiti; mahad bhayaṃ kṣatriyabījaghāti

“The words you have spoken do not please me. Let what pleases you be done, lord of men. Having undertaken that word, you will burn for it afterward — words like these will not prove to accord with dharma. All this was foreseen exactly so by Vidura, who follows intelligence and learning. And now it comes upon me, who am helpless: the great terror, the killer of the seed of warriors.”

2.51.16

एवमुक्त्वा धृतराष्ट्रो मनीषी दैवं मत्वा परमं दुस्तरं च ।evam uktvā dhṛtarāṣṭro manīṣī; daivaṃ matvā paramaṃ dustaraṃ ca शशासोच्चैः पुरुषान्पुत्रवाक्ये स्थितो राजा दैवसंमूढचेताः ॥ १६ ॥śaśāsoccaiḥ puruṣān putravākye; sthito rājā daivasaṃmūḍhacetāḥ

“Having said this, the thoughtful Dhṛtarāṣṭra — holding daiva to be supreme and impossible to cross — loudly gave the order to his men: a king standing on his son’s word, his mind deluded by daiva.”

The hall was built in a short time. He sent for Vidura and told him to go and fetch Yudhiṣṭhira. Vidura refused once more — “I am afraid of the destruction of the family” — and was overruled with the same formula: “this whole world moves in the power of what the Ordainer has appointed; it is not its own master.”

2.52.1

ततः प्रायाद्विदुरोऽश्वैरुदारैर् महाजवैर्बलिभिः साधुदान्तैः ।tataḥ prāyād viduro 'śvair udārair; mahājavair balibhiḥ sādhudāntaiḥ बलान्नियुक्तो धृतराष्ट्रेण राज्ञा मनीषिणां पाण्डवानां सकाशम् ॥ १ ॥balān niyukto dhṛtarāṣṭreṇa rājñā; manīṣiṇāṃ pāṇḍavānāṃ sakāśam

“Then Vidura set out with noble horses, very swift, strong, well broken — commissioned by force by king Dhṛtarāṣṭra — into the presence of the thoughtful Pāṇḍavas.”

He is honored on arrival by the brahmins of the city, and comes to Yudhiṣṭhira in a palace like Kubera’s. Yudhiṣṭhira receives him properly and asks after his uncle and his cousins — and then notices something.

2.52.5ab

विज्ञायते ते मनसो न प्रहर्षः कच्चित्क्षत्तः कुशलेनागतोऽसि ।vijñāyate te manaso na praharṣaḥ; kaccit kṣattaḥ kuśalenāgato 'si

Vidura answers first as an ambassador, in the formal register, then delivers the message he does not believe.

“This is what the Kuru king said to you, having first asked after your health and your welfare: ‘This hall of your brothers’ is equal in form to your own hall. Come, son, and look at it. Meeting with your brothers, Pṛthā’s son, let a friendly game be played there, and let there be pleasure. We shall be delighted at your coming, and all the Kurus are gathered. And the gamblers appointed there by the great king Dhṛtarāṣṭra — you will see them, seated.’ That is why I have come, king. Take pleasure in it.”

Yudhiṣṭhira’s reply is immediate.

2.52.10

द्यूते क्षत्तः कलहो विद्यते नः को वै द्यूतं रोचयेद्बुध्यमानः ।dyūte kṣattaḥ kalaho vidyate naḥ; ko vai dyūtaṃ rocayed budhyamānaḥ किं वा भवान्मन्यते युक्तरूपं भवद्वाक्ये सर्व एव स्थिताः स्म ॥ १० ॥kiṃ vā bhavān manyate yuktarūpaṃ; bhavadvākye sarva eva sthitāḥ sma

“Steward, in dicing there is quarrelling for us. What man, if he were thinking, would want to dice? What do you consider the fitting thing here? We all stand on your word.”

Vidura, sent under compulsion, gives him the most honest answer available to a man in that position and no more.

2.52.11

जानाम्यहं द्यूतमनर्थमूलं कृतश्च यत्नोऽस्य मया निवारणे ।jānāmy ahaṃ dyūtam anarthamūlaṃ; kṛtaś ca yatno 'sya mayā nivāraṇe राजा तु मां प्राहिणोत्त्वत्सकाशं श्रुत्वा विद्वञ्श्रेय इहाचरस्व ॥ ११ ॥rājā tu māṃ prāhiṇot tvatsakāśaṃ; śrutvā vidvañ śreya ihācarasva

“I know that dicing is the root of ruin, and effort was made by me to prevent it. The king sent me to you. Having heard, learned one, do here what is better.”

Yudhiṣṭhira asks who else is playing. Vidura names them: Śakuni, king of Gāndhāra — “a king who is a consummate gambler, practised of hand, who knows the dice” — and Viviṃśati and king Citrasena, Satyavrata, Purumitra, Jaya. And Yudhiṣṭhira sees it whole, and goes.

2.52.14–16

महाभयाः कितवाः संनिविष्टा मायोपधा देवितारोऽत्र सन्ति ।mahābhayāḥ kitavāḥ saṃniviṣṭā; māyopadhā devitāro 'tra santi धात्रा तु दिष्टस्य वशे किलेदं नादेवनं कितवैरद्य तैर्मे ॥ १४ ॥dhātrā tu diṣṭasya vaśe kiledaṃ; nādevanaṃ kitavair adya tair me नाहं राज्ञो धृतराष्ट्रस्य शासनान् न गन्तुमिच्छामि कवे दुरोदरम् ।nāhaṃ rājño dhṛtarāṣṭrasya śāsanān; na gantum icchāmi kave durodaram इष्टो हि पुत्रस्य पिता सदैव तदस्मि कर्ता विदुरात्थ मां यथा ॥ १५ ॥iṣṭo hi putrasya pitā sadaiva; tad asmi kartā vidurāttha māṃ yathā न चाकामः शकुनिना देविताहं न चेन्मां धृष्णुराह्वयिता सभायाम् ।na cākāmaḥ śakuninā devitāhaṃ; na cen māṃ dhṛṣṇur āhvayitā sabhāyām आहूतोऽहं न निवर्ते कदा चित् तदाहितं शाश्वतं वै व्रतं मे ॥ १६ ॥āhūto 'haṃ na nivarte kadā cit; tad āhitaṃ śāśvataṃ vai vrataṃ me

“Very dangerous gamblers are seated there; there are players there whose foundation is trickery. But this lies in the power of what the Ordainer has appointed — so there is no not-playing with those gamblers for me today. Because of king Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s command I do not wish to refuse to go to the dice, wise one — a father is always to be desired by a son; therefore I shall do it, Vidura, as you tell me. And I will not play with Śakuni unwillingly, unless the bold man challenges me in the assembly: summoned, I never turn back. That is the vow laid on me, and it is permanent.”

He orders everything for the journey and sets out the next day, draupadīm ādikṛtvā — “putting Draupadī at their head” (2.52.17).

2.52.18

दैवं प्रज्ञां तु मुष्णाति तेजश्चक्षुरिवापतत् ।daivaṃ prajñāṃ tu muṣṇāti tejaś cakṣur ivāpatat धातुश्च वशमन्वेति पाशैरिव नरः सितः ॥ १८ ॥dhātuś ca vaśam anveti pāśair iva naraḥ sitaḥ

“Daiva steals a man’s discernment as a glare steals the eye, and a man goes along under the Ordainer’s power like one bound with cords.”

He rode in a chariot given him by Bāhlika, blazing with royal fortune, “summoned by Dhṛtarāṣṭra, and by the coming-round of time.” At Hāstinapura the courtesies are exact and complete. He met his uncle; he met Droṇa, Bhīṣma, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāman, Somadatta, Duryodhana, Śalya, Śakuni, Jayadratha, and the kings already gathered. He saw Gāndhārī, devoted to her husband, surrounded by her daughters-in-law like Rohiṇī by the stars; he saw his old father, whose eye is his intelligence; the old king smelled the heads of the four younger brothers. Only one thing snags.

2.52.31–32

विविशुस्तेऽभ्यनुज्ञाता रत्नवन्ति गृहाण्यथ ।viviśus te 'bhyanujñātā ratnavanti gṛhāṇy atha ददृशुश्चोपयातास्तान्द्रौपदीप्रमुखाः स्त्रियः ॥ ३१ ॥dadṛśuś copayātās tān draupadīpramukhāḥ striyaḥ याज्ञसेन्याः परामृद्धिं दृष्ट्वा प्रज्वलितामिव ।yājñasenyāḥ parām ṛddhiṃ dṛṣṭvā prajvalitām iva स्नुषास्ता धृतराष्ट्रस्य नातिप्रमनसोऽभवन् ॥ ३२ ॥snuṣās tā dhṛtarāṣṭrasya nātipramanaso 'bhavan

“Given leave, they entered the jewel-filled apartments; and the women, Draupadī at their head, came out and saw them. Seeing the supreme prosperity of Yajñasena’s daughter, all but blazing, the daughters-in-law of Dhṛtarāṣṭra were not very glad in their minds.”

Nearly the same image: prajvalitām iva here, “all but blazing”; in Duryodhana’s mouth it is jvalantīm iva (2.43.26).

They bathed and were rubbed with sandal-paste, ate agreeable food, and were sung to sleep; the night passed pleasantly, and in the morning, having done the morning rites, well rested:

2.52.37cd

सभां रम्यां प्रविविशुः कितवैरभिसंवृताम् ॥ ३७ ॥sabhāṃ ramyāṃ praviviśuḥ kitavair abhisaṃvṛtām

“— they entered the beautiful hall, which was crowded with gamblers.”

IIIThe Game

Sabhā Parva 2.53–58

The stakes, in order.

Śakuni opens: “The hall is spread, king; these men are ready for the pleasure. Let the dice be cast and let the terms of the play be settled, Yudhiṣṭhira.” Yudhiṣṭhira objects first on principle.

2.53.2–3

निकृतिर्देवनं पापं न क्षात्रोऽत्र पराक्रमः ।nikṛtir devanaṃ pāpaṃ na kṣātro 'tra parākramaḥ न च नीतिर्ध्रुवा राजन्किं त्वं द्यूतं प्रशंससि ॥ २ ॥na ca nītir dhruvā rājan kiṃ tvaṃ dyūtaṃ praśaṃsasi न हि मानं प्रशंसन्ति निकृतौ कितवस्य ह ।na hi mānaṃ praśaṃsanti nikṛtau kitavasya ha शकुने मैव नो जैषीरमार्गेण नृशंसवत् ॥ ३ ॥śakune maiva no jaiṣīr amārgeṇa nṛśaṃsavat

“Play is trickery, and evil; there is no warrior’s valor in it, and no settled policy. Why do you praise dicing? Men do not praise a gambler’s honor when there is fraud in it. Śakuni — do not beat us that way, off the road, cruelly.”

Śakuni answers with a piece of sophistry.

2.53.11–12

श्रोत्रियोऽश्रोत्रियमुत निकृत्यैव युधिष्ठिर ।śrotriyo 'śrotriyam uta nikṛtyaiva yudhiṣṭhira विद्वानविदुषोऽभ्येति नाहुस्तां निकृतिं जनाः ॥ ११ ॥vidvān aviduṣo 'bhyeti nāhus tāṃ nikṛtiṃ janāḥ एवं त्वं मामिहाभ्येत्य निकृतिं यदि मन्यसे ।evaṃ tvaṃ mām ihābhyetya nikṛtiṃ yadi manyase देवनाद्विनिवर्तस्व यदि ते विद्यते भयम् ॥ १२ ॥devanād vinivartasva yadi te vidyate bhayam

“A learned man approaches an unlearned man by exactly that trickery, Yudhiṣṭhira; a man who knows approaches men who do not know — and people do not call that fraud. If, having come to me here, you consider it fraud, then turn back from the game — if you are afraid.”

2.53.13

आहूतो न निवर्तेयमिति मे व्रतमाहितम् ।āhūto na nivarteyam iti me vratam āhitam विधिश्च बलवान्राजन्दिष्टस्यास्मि वशे स्थितः ॥ १३ ॥vidhiś ca balavān rājan diṣṭasyāsmi vaśe sthitaḥ

“‘Summoned, I do not turn back’ — that is the vow laid on me. And Ordinance is strong, king: I stand in the power of what is appointed.”

Duryodhana announces that he will supply the wealth and Śakuni will play on his behalf. Yudhiṣṭhira objects once more.

2.53.16

अन्येनान्यस्य विषमं देवनं प्रतिभाति मे ।anyenānyasya viṣamaṃ devanaṃ pratibhāti me एतद्विद्वन्नुपादत्स्व काममेवं प्रवर्तताम् ॥ १६ ॥etad vidvann upādatsva kāmam evaṃ pravartatām

“For one man to play on another’s behalf seems to me irregular. You know this — take it up if you like; let it proceed even so.”

The kings enter with Dhṛtarāṣṭra at their head; Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa and Vidura follow, “not very glad in their minds”; the assembly shines like heaven with gods in it; and prāvartata mahārāja suhṛddyūtam anantaram — “and then, great king, the friendly game began.”

What follows is one formula, repeated with almost no variation. Yudhiṣṭhira names a thing in loving detail — “this, king, is my wealth; with it I play against you” — and then, in near-identical words, ten times and more:

2.54.7 (refrain; repeated at 2.54.15, 18, 23, 27, 29; 2.58.4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 21, 25, 28)

एतच्छ्रुत्वा व्यवसितो निकृतिं समुपाश्रितः ।etac chrutvā vyavasito nikṛtiṃ samupāśritaḥ जितमित्येव शकुनिर्युधिष्ठिरमभाषत ॥ ७ ॥jitam ity eva śakunir yudhiṣṭhiram abhāṣata

“Hearing that, the determined one, who had taken his stand on fraud, said just this to Yudhiṣṭhira: ‘Won.’”

First, a jewel born of the ocean’s eddy, a splendid necklace with gold for its finest ornament. Won.

Second, a hundred jars each filled with a thousand niṣkas of gold; an inexhaustible treasury of gold, native gold in abundance. Won.

Third, the chariot: tiger-skinned, sound of wheel and frame, hung with a net of little bells, thunder-sounding, drawn by eight horses the color of ospreys, so admired that “of these it cannot be said that their feet touch the ground.” Won.

Fourth, a thousand rutting elephants with golden girths and garlands, each with eight cow-elephants, like mountains, like rainclouds. Won.

Fifth, a hundred thousand serving-women, young, scented with sandal, skilled in dance and song. Won.

Sixth, the same number of male servants — wise, capable, young, plate in hand, feeding guests day and night. Won.

Seventh, the same number of chariots with gold trappings and banners, warriors who fight with skill, each drawing a thousand a month whether he fights or not. Won.

Eighth, the horses speckled like partridges that Citraratha gave to the wielder of Gāṇḍīva. Won.

Ninth, ten thousand carts and chariots and horses, and sixty thousand men picked class by class. Won.

Tenth, four hundred treasure-chests bound with copper and iron, each holding five droṇas of refined gold. Won.

Then Śakuni changes the register.

2.58.1

बहु वित्तं पराजैषीः पाण्डवानां युधिष्ठिर ।bahu vittaṃ parājaiṣīḥ pāṇḍavānāṃ yudhiṣṭhira आचक्ष्व वित्तं कौन्तेय यदि तेऽस्त्यपराजितम् ॥ १ ॥ācakṣva vittaṃ kaunteya yadi te 'sty aparājitam

“You have gambled away much of the Pāṇḍavas’ wealth, Yudhiṣṭhira. Declare your wealth, son of Kuntī, if you have any that is not yet lost.”

Yudhiṣṭhira, stung, answers with numbers instead of things — ten thousand, a million, and on up through the named orders of magnitude to “an ocean.” Won. Then all the cattle and horses and goats and sheep, uncountable, belonging to the lesser classes east of the Sindhu. Won.

2.58.7a–d

पुरं जनपदो भूमिरब्राह्मणधनैः सह ।puraṃ janapado bhūmir abrāhmaṇadhanaiḥ saha अब्राह्मणाश्च पुरुषा राजञ्शिष्टं धनं मम ।abrāhmaṇāś ca puruṣā rājañ śiṣṭaṃ dhanaṃ mama

“The city, the countryside, the land, together with the property of all who are not brahmins, and the men who are not brahmins — king, that is the wealth left to me.” Won.

The kingdom is gone.

2.58.9a–d

राजपुत्रा इमे राजञ्शोभन्ते येन भूषिताः ।rājaputrā ime rājañ śobhante yena bhūṣitāḥ कुण्डलानि च निष्काश्च सर्वं चाङ्गविभूषणम् ।kuṇḍalāni ca niṣkāś ca sarvaṃ cāṅgavibhūṣaṇam

“The ornaments by which these princes here are made splendid — the earrings, the gold pieces, every body-ornament.” Won.

He has stripped his brothers where they sit. Then, with nothing between him and them:

2.58.11

श्यामो युवा लोहिताक्षः सिंहस्कन्धो महाभुजः ।śyāmo yuvā lohitākṣaḥ siṃhaskandho mahābhujaḥ नकुलो ग्लह एको मे यच्चैतत्स्वगतं धनम् ॥ ११ ॥nakulo glaha eko me yac caitat svagataṃ dhanam

“Dark, young, red-eyed, lion-shouldered, great-armed — Nakula is my single throw; that is the wealth that belongs to me.”

Śakuni’s reply is the first cruelty spoken aloud in the hall: “Nakula is dear to you, prince Yudhiṣṭhira. He has come into our possession. With what will you play now?”

2.58.14

अयं धर्मान्सहदेवोऽनुशास्ति लोके ह्यस्मिन्पण्डिताख्यां गतश्च ।ayaṃ dharmān sahadevo 'nuśāsti; loke hy asmin paṇḍitākhyāṃ gataś ca अनर्हता राजपुत्रेण तेन त्वया दीव्याम्यप्रियवत्प्रियेण ॥ १४ ॥anarhatā rājaputreṇa tena; tvayā dīvyāmy apriyavat priyeṇa

“This Sahadeva teaches the dharmas; in this world he has gone by the name of scholar. With that prince, who does not deserve it, dear to me as though he were not dear — with him I play against you.” Won.

Śakuni pauses to point.

2.58.16

माद्रीपुत्रौ प्रियौ राजंस्तवेमौ विजितौ मया ।mādrīputrau priyau rājaṃs tavemau vijitau mayā गरीयांसौ तु ते मन्ये भीमसेनधनंजयौ ॥ १६ ॥garīyāṃsau tu te manye bhīmasenadhanaṃjayau

“These two sons of Mādrī, dear to you, king, have been won by me. But I think Bhīmasena and Arjuna are weightier to you.”

Yudhiṣṭhira hears it exactly.

2.58.17

अधर्मं चरसे नूनं यो नावेक्षसि वै नयम् ।adharmaṃ carase nūnaṃ yo nāvekṣasi vai nayam यो नः सुमनसां मूढ विभेदं कर्तुमिच्छसि ॥ १७ ॥yo naḥ sumanasāṃ mūḍha vibhedaṃ kartum icchasi

“You are certainly practicing adharma, you who pay no attention to right conduct — you who want to make a split among us, fool, when our minds are good toward each other.”

Śakuni’s answer is a proverb and a bow: “A drunk man falls in a hole; a careless man runs into a post. You are the elder, king; you are the better man. Homage to you, bull of the Bharatas.”

Then Arjuna is staked: “He who is our ferryman across the battle as a ship across water, the swift prince, conqueror of enemies — with him, who does not deserve it, the hero of the world, with Phalguna I play, Śakuni.” Won. Then Śakuni, unprompted: “I have won the bowman of the Pāṇḍavas, the left-handed archer. Play now with Bhīma, whom you hold dear, king — with what remains to you of a stake.” Then Bhīma is staked:

2.58.23–24

यो नो नेता यो युधां नः प्रणेता यथा वज्री दानवशत्रुरेकः ।yo no netā yo yudhāṃ naḥ praṇetā; yathā vajrī dānavaśatrur ekaḥ तिर्यक्प्रेक्षी संहतभ्रूर्महात्मा सिंहस्कन्धो यश्च सदात्यमर्षी ॥ २३ ॥tiryakprekṣī saṃhatabhrūr mahātmā; siṃhaskandho yaś ca sadātyamarṣī बलेन तुल्यो यस्य पुमान्न विद्यते गदाभृतामग्र्य इहारिमर्दनः ।balena tulyo yasya pumān na vidyate; gadābhṛtām agrya ihārimardanaḥ अनर्हता राजपुत्रेण तेन दीव्याम्यहं भीमसेनेन राजन् ॥ २४ ॥anarhatā rājaputreṇa tena; dīvyāmy ahaṃ bhīmasenena rājan

“He who is our leader, who leads us in battle, like the wielder of the thunderbolt, sole enemy of the demons; who looks sidelong with his brows knit together, the great-souled one, lion-shouldered, who is always past bearing anger; the man whose equal in strength does not exist, first of mace-bearers, crusher of enemies here — with that prince, who does not deserve it, with Bhīmasena I play, king.” Won.

Śakuni, again: “You have gambled away much wealth, and your brothers with their horses and elephants. Declare your wealth, son of Kuntī, if you have any that is not yet lost.”

2.58.27

अहं विशिष्टः सर्वेषां भ्रातॄणां दयितस्तथा ।ahaṃ viśiṣṭaḥ sarveṣāṃ bhrātṝṇāṃ dayitas tathā कुर्यामस्ते जिताः कर्म स्वयमात्मन्युपप्लवे ॥ २७ ॥kuryāmas te jitāḥ karma svayam ātmany upaplave

“I am the distinguished one of all my brothers, and the beloved one. I stake myself: won, we would do your work ourselves, in our own ruin.” Won.

Now Śakuni delivers a piece of moral instruction.

2.58.29

एतत्पापिष्ठमकरोर्यदात्मानं पराजितः ।etat pāpiṣṭham akaror yad ātmānaṃ parājitaḥ शिष्टे सति धने राजन्पाप आत्मपराजयः ॥ २९ ॥śiṣṭe sati dhane rājan pāpa ātmaparājayaḥ

“You have done the most evil thing there is, in that you have gambled yourself away. When wealth is still left, king, the losing of one’s own self is an evil act.”

And he supplies the wealth that remains.

2.58.31

अस्ति वै ते प्रिया देवी ग्लह एकोऽपराजितः ।asti vai te priyā devī glaha eko 'parājitaḥ पणस्व कृष्णां पाञ्चालीं तयात्मानं पुनर्जय ॥ ३१ ॥paṇasva kṛṣṇāṃ pāñcālīṃ tayātmānaṃ punar jaya

“You do have a beloved queen — one throw not yet lost. Stake Kṛṣṇā of Pāñcāla; with her, win yourself back.”

Yudhiṣṭhira, a slave being told how to become free, describes his wife for six verses.

2.58.32–35

नैव ह्रस्वा न महती नातिकृष्णा न रोहिणी ।naiva hrasvā na mahatī nātikṛṣṇā na rohiṇī सरागरक्तनेत्रा च तया दीव्याम्यहं त्वया ॥ ३२ ॥sarāgaraktanetrā ca tayā dīvyāmy ahaṃ tvayā शारदोत्पलपत्राक्ष्या शारदोत्पलगन्धया ।śāradotpalapatrākṣyā śāradotpalagandhayā शारदोत्पलसेविन्या रूपेण श्रीसमानया ॥ ३३ ॥śāradotpalasevinyā rūpeṇa śrīsamānayā तथैव स्यादानृशंस्यात्तथा स्याद्रूपसंपदा ।tathaiva syād ānṛśaṃsyāt tathā syād rūpasaṃpadā तथा स्याच्छीलसंपत्त्या यामिच्छेत्पुरुषः स्त्रियम् ॥ ३४ ॥tathā syāc chīlasaṃpattyā yām icchet puruṣaḥ striyam चरमं संविशति या प्रथमं प्रतिबुध्यते ।caramaṃ saṃviśati yā prathamaṃ pratibudhyate आ गोपालाविपालेभ्यः सर्वं वेद कृताकृतम् ॥ ३५ ॥ā gopālāvipālebhyaḥ sarvaṃ veda kṛtākṛtam …

“Not short and not tall, not too dark and not ruddy, her eyes reddened with passion — with her I play against you. Her eyes are the petals of the autumn lotus; her fragrance is the autumn lotus; she is the attendant of the autumn lotus; in beauty she is the equal of Śrī. Such a woman a man would wish for, in her freedom from cruelty, in her perfection of form, in her perfection of character. She is the last to lie down and the first to wake. Down to the cowherds and the shepherds, she knows everything that has been done and not done.”

(The comparison to Śrī echoes the account of her birth: śriyas tu bhāgaḥ, “a portion of Śrī,” above.)

2.58.37

तयैवंविधया राजन्पाञ्चाल्याहं सुमध्यया ।tayaivaṃvidhayā rājan pāñcālyāhaṃ sumadhyayā ग्लहं दीव्यामि चार्वङ्ग्या द्रौपद्या हन्त सौबल ॥ ३७ ॥glahaṃ dīvyāmi cārvaṅgyā draupadyā hanta saubala

“With her, of such a kind, king — with the Pāñcāla woman, slender-waisted, beautiful of limb, with Draupadī — I play the throw. There, Saubala.”

And the hall breaks.

2.58.38–42

एवमुक्ते तु वचने धर्मराजेन भारत ।evam ukte tu vacane dharmarājena bhārata धिग्धिगित्येव वृद्धानां सभ्यानां निःसृता गिरः ॥ ३८ ॥dhig dhig ity eva vṛddhānāṃ sabhyānāṃ niḥsṛtā giraḥ चुक्षुभे सा सभा राजन्राज्ञां संजज्ञिरे कथाः ।cukṣubhe sā sabhā rājan rājñāṃ saṃjajñire kathāḥ भीष्मद्रोणकृपादीनां स्वेदश्च समजायत ॥ ३९ ॥bhīṣmadroṇakṛpādīnāṃ svedaś ca samajāyata शिरो गृहीत्वा विदुरो गतसत्त्व इवाभवत् ।śiro gṛhītvā viduro gatasattva ivābhavat आस्ते ध्यायन्नधोवक्त्रो निःश्वसन्पन्नगो यथा ॥ ४० ॥āste dhyāyann adhovaktro niḥśvasan pannago yathā धृतराष्ट्रस्तु संहृष्टः पर्यपृच्छत्पुनः पुनः ।dhṛtarāṣṭras tu saṃhṛṣṭaḥ paryapṛcchat punaḥ punaḥ किं जितं किं जितमिति ह्याकारं नाभ्यरक्षत ॥ ४१ ॥kiṃ jitaṃ kiṃ jitam iti hy ākāraṃ nābhyarakṣata जहर्ष कर्णोऽतिभृशं सह दुःशासनादिभिः ।jaharṣa karṇo 'tibhṛśaṃ saha duḥśāsanādibhiḥ इतरेषां तु सभ्यानां नेत्रेभ्यः प्रापतज्जलम् ॥ ४२ ॥itareṣāṃ tu sabhyānāṃ netrebhyaḥ prāpataj jalam

“When that word had been spoken by the Dharma King, cries of ‘Shame! Shame!’ broke from the elders in the assembly. The hall shook; talk started up among the kings; sweat came out on Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Kṛpa and the rest. Vidura took hold of his head and became like a man whose strength has gone out of him; he sat brooding, face down, hissing like a snake. But Dhṛtarāṣṭra, thrilled, kept asking over and over, ‘What is won? What is won?’ — he could not keep his composure. Karṇa was delighted beyond measure, and Duḥśāsana and the others with him. But from the eyes of the rest of the assembly, water fell.”

2.58.43

सौबलस्त्वविचार्यैव जितकाशी मदोत्कटः ।saubalas tv avicāryaiva jitakāśī madotkaṭaḥ जितमित्येव तानक्षान्पुनरेवान्वपद्यत ॥ ४३ ॥jitam ity eva tān akṣān punar evānvapadyata

“And Saubala, without any deliberation at all, wearing the look of a winner, swollen with intoxication, took up those dice again with just this: ‘Won.’”

IVThe Hall

Sabhā Parva 2.59–63

From Duryodhana’s order through Draupadī’s question to the granting of the boons.

Duryodhana does not wait a verse.

2.59.1

एहि क्षत्तर्द्रौपदीमानयस्व प्रियां भार्यां संमतां पाण्डवानाम् ।ehi kṣattar draupadīm ānayasva; priyāṃ bhāryāṃ saṃmatāṃ pāṇḍavānām संमार्जतां वेश्म परैतु शीघ्रम् आनन्दो नः सह दासीभिरस्तु ॥ १ ॥saṃmārjatāṃ veśma paraitu śīghram; ānando naḥ saha dāsībhir astu

“Come, steward, bring Draupadī — the beloved wife, honored by the Pāṇḍavas. Let her sweep the house; let her go at once; let there be pleasure for us, with the slave-women.”

Vidura refuses out loud and on a point of law.

2.59.4

न हि दासीत्वमापन्ना कृष्णा भवति भारत ।na hi dāsītvam āpannā kṛṣṇā bhavati bhārata अनीशेन हि राज्ञैषा पणे न्यस्तेति मे मतिः ॥ ४ ॥anīśena hi rājñaiṣā paṇe nyasteti me matiḥ

“Kṛṣṇā has not come into slavery, Bhārata. My judgment is that she was put down as a stake by a king who was no longer her master.”

He goes on: a man should not cut to the quick, nor speak cruelly, nor take what is another’s by base means. “This is the crooked and terrible doorway of hell, and many Kurus are going in after you, with Duḥśāsana.” Duryodhana says “to hell with the steward” and turns to a servant.

The messenger finds Draupadī and delivers the sentence flatly: “With Yudhiṣṭhira drunk on the madness of dice, Duryodhana has won you, Draupadī. Come to Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s house; I am taking you to your work, Yājñasenī.” She does not weep. She finds the flaw.

2.60.5, 7

कथं त्वेवं वदसि प्रातिकामिन् को वै दीव्येद्भार्यया राजपुत्रः ।kathaṃ tv evaṃ vadasi prātikāmin; ko vai dīvyed bhāryayā rājaputraḥ मूढो राजा द्यूतमदेन मत्त आहो नान्यत्कैतवमस्य किं चित् ॥ ५ ॥mūḍho rājā dyūtamadena matta; āho nānyat kaitavam asya kiṃ cit … गच्छ त्वं कितवं गत्वा सभायां पृच्छ सूतज ।gaccha tvaṃ kitavaṃ gatvā sabhāyāṃ pṛccha sūtaja किं नु पूर्वं पराजैषीरात्मानं मां नु भारत ।kiṃ nu pūrvaṃ parājaiṣīr ātmānaṃ māṃ nu bhārata एतज्ज्ञात्वा त्वमागच्छ ततो मां नय सूतज ॥ ७ ॥etaj jñātvā tvam āgaccha tato māṃ naya sūtaja

“How can you say such a thing, messenger? What prince would play with his wife? Was the king so deluded, so drunk with the madness of dice — was there nothing else left to him to stake? … Go. Go to the gambler in the hall and ask him, charioteer’s son: which did you lose first, yourself or me, Bhārata? Come back when you know that; then take me, charioteer’s son.”

The messenger asks it in open court. Whose lord was Yudhiṣṭhira when he lost them — which did he lose first, himself, or her?

2.60.9

युधिष्ठिरस्तु निश्चेष्टो गतसत्त्व इवाभवत् ।yudhiṣṭhiras tu niśceṣṭo gatasattva ivābhavat न तं सूतं प्रत्युवाच वचनं साध्वसाधु वा ॥ ९ ॥na taṃ sūtaṃ pratyuvāca vacanaṃ sādhv asādhu vā

“But Yudhiṣṭhira, motionless, became like a man whose strength has gone out of him. He answered the charioteer’s son not a word, good or bad.”

In this scene he never answers it. Duryodhana orders her brought into the hall in person. The messenger goes back, shaken.

2.60.12

सभ्यास्त्वमी राजपुत्र्याह्वयन्ति मन्ये प्राप्तः संक्षयः कौरवाणाम् ।sabhyās tv amī rājaputry āhvayanti; manye prāptaḥ saṃkṣayaḥ kauravāṇām न वै समृद्धिं पालयते लघीयान् यत्त्वं सभामेष्यसि राजपुत्रि ॥ १२ ॥na vai samṛddhiṃ pālayate laghīyān; yat tvaṃ sabhām eṣyasi rājaputri

“The assembly is calling you, princess. I think the destruction of the Kauravas has arrived. A lesser man does not protect his own prosperity — since you, a princess, are to come into the hall.”

Her answer names the mechanism the whole poem runs on.

2.60.13

एवं नूनं व्यदधात्संविधाता स्पर्शावुभौ स्पृशतो धीरबालौ ।evaṃ nūnaṃ vyadadhāt saṃvidhātā; sparśāv ubhau spṛśato dhīrabālau धर्मं त्वेकं परमं प्राह लोके स नः शमं धास्यति गोप्यमानः ॥ १३ ॥dharmaṃ tv ekaṃ paramaṃ prāha loke; sa naḥ śamaṃ dhāsyati gopyamānaḥ

“Surely the Ordainer has arranged it so: both kinds of touch, the pleasant and the painful, reach the wise man and the child. But he has declared dharma alone supreme in this world; kept guarded, it will set us at peace.”

Duḥśāsana is sent when the servant will not go again.

2.60.20

एह्येहि पाञ्चालि जितासि कृष्णे दुर्योधनं पश्य विमुक्तलज्जा ।ehy ehi pāñcāli jitāsi kṛṣṇe; duryodhanaṃ paśya vimuktalajjā कुरून्भजस्वायतपद्मनेत्रे धर्मेण लब्धासि सभां परैहि ॥ २० ॥kurūn bhajasvāyatapadmanetre; dharmeṇa labdhāsi sabhāṃ paraihi

“Come, come, Pāñcālī; you have been won, Kṛṣṇā. Look at Duryodhana, and let your shame go. Long-lotus-eyed one, take to the Kurus. You have been acquired by dharma. Go to the hall.”

Dharmeṇa labdhā — acquired lawfully. The claim will be repeated in the form dharmeṇa vijitā, “won by dharma” (2.61.28, 37; cf. dharmajitām, 2.61.32).

2.60.21–22

ततः समुत्थाय सुदुर्मनाः सा विवर्णमामृज्य मुखं करेण ।tataḥ samutthāya sudurmanāḥ sā; vivarṇam āmṛjya mukhaṃ kareṇa आर्ता प्रदुद्राव यतः स्त्रियस्ता वृद्धस्य राज्ञः कुरुपुंगवस्य ॥ २१ ॥ārtā pradudrāva yataḥ striyas tā; vṛddhasya rājñaḥ kurupuṃgavasya ततो जवेनाभिससार रोषाद् दुःशासनस्तामभिगर्जमानः ।tato javenābhisasāra roṣād; duḥśāsanas tām abhigarjamānaḥ दीर्घेषु नीलेष्वथ चोर्मिमत्सु जग्राह केशेषु नरेन्द्रपत्नीम् ॥ २२ ॥dīrgheṣu nīleṣv atha cormimatsu; jagrāha keśeṣu narendrapatnīm

“Then she stood up, sick at heart, wiped her bloodless face with her hand, and in her distress ran toward the women of the old king, the bull of the Kurus. Then Duḥśāsana rushed at her at speed, in fury, roaring at her, and seized the wife of a lord of men by the hair — long, dark, and waved.”

2.60.23

ये राजसूयावभृथे जलेन महाक्रतौ मन्त्रपूतेन सिक्ताः ।ye rājasūyāvabhṛthe jalena; mahākratau mantrapūtena siktāḥ ते पाण्डवानां परिभूय वीर्यं बलात्प्रमृष्टा धृतराष्ट्रजेन ॥ २३ ॥te pāṇḍavānāṃ paribhūya vīryaṃ; balāt pramṛṣṭā dhṛtarāṣṭrajena

“Those locks, which at the final bath of the Rājasūya, at that great rite, had been sprinkled with water purified by mantra — those, in contempt of the Pāṇḍavas’ valor, were mauled by force by the son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra.”

2.60.24

स तां परामृश्य सभासमीपम् आनीय कृष्णामतिकृष्णकेशीम् ।sa tāṃ parāmṛśya sabhāsamīpam; ānīya kṛṣṇām atikṛṣṇakeśīm दुःशासनो नाथवतीमनाथवच् चकर्ष वायुः कदलीमिवार्ताम् ॥ २४ ॥duḥśāsano nāthavatīm anāthavac; cakarṣa vāyuḥ kadalīm ivārtām

“Having laid hands on her, Duḥśāsana brought Kṛṣṇā of the very dark hair up to the hall and dragged her — a woman who had protectors, as though she had none — the way the wind drags a plantain tree that suffers it.”

She says quietly, being dragged, her body bent: “Today I am in my period, and I have one garment on, dull-witted man; you should not take me to the hall, unworthy man.”

2.60.26cd–27

कृष्णं च जिष्णुं च हरिं नरं च त्राणाय विक्रोश नयामि हि त्वाम् ॥ २६ ॥kṛṣṇaṃ ca jiṣṇuṃ ca hariṃ naraṃ ca; trāṇāya vikrośa nayāmi hi tvām रजस्वला वा भव याज्ञसेनि एकाम्बरा वाप्यथ वा विवस्त्रा ।rajasvalā vā bhava yājñaseni; ekāmbarā vāpy atha vā vivastrā द्यूते जिता चासि कृतासि दासी दासीषु कामश्च यथोपजोषम् ॥ २७ ॥dyūte jitā cāsi kṛtāsi dāsī; dāsīṣu kāmaś ca yathopajoṣam

“Cry out to Kṛṣṇa and Jiṣṇu and Hari and Nara for rescue — I am taking you all the same. Be in your period, daughter of Yajñasena, or in one garment, or with no garment at all: you have been won at dice, and made a slave; and with slave women, desire goes as it pleases.”

Hair scattered, half her garment fallen, she speaks — and what she says first is not about herself.

Note. Rajasvalā is a menstruating woman. She was impure and kept apart, and wore a single garment; dragging her into the public hall broke that seclusion, which is why the text keeps returning to her condition. The word can also mean “covered with dust,” and has been read that way elsewhere; that reading does not fit Draupadī here.

2.60.29–31

इमे सभायामुपदिष्टशास्त्राः क्रियावन्तः सर्व एवेन्द्रकल्पाः ।ime sabhāyām upadiṣṭaśāstrāḥ; kriyāvantaḥ sarva evendrakalpāḥ गुरुस्थाना गुरवश्चैव सर्वे तेषामग्रे नोत्सहे स्थातुमेवम् ॥ २९ ॥gurusthānā guravaś caiva sarve; teṣām agre notsahe sthātum evam नृशंसकर्मंस्त्वमनार्यवृत्त मा मां विवस्त्रां कृधि मा विकार्षीः ।nṛśaṃsakarmaṃs tvam anāryavṛtta; mā māṃ vivastrāṃ kṛdhi mā vikārṣīḥ न मर्षयेयुस्तव राजपुत्राः सेन्द्रापि देवा यदि ते सहायाः ॥ ३० ॥na marṣayeyus tava rājaputrāḥ; sendrāpi devā yadi te sahāyāḥ धर्मे स्थितो धर्मसुतश्च राजा धर्मश्च सूक्ष्मो निपुणोपलभ्यः ।dharme sthito dharmasutaś ca rājā; dharmaś ca sūkṣmo nipuṇopalabhyaḥ वाचापि भर्तुः परमाणुमात्रं नेच्छामि दोषं स्वगुणान्विसृज्य ॥ ३१ ॥vācāpi bhartuḥ paramāṇumātraṃ; necchāmi doṣaṃ svaguṇān visṛjya

“These men in the hall have been taught the treatises; they all perform the rites; they are all like Indra. All of them stand in the place of teachers, and are teachers. Before them I cannot bear to stand like this. Man of cruel acts, of ignoble conduct — do not make me naked, do not drag me. The princes would not endure it from you, not if the gods with Indra were your allies. The king, the son of Dharma, stands in dharma — and dharma is subtle, to be grasped only by the skilful. Even by a word I do not wish to give up my own good qualities and impute a fault to my husband, not so much as an atom’s worth.”

Then she turns on the room.

2.60.32–34

इदं त्वनार्यं कुरुवीरमध्ये रजस्वलां यत्परिकर्षसे माम् ।idaṃ tv anāryaṃ kuruvīramadhye; rajasvalāṃ yat parikarṣase mām न चापि कश्चित्कुरुतेऽत्र पूजां ध्रुवं तवेदं मतमन्वपद्यन् ॥ ३२ ॥na cāpi kaś cit kurute 'tra pūjāṃ; dhruvaṃ tavedaṃ matam anvapadyan धिगस्तु नष्टः खलु भारतानां धर्मस्तथा क्षत्रविदां च वृत्तम् ।dhig astu naṣṭaḥ khalu bhāratānāṃ; dharmas tathā kṣatravidāṃ ca vṛttam यत्राभ्यतीतां कुरुधर्मवेलां प्रेक्षन्ति सर्वे कुरवः सभायाम् ॥ ३३ ॥yatrābhyatītāṃ kurudharmavelāṃ; prekṣanti sarve kuravaḥ sabhāyām द्रोणस्य भीष्मस्य च नास्ति सत्त्वं ध्रुवं तथैवास्य महात्मनोऽपि ।droṇasya bhīṣmasya ca nāsti sattvaṃ; dhruvaṃ tathaivāsya mahātmano 'pi राज्ञस्तथा हीममधर्ममुग्रं न लक्षयन्ते कुरुवृद्धमुख्याः ॥ ३४ ॥rājñas tathā hīmam adharmam ugraṃ; na lakṣayante kuruvṛddhamukhyāḥ

“But this is ignoble — that in the middle of the Kuru heroes you drag me while I am in my period. And no one here pays me any honour. Surely they have all gone over to your opinion. Shame! The dharma of the Bhāratas is lost, and so is the practice of those who know the warrior’s code — for all the Kurus in this hall are watching the boundary-line of Kuru dharma being crossed. Droṇa has no strength in him, and neither has Bhīṣma — nor, surely, has this great-souled king either — for the chief elders of the Kurus likewise take no notice of this terrible adharma of the king.”

2.60.35–36

तथा ब्रुवन्ती करुणं सुमध्यमा काक्षेण भर्तॄन्कुपितानपश्यत् ।tathā bruvantī karuṇaṃ sumadhyamā; kākṣeṇa bhartṝn kupitān apaśyat सा पाण्डवान्कोपपरीतदेहान् संदीपयामास कटाक्षपातैः ॥ ३५ ॥sā pāṇḍavān kopaparītadehān; saṃdīpayām āsa kaṭākṣapātaiḥ हृतेन राज्येन तथा धनेन रत्नैश्च मुख्यैर्न तथा बभूव ।hṛtena rājyena tathā dhanena; ratnaiś ca mukhyair na tathā babhūva यथार्तया कोपसमीरितेन कृष्णाकटाक्षेण बभूव दुःखम् ॥ ३६ ॥yathārtayā kopasamīritena; kṛṣṇākaṭākṣeṇa babhūva duḥkham

“Speaking so, pitiably, the slender-waisted one looked sidelong at her furious husbands. And she set the Pāṇḍavas — whose bodies were already wrapped in rage — on fire, with the falling of those sidelong glances. Not by the kingdom taken, nor by the wealth, nor by the chief jewels, did it become as it then became: the grief that came of Kṛṣṇā’s sidelong glance, driven by anguish and by rage.”

Duḥśāsana shakes her violently until she is nearly senseless, laughing, and calls her dāsī — slave-woman. Karṇa applauds loudly; Śakuni likewise congratulates Duḥśāsana. Everyone else in the hall is in pain (2.60.37–39). Then Bhīṣma answers her question.

2.60.40–42

न धर्मसौक्ष्म्यात्सुभगे विवक्तुं शक्नोमि ते प्रश्नमिमं यथावत् ।na dharmasaukṣmyāt subhage vivaktuṃ; śaknomi te praśnam imaṃ yathāvat अस्वो ह्यशक्तः पणितुं परस्वं स्त्रियश्च भर्तुर्वशतां समीक्ष्य ॥ ४० ॥asvo hy aśaktaḥ paṇituṃ parasvaṃ; striyaś ca bhartur vaśatāṃ samīkṣya त्यजेत सर्वां पृथिवीं समृद्धां युधिष्ठिरः सत्यमथो न जह्यात् ।tyajeta sarvāṃ pṛthivīṃ samṛddhāṃ; yudhiṣṭhiraḥ satyam atho na jahyāt उक्तं जितोऽस्मीति च पाण्डवेन तस्मान्न शक्नोमि विवेक्तुमेतत् ॥ ४१ ॥uktaṃ jito 'smīti ca pāṇḍavena; tasmān na śaknomi vivektum etat द्यूतेऽद्वितीयः शकुनिर्नरेषु कुन्तीसुतस्तेन निसृष्टकामः ।dyūte 'dvitīyaḥ śakunir nareṣu; kuntīsutas tena nisṛṣṭakāmaḥ न मन्यते तां निकृतिं महात्मा तस्मान्न ते प्रश्नमिमं ब्रवीमि ॥ ४२ ॥na manyate tāṃ nikṛtiṃ mahātmā; tasmān na te praśnam imaṃ bravīmi

“Because of the subtlety of dharma, fortunate woman, I am not able to decide this question of yours as it should be decided. One who has nothing of his own cannot stake what belongs to another — and yet, considering that a woman is in her husband’s power … Yudhiṣṭhira would abandon the whole prosperous earth and would not abandon truth; and the Pāṇḍava has said ‘I am won.’ Therefore I cannot decide it. Śakuni has no second among men at dice, and the son of Kuntī was left by him free to play as he chose; the great-souled one does not consider it fraud. Therefore I do not answer this question of yours.”

Draupadī’s reply hits the actual flaw.

2.60.43–45

आहूय राजा कुशलैः सभायां दुष्टात्मभिर्नैकृतिकैरनार्यैः ।āhūya rājā kuśalaiḥ sabhāyāṃ; duṣṭātmabhir naikṛtikair anāryaiḥ द्यूतप्रियैर्नातिकृतप्रयत्नः कस्मादयं नाम निसृष्टकामः ॥ ४३ ॥dyūtapriyair nātikṛtaprayatnaḥ; kasmād ayaṃ nāma nisṛṣṭakāmaḥ स शुद्धभावो निकृतिप्रवृत्तिम् अबुध्यमानः कुरुपाण्डवाग्र्यः ।sa śuddhabhāvo nikṛtipravṛttim; abudhyamānaḥ kurupāṇḍavāgryaḥ संभूय सर्वैश्च जितोऽपि यस्मात् पश्चाच्च यत्कैतवमभ्युपेतः ॥ ४४ ॥saṃbhūya sarvaiś ca jito 'pi yasmāt; paścāc ca yat kaitavam abhyupetaḥ तिष्ठन्ति चेमे कुरवः सभायाम् ईशाः सुतानां च तथा स्नुषाणाम् ।tiṣṭhanti ceme kuravaḥ sabhāyām; īśāḥ sutānāṃ ca tathā snuṣāṇām समीक्ष्य सर्वे मम चापि वाक्यं विब्रूत मे प्रश्नमिमं यथावत् ॥ ४५ ॥samīkṣya sarve mama cāpi vākyaṃ; vibrūta me praśnam imaṃ yathāvat

“The king was summoned into the hall by experts — by men of ruined character, cheats, ignoble men, lovers of dicing — and he was not much practiced. How can he be called a man who was given his choice? He is pure in nature and did not understand the operation of the fraud, that first man of the Kurus and Pāṇḍavas; he was beaten by all of them acting together, and only afterward did he agree to the wager. And these Kurus stand in the hall, masters of their sons and of their sons’ wives. Let them all consider — and my words too — and answer this question of mine as it should be answered.”

Nobody does. Bhīma erupts at Yudhiṣṭhira instead: “Gamblers in this land have whores, Yudhiṣṭhira, and even with them they do not play; even for those there is pity. … But this I hold to be gone too far, that Draupadī was staked. I am going to burn your arms. Sahadeva — bring fire.” Arjuna stops him: “No one should transgress an elder brother who is righteous. The king was summoned by enemies, and remembering the warrior’s dharma he played at another’s wish; that is a great glory to us.” Bhīma answers in one line: “If I did not know that this was so, Arjuna, I would force those two arms of his into a blazing fire.”

Then a Kaurava speaks — Vikarṇa, Duryodhana’s own brother. He asks the assembly again and again to answer the woman’s question, and gets nothing. He grinds his palms together, breathes out, and rules.

2.61.20–21, 23–24

चत्वार्याहुर्नरश्रेष्ठा व्यसनानि महीक्षिताम् ।catvāry āhur naraśreṣṭhā vyasanāni mahīkṣitām मृगयां पानमक्षांश्च ग्राम्ये चैवातिसक्तताम् ॥ २० ॥mṛgayāṃ pānam akṣāṃś ca grāmye caivātisaktatām एतेषु हि नरः सक्तो धर्ममुत्सृज्य वर्तते ।eteṣu hi naraḥ sakto dharmam utsṛjya vartate तथायुक्तेन च कृतां क्रियां लोको न मन्यते ॥ २१ ॥tathāyuktena ca kṛtāṃ kriyāṃ loko na manyate … साधारणी च सर्वेषां पाण्डवानामनिन्दिता ।sādhāraṇī ca sarveṣāṃ pāṇḍavānām aninditā जितेन पूर्वं चानेन पाण्डवेन कृतः पणः ॥ २३ ॥jitena pūrvaṃ cānena pāṇḍavena kṛtaḥ paṇaḥ इयं च कीर्तिता कृष्णा सौबलेन पणार्थिना ।iyaṃ ca kīrtitā kṛṣṇā saubalena paṇārthinā एतत्सर्वं विचार्याहं मन्ये न विजितामिमाम् ॥ २४ ॥etat sarvaṃ vicāryāhaṃ manye na vijitām imām

“The best of men say there are four vices of kings: hunting, drink, dice, and excessive attachment to women. A man attached to these acts with dharma thrown aside, and the world does not recognize an act done by a man so joined. … Furthermore, the blameless woman is held in common by all the Pāṇḍavas; and this Pāṇḍava had already been won when he made the stake; and Kṛṣṇā was named by Saubala, who wanted the stake. Weighing all of this, I hold that she has not been won.”

A shout goes up praising Vikarṇa and reviling Śakuni. When it dies down, Karṇa answers, and his answer is the true voice of the hall.

एते न किं चिदप्याहुश्चोद्यमानापि कृष्णया ।ete na kiṃ cid apy āhuś codyamānāpi kṛṣṇayā धर्मेण विजितां मन्ये मन्यन्ते द्रुपदात्मजाम् ॥ २८ ॥dharmeṇa vijitāṃ manye manyante drupadātmajām … यदा सभायां सर्वस्वं न्यस्तवान्पाण्डवाग्रजः ॥ ३१ ॥yadā sabhāyāṃ sarvasvaṃ nyastavān pāṇḍavāgrajaḥ अभ्यन्तरा च सर्वस्वे द्रौपदी भरतर्षभ ।abhyantarā ca sarvasve draupadī bharatarṣabha एवं धर्मजितां कृष्णां मन्यसे न जितां कथम् ॥ ३२ ॥evaṃ dharmajitāṃ kṛṣṇāṃ manyase na jitāṃ katham कीर्तिता द्रौपदी वाचा अनुज्ञाता च पाण्डवैः ।kīrtitā draupadī vācā anujñātā ca pāṇḍavaiḥ भवत्यविजिता केन हेतुनैषा मता तव ॥ ३३ ॥bhavaty avijitā kena hetunaiṣā matā tava … एको भर्ता स्त्रिया देवैर्विहितः कुरुनन्दन ।eko bhartā striyā devair vihitaḥ kurunandana इयं त्वनेकवशगा बन्धकीति विनिश्चिता ॥ ३५ ॥iyaṃ tv anekavaśagā bandhakīti viniścitā अस्याः सभामानयनं न चित्रमिति मे मतिः ।asyāḥ sabhām ānayanaṃ na citram iti me matiḥ एकाम्बरधरत्वं वाप्यथ वापि विवस्त्रता ॥ ३६ ॥ekāmbaradharatvaṃ vāpy atha vāpi vivastratā

“These men have said nothing at all, though pressed by Kṛṣṇā. I take it they consider Drupada’s daughter lawfully won. … When the eldest of the Pāṇḍavas staked everything in the hall, and Draupadī was included within ‘everything’, bull of the Bharatas — how do you consider Kṛṣṇā, thus won by dharma, not won? She was named aloud, and the Pāṇḍavas consented. On what ground do you hold her to be unwon? … One husband is ordained for a woman by the gods, son of Kuru; but this one is in the power of many, and is therefore determined to be a whore. Bringing her into the hall is nothing remarkable, in my judgment — nor is her being in one garment, nor her being in none.”

2.61.37–38

यच्चैषां द्रविणं किं चिद्या चैषा ये च पाण्डवाः ।yac caiṣāṃ draviṇaṃ kiṃ cid yā caiṣā ye ca pāṇḍavāḥ सौबलेनेह तत्सर्वं धर्मेण विजितं वसु ॥ ३७ ॥saubaleneha tat sarvaṃ dharmeṇa vijitaṃ vasu दुःशासन सुबालोऽयं विकर्णः प्राज्ञवादिकः ।duḥśāsana subālo 'yaṃ vikarṇaḥ prājñavādikaḥ पाण्डवानां च वासांसि द्रौपद्याश्चाप्युपाहर ॥ ३८ ॥pāṇḍavānāṃ ca vāsāṃsi draupadyāś cāpy upāhara

“Whatever property is theirs, and this woman, and the Pāṇḍavas themselves — all of that wealth has been lawfully won here by Saubala. Duḥśāsana: this Vikarṇa is a child talking like a wise man. Take away the garments of the Pāṇḍavas, and Draupadī’s too.”

The five brothers strip off their own upper garments and sit down.

Note. Bandhakī: a woman “tied to” many men (a guess from the root; the attested sense is unchaste woman, harlot). Bhīma uses the plural bandhakyaḥ earlier (2.61.1). Karṇa’s argument runs: the gods ordain one husband; she is subject to many; so she is a bandhakī. How coarse the word was is uncertain. The blunt English is kept because the speech is meant to degrade.

ततो दुःशासनो राजन्द्रौपद्या वसनं बलात् ।tato duḥśāsano rājan draupadyā vasanaṃ balāt सभामध्ये समाक्षिप्य व्यपक्रष्टुं प्रचक्रमे ॥ ४० ॥sabhāmadhye samākṣipya vyapakraṣṭuṃ pracakrame आकृष्यमाणे वसने द्रौपद्यास्तु विशां पते ।ākṛṣyamāṇe vasane draupadyās tu viśāṃ pate तद्रूपमपरं वस्त्रं प्रादुरासीदनेकशः ॥ ४१ ॥tadrūpam aparaṃ vastraṃ prādurāsīd anekaśaḥ ततो हलहलाशब्दस्तत्रासीद्घोरनिस्वनः ।tato halahalāśabdas tatrāsīd ghoranisvanaḥ तदद्भुततमं लोके वीक्ष्य सर्वमहीक्षिताम् ॥ ४२ ॥tad adbhutatamaṃ loke vīkṣya sarvamahīkṣitām

“Then Duḥśāsana, king, seizing Draupadī’s garment by force in the middle of the hall, began to pull it off. And as Draupadī’s garment was being pulled, lord of peoples, another garment of the same kind appeared — again and again, many times over. Then there arose a terrible roar of voices there, as all the lords of earth looked upon the greatest wonder in the world.”

In the constituted text the miracle has no stated cause: Draupadī does not pray, and no god is named when the garments appear. In the hall scene, the only mention of Kṛṣṇa before it is Duḥśāsana’s taunt at 2.60.26. The starred insertions add prayers and credit Dharma; how they spread across the recensions is not settled here. The garment simply prādurāsīt, “appeared.”

Bhīma swears, in the middle of the kings, grinding one hand into the other.

“Take up this word of mine, you warriors who dwell in the world — a thing never spoken before by other men, and which no other will speak after: if, having said this, I do not do it, lords of the earth, may I not attain the destination of my ancestors. When the heap of garments in the middle of the hall was piled high, Duḥśāsana sat down, exhausted and ashamed. Vidura raises both arms and makes a formal appeal.

“Draupadī has stated her question and cries out like a woman with no protector — and you do not answer that question. Members of the assembly, dharma is being crushed here. And a man who sees dharma, who has come into an assembly and answers falsely, obtains the whole fruit of the lie: that is settled.”

He backs it with an old story, Prahlāda’s, and Kaśyapa’s ruling in it.

2.62.1–2, 8–9, 13

पुरस्तात्करणीयं मे न कृतं कार्यमुत्तरम् ।purastāt karaṇīyaṃ me na kṛtaṃ kāryam uttaram विह्वलास्मि कृतानेन कर्षता बलिना बलात् ॥ १ ॥vihvalāsmi kṛtānena karṣatā balinā balāt अभिवादं करोम्येषां गुरूणां कुरुसंसदि ।abhivādaṃ karomy eṣāṃ gurūṇāṃ kurusaṃsadi न मे स्यादपराधोऽयं यदिदं न कृतं मया ॥ २ ॥na me syād aparādho 'yaṃ yad idaṃ na kṛtaṃ mayā … किं त्वतः कृपणं भूयो यदहं स्त्री सती शुभा ।kiṃ tv ataḥ kṛpaṇaṃ bhūyo yad ahaṃ strī satī śubhā सभामध्यं विगाहेऽद्य क्व नु धर्मो महीक्षिताम् ॥ ८ ॥sabhāmadhyaṃ vigāhe 'dya kva nu dharmo mahīkṣitām धर्म्याः स्त्रियः सभां पूर्वं न नयन्तीति नः श्रुतम् ।dharmyāḥ striyaḥ sabhāṃ pūrvaṃ na nayantīti naḥ śrutam स नष्टः कौरवेयेषु पूर्वो धर्मः सनातनः ॥ ९ ॥sa naṣṭaḥ kauraveyeṣu pūrvo dharmaḥ sanātanaḥ … जितां वाप्यजितां वापि मन्यध्वं वा यथा नृपाः ।jitāṃ vāpy ajitāṃ vāpi manyadhvaṃ vā yathā nṛpāḥ तथा प्रत्युक्तमिच्छामि तत्करिष्यामि कौरवाः ॥ १३ ॥tathā pratyuktam icchāmi tat kariṣyāmi kauravāḥ

“The duty that I should have performed first, the duty owed before all others, I have not performed; I have been made helpless by this strong man dragging me by force. I make my salutation to these elders in the assembly of the Kurus. Let this not be my fault, that it was not done by me. … What is more wretched than this: that I, a woman, chaste and virtuous, plunge today into the middle of the hall? Where has the dharma of the lords of the earth gone? We have heard that in earlier times people did not lead virtuous women into an assembly. That ancient eternal dharma is destroyed among the Kauravas. … Whether you judge me won or not won, kings — I want to be answered on that, and I will do it, Kauravas.”

Bhīṣma answers a second time and gives up entirely.

2.62.14–15, 17, 20–21ab

उक्तवानस्मि कल्याणि धर्मस्य तु परां गतिम् ।uktavān asmi kalyāṇi dharmasya tu parāṃ gatim लोके न शक्यते गन्तुमपि विप्रैर्महात्मभिः ॥ १४ ॥loke na śakyate gantum api viprair mahātmabhiḥ बलवांस्तु यथा धर्मं लोके पश्यति पूरुषः ।balavāṃs tu yathā dharmaṃ loke paśyati pūruṣaḥ स धर्मो धर्मवेलायां भवत्यभिहितः परैः ॥ १५ ॥sa dharmo dharmavelāyāṃ bhavaty abhihitaḥ paraiḥ … नूनमन्तः कुलस्यास्य भविता नचिरादिव ।nūnam antaḥ kulasyāsya bhavitā nacirād iva तथा हि कुरवः सर्वे लोभमोहपरायणाः ॥ १७ ॥tathā hi kuravaḥ sarve lobhamohaparāyaṇāḥ … एते द्रोणादयश्चैव वृद्धा धर्मविदो जनाः ।ete droṇādayaś caiva vṛddhā dharmavido janāḥ शून्यैः शरीरैस्तिष्ठन्ति गतासव इवानताः ॥ २० ॥śūnyaiḥ śarīrais tiṣṭhanti gatāsava ivānatāḥ युधिष्ठिरस्तु प्रश्नेऽस्मिन्प्रमाणमिति मे मतिः ।yudhiṣṭhiras tu praśne 'smin pramāṇam iti me matiḥ

“I have told you, good woman: the ultimate course of dharma cannot be reached in this world, not even by great brahmins. But whatever a strong man in the world sees as dharma — that, when dharma is in question, is what gets called dharma by others. … Surely the end of this family will come, and not long from now, since all the Kurus have gone over to greed and delusion. … These men here, Droṇa and the elders, people who know dharma, sit with empty bodies, bowed like men whose life-breath is gone. But in this question, my judgment is that Yudhiṣṭhira is the authority.”

2.62.22

तथा तु दृष्ट्वा बहु तत्तदेवं रोरूयमाणां कुररीमिवार्ताम् ।tathā tu dṛṣṭvā bahu tat tad evaṃ; rorūyamāṇāṃ kurarīm ivārtām नोचुर्वचः साध्वथ वाप्यसाधु महीक्षितो धार्तराष्ट्रस्य भीताः ॥ २२ ॥nocur vacaḥ sādhv atha vāpy asādhu; mahīkṣito dhārtarāṣṭrasya bhītāḥ

“Seeing her so, again and again, wailing like an osprey in distress, the lords of earth said not a word, good or bad, in fear of Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s son.”

Duryodhana, seeing the silence, smiles and makes an offer.

2.62.24–25

तिष्ठत्वयं प्रश्न उदारसत्त्वे भीमेऽर्जुने सहदेवे तथैव ।tiṣṭhatv ayaṃ praśna udārasattve; bhīme 'rjune sahadeve tathaiva पत्यौ च ते नकुले याज्ञसेनि वदन्त्वेते वचनं त्वत्प्रसूतम् ॥ २४ ॥patyau ca te nakule yājñaseni; vadantv ete vacanaṃ tvatprasūtam अनीश्वरं विब्रुवन्त्वार्यमध्ये युधिष्ठिरं तव पाञ्चालि हेतोः ।anīśvaraṃ vibruvantv āryamadhye; yudhiṣṭhiraṃ tava pāñcāli hetoḥ कुर्वन्तु सर्वे चानृतं धर्मराजं पाञ्चालि त्वं मोक्ष्यसे दासभावात् ॥ २५ ॥kurvantu sarve cānṛtaṃ dharmarājaṃ; pāñcāli tvaṃ mokṣyase dāsabhāvāt

“Let this question rest on the noble-natured Bhīma, on Arjuna, on Sahadeva, and on your husband Nakula, daughter of Yajñasena; let them speak the word that comes from you. Let them declare in the midst of these nobles, for your sake, Pāñcālī, that Yudhiṣṭhira was not master of you; let them all make the Dharma King a liar, and you will be freed from slavery.”

Bhīma takes hold of his own arm and answers.

2.62.32–34, 36

यद्येष गुरुरस्माकं धर्मराजो युधिष्ठिरः ।yady eṣa gurur asmākaṃ dharmarājo yudhiṣṭhiraḥ न प्रभुः स्यात्कुलस्यास्य न वयं मर्षयेमहि ॥ ३२ ॥na prabhuḥ syāt kulasyāsya na vayaṃ marṣayemahi ईशो नः पुण्यतपसां प्राणानामपि चेश्वरः ।īśo naḥ puṇyatapasāṃ prāṇānām api ceśvaraḥ मन्यते जितमात्मानं यद्येष विजिता वयम् ॥ ३३ ॥manyate jitam ātmānaṃ yady eṣa vijitā vayam न हि मुच्येत जीवन्मे पदा भूमिमुपस्पृशन् ।na hi mucyeta jīvan me padā bhūmim upaspṛśan मर्त्यधर्मा परामृश्य पाञ्चाल्या मूर्धजानिमान् ॥ ३४ ॥martyadharmā parāmṛśya pāñcālyā mūrdhajān imān … धर्मपाशसितस्त्वेवं नाधिगच्छामि संकटम् ।dharmapāśasitas tv evaṃ nādhigacchāmi saṃkaṭam गौरवेण निरुद्धश्च निग्रहादर्जुनस्य च ॥ ३६ ॥gauraveṇa niruddhaś ca nigrahād arjunasya ca

“If this Yudhiṣṭhira, the Dharma King, our elder, were not master of this family, we would not put up with it. He is master of our merits and our austerities, and master even of our lives. If he considers himself won, then we are won. Otherwise no mortal man who had laid hands on these locks of the Pāñcāla woman would get away alive with his foot touching the ground. … But bound like this by the cord of dharma, I find no way through the strait; I am blocked by reverence, and by Arjuna’s restraint.”

Dharmapāśasitaḥ — bound by the noose of dharma. It shares pāśa with Varuṇa’s nooses (2.61.67) and both pāśa and sita with 2.52.18; cf. also dharmapāśānubaddhaḥ of Bhīma at 2.63.6. Karṇa answers in the same key.

2.63.1, 3ab

त्रयः किलेमे अधना भवन्ति दासः शिष्यश्चास्वतन्त्रा च नारी ।trayaḥ kileme adhanā bhavanti; dāsaḥ śiṣyaś cāsvatantrā ca nārī दासस्य पत्नी त्वं धनमस्य भद्रे हीनेश्वरा दासधनं च दासी ॥ १ ॥dāsasya patnī tvaṃ dhanam asya bhadre; hīneśvarā dāsadhanaṃ ca dāsī … अन्यं वृणीष्व पतिमाशु भामिनि यस्माद्दास्यं न लभसे देवनेन ।anyaṃ vṛṇīṣva patim āśu bhāmini; yasmād dāsyaṃ na labhase devanena

“These three are said to own nothing: a slave, a student, and a woman who is not independent. You are a slave’s wife, and his property, good woman; your master is gone, and a slave’s property is itself a slave. … Choose another husband quickly, passionate one — one by whom you will not get slavery through dicing.”

Duryodhana, laughing, uncovers his left thigh and shows it to her. Bhīma swears his second oath: “May Wolf-belly not go to the world of his fathers if I do not split that thigh of yours with my mace in the great battle.” And then the house answers.

2.63.22

ततो राज्ञो धृतराष्ट्रस्य गेहे गोमायुरुच्चैर्व्याहरदग्निहोत्रे ।tato rājño dhṛtarāṣṭrasya gehe; gomāyur uccair vyāharad agnihotre तं रासभाः प्रत्यभाषन्त राजन् समन्ततः पक्षिणश्चैव रौद्राः ॥ २२ ॥taṃ rāsabhāḥ pratyabhāṣanta rājan; samantataḥ pakṣiṇaś caiva raudrāḥ

“Then in the house of king Dhṛtarāṣṭra a jackal howled aloud at the fire-offering; and asses answered it, king, and terrible birds on every side.”

Vidura heard it, and Gāndhārī; Bhīṣma and Droṇa and Kṛpa cried out “Peace! Peace!” Gāndhārī and Vidura reported the omen to the king, and Dhṛtarāṣṭra — who has not spoken since asking “what is won, what is won” — finally speaks.

2.63.25

हतोऽसि दुर्योधन मन्दबुद्धे यस्त्वं सभायां कुरुपुंगवानाम् ।hato 'si duryodhana mandabuddhe; yas tvaṃ sabhāyāṃ kurupuṃgavānām स्त्रियं समाभाषसि दुर्विनीत विशेषतो द्रौपदीं धर्मपत्नीम् ॥ २५ ॥striyaṃ samābhāṣasi durvinīta; viśeṣato draupadīṃ dharmapatnīm

“You are a dead man, Duryodhana, you dull-witted thing — you who in the assembly of the bulls of the Kurus address a woman so, ill-disciplined creature; and above all Draupadī, a lawful wife.”

He turns to Draupadī, conciliating, and offers her a boon. She asks that Yudhiṣṭhira be no slave, so that his son will not be called a slave’s son by other boys. He offers a second; she asks for the four brothers with their chariots and their bows. He offers a third, and she refuses it.

2.63.34–36

लोभो धर्मस्य नाशाय भगवन्नाहमुत्सहे ।lobho dharmasya nāśāya bhagavan nāham utsahe अनर्हा वरमादातुं तृतीयं राजसत्तम ॥ ३४ ॥anarhā varam ādātuṃ tṛtīyaṃ rājasattama एकमाहुर्वैश्यवरं द्वौ तु क्षत्रस्त्रिया वरौ ।ekam āhur vaiśyavaraṃ dvau tu kṣatrastriyā varau त्रयस्तु राज्ञो राजेन्द्र ब्राह्मणस्य शतं वराः ॥ ३५ ॥trayas tu rājño rājendra brāhmaṇasya śataṃ varāḥ पापीयांस इमे भूत्वा संतीर्णाः पतयो मम ।pāpīyāṃsa ime bhūtvā saṃtīrṇāḥ patayo mama वेत्स्यन्ति चैव भद्राणि राजन्पुण्येन कर्मणा ॥ ३६ ॥vetsyanti caiva bhadrāṇi rājan puṇyena karmaṇā

“Greed is the destruction of dharma, lord; I cannot. I do not deserve to take a third boon, best of kings. They say one boon for a vaiśya, two for a kṣatriya woman, three for a king, and a hundred for a brahmin. My husbands, brought low, have been carried across; and they will come to know good things, king, by pure action.”

Karṇa’s comment is the last word on it.

2.64.1, 3

या नः श्रुता मनुष्येषु स्त्रियो रूपेण संमताः ।yā naḥ śrutā manuṣyeṣu striyo rūpeṇa saṃmatāḥ तासामेतादृशं कर्म न कस्यां चन शुश्रुमः ॥ १ ॥tāsām etādṛśaṃ karma na kasyāṃ cana śuśrumaḥ … अप्लवेऽम्भसि मग्नानामप्रतिष्ठे निमज्जताम् ।aplave 'mbhasi magnānām apratiṣṭhe nimajjatām पाञ्चाली पाण्डुपुत्राणां नौरेषा पारगाभवत् ॥ ३ ॥pāñcālī pāṇḍuputrāṇāṃ naur eṣā pāragābhavat

“Of the women famed among men for their beauty, of whom we have heard, we have never heard of such a deed as this from any of them. … For the sons of Pāṇḍu, sunk in water with no boat, going under with no footing anywhere, this Pāñcāla woman became the ship that carried them across.”

Bhīma, having heard Karṇa, says one line (2.64.4): strī gatiḥ pāṇḍuputrāṇām — “A woman is the recourse of the sons of Pāṇḍu.” Arjuna answers (2.64.8), and Bhīma speaks again at 2.64.10–11.

VThe Second Game

Sabhā Parva 2.67

The Pāṇḍavas, released with their kingdom restored, are overtaken on the road.

Even Karṇa had marvelled, in the same breath as his praise of Draupadī, at what her intervention had done:

2.64.2

क्रोधाविष्टेषु पार्थेषु धार्तराष्ट्रेषु चाप्यति ।krodhāviṣṭeṣu pārtheṣu dhārtarāṣṭreṣu cāpy ati द्रौपदी पाण्डुपुत्राणां कृष्णा शान्तिरिहाभवत् ॥ २ ॥draupadī pāṇḍuputrāṇāṃ kṛṣṇā śāntir ihābhavat

“When the Pārthas were possessed by wrath, and the Dhārtarāṣṭras exceedingly so, Draupadī — Kṛṣṇā — became the peace of the sons of Pāṇḍu.”

But Duryodhana extracted one more game from his father — a single throw, with exile as the stake — and a messenger overtook Yudhiṣṭhira on the road: the hall is spread, the dice are cast, your father says come and play.

2.67.3–4

धातुर्नियोगाद्भूतानि प्राप्नुवन्ति शुभाशुभम् ।dhātur niyogād bhūtāni prāpnuvanti śubhāśubham न निवृत्तिस्तयोरस्ति देवितव्यं पुनर्यदि ॥ ३ ॥na nivṛttis tayor asti devitavyaṃ punar yadi अक्षद्यूते समाह्वानं नियोगात्स्थविरस्य च ।akṣadyūte samāhvānaṃ niyogāt sthavirasya ca जानन्नपि क्षयकरं नातिक्रमितुमुत्सहे ॥ ४ ॥jānann api kṣayakaraṃ nātikramitum utsahe

“By the appointment of the Ordainer, beings obtain the fair and the foul; there is no turning-away from those two, if it must be diced again. The summons to the dice-game comes by appointment of the elder: though knowing it works destruction, I cannot step across it.”

2.67.7

यथोपजोषमासीनाः पुनर्द्यूतप्रवृत्तये ।yathopajoṣam āsīnāḥ punardyūtapravṛttaye सर्वलोकविनाशाय दैवेनोपनिपीडिताः ॥ ७ ॥sarvalokavināśāya daivenopanipīḍitāḥ

“They sat down at their ease for the resumption of the dicing — for the destruction of the whole world, pressed down by daiva.”

The single throw was lost. The Pāṇḍavas went into exile: twelve years in the forest, and a thirteenth year unknown among men (2.67.9–11).

1.58.46 (reprise)

अस्या भूमेर्निरसितुं भारं भागैः पृथक्पृथक् ।asyā bhūmer nirasituṃ bhāraṃ bhāgaiḥ pṛthak pṛthak अस्यामेव प्रसूयध्वं विरोधायेति चाब्रवीत् ॥ ४६ ॥asyām eva prasūyadhvaṃ virodhāyeti cābravīt

“To cast off the burden of this earth, each by his own separate portion — be born upon her, for conflict. So he said.”
