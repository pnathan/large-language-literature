The reader's edition

The Epic Entire, with Commentary

The Standard Babylonian epic read straight through in 156 titled pericopes, with philological glosses in the inner margin, discursive notes in the outer, a resonance register for Akkadian wordplay the English misses, and four passages where the page splits into Old Babylonian and Standard columns. Seven museum plates.

 The Old Babylonian witnesses

Fourteen Tablets, ca. 1800 BC

All fourteen surviving Old Babylonian witnesses — from the Pennsylvania and Yale tablets to the Sippar ale-wife — with a three-panel synopsis against the Standard edition and notes reading each witness against the later text.

These are original English translations made line by line against the Akkadian of the electronic Babylonian Library's critical editions (retrieved 25–26 August 2026 via the eBL API), which rest on A. R. George's 2003 edition and the manuscripts recovered since. The English is not George's wording, not the eBL's, though any honest translation of the same Akkadian will rhyme with its predecessors.
