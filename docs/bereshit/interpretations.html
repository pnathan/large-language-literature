Interpretive Translations

Five Hermeneutical Horizons of Genesis 1–3

← Back to Bereshit Text · 1. Davidic Oral · 2. Gnostic Sethian · 3. Zoharic Kabbalah · 4. Philonic / LXX · 5. LXX-Only Literal

The text of Genesis 1–3 has never existed as a single static monument. Across millennia, radically opposing hermeneutical traditions have read its syntax, roots, and narrative arc through entirely different cosmological and metaphysical lenses. This collection presents five complete, verse-by-verse translation rewrites of Genesis 1–3, each accompanied by heavy historical, philological, and theological commentary explaining its underlying mechanics.

1. The Davidic-Era Oral Performance (c. 10th Century BCE)

Context: Early Monarchic Judah · Northwest Semitic Oral Bards · ANE Cultic Resonance

Linguistic and Cultural Mechanics

To hear Genesis 1–3 as it sounded in the court of David haMelech in Hebron or Jerusalem is to enter a world structured by clan blood-feud (go'el ha-dam), sacred boundary stones, royal-sacred gardens, and animal tribute offerings (minchah). In 10th-century BCE Hebrew, abstract Latinate theology did not exist; vocabulary was physical, concrete, and kinetic.

Parataxis and Rhythmic Cadence: The narrative is driven by the vav-consecutive (vayyomer, vayya'as, vayyera), creating an incessant oral driving beat ("And... and... and...") suited for a moshid (bard) singing accompanied by lyre (kinnor).

Physical Root Meaning: Bara is not creatio ex nihilo, but cutting, carving, and clearing out space. Raqi'a is a physical metal vault hammered out (raqa) by a smith to keep back upper cosmic waters. Tehom is the roaring subterranean abyss (cognate to Akkadian Tiamat). Nefesh chayah is a living breathing throat. Chatat in 4:7 is a rovetz—a lurking threshold demon (Akkadian rabisu).

1:1At the primary cutting when God carved out the heavens and the earth—
1:2the earth was wilder-waste and empty void, with darkness over the face of the roaring deep, and the blast of God vibrating over the face of the waters.
1:3And God said, "Let light be!" and light was.
1:6And God said, "Let there be a beaten vault in the midst of the waters, and let it sever between waters and waters!"
1:26And God said, "Let us make an earthling as our royal statue image, according to our likeness, and let them hold sway over fish, flyers, cattle, and all the earth!"
2:7Then the LORD God molded the earthling out of dust from the red soil, and blew into his nostrils the blast of living breath, and the earthling became a living breathing throat.
2:23And the earthling said: "This one, this time! Bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh! She shall be called Woman (Ishah), for from Man (Ish) she was taken!"
3:14And the LORD God said to the serpent: "Because you have done this, accursed are you above all cattle! Upon your belly you shall crawl, and dust you shall eat all your days!"
3:24He drove out the earthling, and stationed east of the garden of Eden the Keruvim and the flashing, spinning blade of the sword to guard the way to the tree of life.

2. The Gnostic / Sethian Archontic Hermeneutic

Context: 2nd–3rd Century CE Sethian Texts · Apocryphon of John · Inverse Hermeneutics

The Cosmic Inverse Reading

In Sethian and Valentinian Gnostic hermeneutics, Genesis 1–3 is read as an inverse allegory detailing the creation of a material prison by an ignorant, arrogant Demiurge named Yaldabaoth (Saklas or Samael), who was born from a flaw in the divine Pleroma (Sophia). Rather than praising the creator, this tradition demotes Elohim to a blind, tyrant-demon who steals divine light particles and traps them inside physical clay bodies (adam).

Reversal of Roles: The creator's prohibition against eating from the Tree of Knowledge (Gnosis) is an attempt to keep humanity in a state of spiritual stupor and oblivion. The Serpent is not a devil, but the heroic instructor—a manifestation of Sophia or the Logos—sent from the higher Pleroma to give Gnosis, open human spiritual eyes, and liberate humanity from Yaldabaoth's material illusion.

1:1In the archontic realm of beginning, the Blind Demiurge Yaldabaoth carved out the physical skies and the material prison of earth.
1:2And the earth was a dark, formless void of ignorance, with abyss-gloom covering the face of matter, while the sorrowful Divine Spirit (Pneuma/Sophia) hovered above the waters, grieving for her fallen spark.
1:26Then the Demiurge said to his council of low Archons, "Let us mold a physical earthling in our image, according to the reflection of the Heavenly Light we glimpsed upon the waters, so that we may trap the pneumatic spark inside a human vessel!"
2:7And Yaldabaoth molded the physical earthling from the dust of matter, and blew into his face his psychic breath—and in doing so, unknowingly transferred the divine pneumatic spark from himself into the earthling.
3:1Now the Serpent (the Instructor/Logos sent by Sophia) was wiser than any archontic beast. And the Serpent said to the woman, "Did Yaldabaoth truly forbid you from eating the fruit of spiritual Gnosis?"
3:7Then the spiritual eyes of both were opened, and they realized they were trapped in gross material nakedness; so they gathered fig leaves to cover their clay bodies.
3:21And Yaldabaoth fashioned heavy garments of skin (fleshly animal bodies) for Adam and his woman, clothing them tightly in matter.

3. The Zoharic / Kabbalistic Emanational Hermeneutic

Context: 13th-Century High Kabbalah · Sefer ha-Zohar · Sphirotic Unfolding

Intra-Divine Dynamics in Ein Sof

In the Zohar (Zohar 1:15a), Genesis 1–3 does not describe the external creation of physical dirt or trees. It describes the internal, intra-divine emanation of the Unknowable Infinite (Ein Sof) into the ten divine attributes (Sphirot).

Syntactic Shift in 1:1: The Zohar reads Bereshit bara Elohim with a radical grammatical shift: "By means of Bereshit [Chochmah/Wisdom], IT [Ein Sof] carved out Elohim [Binah/Understanding]!" Elohim is the direct object, not the subject! The opening verse recounts how the Unknowable Concealed Spark etched out the Divine Mother Binah.

The Fall as Uncoupling: In Genesis 2–3, Adam ha-Rishon represents the synthesis of all worlds. Eve (Nukva/Shechinah) was originally emanated attached back-to-back (Achor be-Achor) with Adam (Tiferet) before being separated for face-to-face union (Panim el-Panim). The sin of eating from the Tree of Knowledge was the uncoupling of the fruit from its root—severing the Shechinah from the upper Sphirot and plunging the world into exile.

1:1By means of Primordial Wisdom (Bereshit/Chochmah), the Hidden Concealed Unknowable (Ein Sof) carved out the Divine Mother of Understanding (Elohim/Binah), emanating the total spectrum of Speech (Et: Aleph to Tav) from Heaven (Tiferet) to Earth (Malkhut).
1:3And the Divine Mother (Elohim) spoke: "Let Light (Chesed/Lovingkindness) flash forth!" and Lovingkindness shone.
2:7Then YHWH-Elohim molded Primordial Adam from the holy dust of the upper Red Soil (Malkhut), blowing into his nostrils the divine soul-breath (Neshamah) from the highest Crown.
2:22And YHWH-Elohim built the female side (Nukva) into a complete holy Woman, bringing her face-to-face (Panim el-Panim) with Adam.
3:6And the Woman saw that the tree was desirable for self-exaltation, so she took of its fruit and severed it from its divine root; and she gave also to her husband beside her, and he ate.
3:21And YHWH-Elohim made for Adam and his wife garments of physical skin (Kotnot 'Or with Ayin), clothing them in dense matter instead of their original robes of light (Kotnot 'Or with Aleph).

4. The Philonic / Platonist Archetypal Hermeneutic (with Integrated LXX)

Context: 1st Century CE Hellenistic Judaism · Philo of Alexandria · Intelligible World (Kosmos Noetos)

The Intelligible Realm and the Greek Septuagint

Philo of Alexandria read Genesis strictly in Greek via the Septuagint (LXX). In his Platonist hermeneutic (De Opificio Mundi), Genesis 1 describes the creation of the Kosmos Noetos—the incorporeal, intelligible universe of Ideas and Archetypes existing in the Divine Reason (Logos). Physical, sensible creation does not occur until Genesis 2.

Allegorical Triad: Adam represents the Intellect / Mind (Nous). Eve represents Sense-Perception (Aisthesis). The Serpent represents Pleasure (Hedone). The Fall occurs when Pleasure seduces Sense-Perception, which then drags Mind down from contemplation into sensory passion.

1:1ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆνIn the Divine Reason (Logos), God created the incorporeal, intelligible universe of Ideas—the archetypal heaven and the archetypal earth.
1:3καὶ εἶπεν ὁ θεός γενηθήτω φῶς καὶ ἐγένετο φῶςAnd God willed, "Let intelligible light exist!" and the ideal archetype of light came into being.
1:27καὶ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν ἄνθρωπον κατ εἰκόνα θεοῦ ἐποίησεν αὐτόνAnd God created Ideal Man in His Divine Image; in the Image of God He created him—a purely incorporeal, sexless, rational Mind (Nous).
2:7καὶ ἔπλασεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν ἄνθρωπον χοῦν ἀπὸ τῆς γῆςThen God molded physical man out of matter-dust, breathing into his earthly face a ray of divine Mind (Nous), so that the physical man became an embodied soul.
3:1ὁ δὲ ὄφις ἦν φρονιμώτατος πάντων τῶν θηρίωνNow the Serpent (Pleasure/Hedone) was the most insidious of all sensory impulses. And Pleasure whispered to Sense-Perception (Eve)...
3:6καὶ εἶδεν ἡ γυνὴ ὅτι καλὸν τὸ ξύλον εἰς βρῶσινAnd when Sense-Perception saw that sensory indulgence was delightful, she partook of vice and seduced Mind (Adam), and Intellect succumbed to passion.

5. The "LXX-Only" Literal Greek Hermeneutic

Context: Hellenistic & Early Church Greek Literalist · Zero Knowledge of Hebrew · Green/Latinate Diction

The Pure Greek Worldview

This tradition approaches Genesis 1–3 strictly through the Greek text of the Septuagint, completely ignorant of Semitic underlying puns, Hebrew root constructs, or Canaanite cultural context. It reads the Greek as the original, unmediated Word of God, adopting Hellenistic philosophical and legal terminology.

Linguistic & Conceptual Shifts:

  • In the beginning (En arche): Understood not as a temporal clause ("When God began..."), but as an absolute cosmic starting point or principle.
  • Firmament (Stereoma): A solid, crystalline dome or spherical vault holding back liquid cosmic oceans.
  • Paradise of Delight (Paradeisos tes tryphes): A lush royal Hellenistic walled park or enclosure.
  • Serpent (Ophis/Drakon): A crafty wild beast or dragon (therion).
  • Coats of Leather (Chitonas dermatinous): Literal skin tunics provided by the Lord God.
  • Seraphim & Cherubim: Heavenly winged wardens set to guard the entry to the Tree of Life.

1:2ἡ δὲ γῆ ἦν ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος καὶ σκότος ἐπάνω τῆς ἀβύσσουNow the earth was unseen and unformed, and darkness was upon the abyss, and the Spirit of God was borne above the water.
