Bereshit

Genesis 1:1 – 4:18 · Hebrew → English

An original translation, made from the Hebrew text with the Septuagint beside it, of the first verses of the book. The English is deliberately strange and primal: the aim is a text the reader does not recognise, not one they half remember. The middle column gives the Greek. The right-hand column carries the commentary: root sense, the readings the Septuagint takes instead, and every place the register bends the Hebrew.

No published English translation was consulted.

Conventions. Elohim: Divinity. YHWH: the Lord. Adamah: the red earth. Erets: the ground (land only for place names). Nachash: the serpent. Ish and ishah are kept as the Hebrew for the pun. Each verse's apparatus lists the readings held open.

1:1 At the start, Divinity בָּרָ֣א: the sky, and the ground.
Septuagintἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆν
בְּרֵאשִׁ֖ית בָּרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֑ים אֵ֥ת הַשָּׁמַ֖יִם וְאֵ֥ת הָאָֽרֶץ׃

Re'shit (רֵאשִׁית) is a construct: 'at the head of' or 'in the beginning of'. Read as a temporal clause, the verse becomes subordinate; the English keeps the main-clause reading, 'At the start, Divinity cut'. Bara' (בָּרָא) is used of Divinity in the Qal alone; its root is disputed, and 'cut' is one proposal, kept as the head term. 'Of it' is added to re'shit. The two objects, 'the sky' and 'the ground', are given as the English; 'erets is not adamah, and 'ground' is its head term. The particle 'et (אֵת) marks the object and is not rendered. LXX: 'en archē epoiēsen ho theos', 'in beginning made Divinity'; poiein replaces bara', and archē has no article.

Apparatus
  • re'shit: 'beginning' or 'head'. The construct allows 'at the beginning of' and the temporal reading 'when Divinity began'.
  • bara': 'cut' is the chosen head term; the LXX has poiēsen, 'made'. The root is disputed.

1:2 The ground was a waste and a void. Emptiness, nothing in it. And dark was on the face of the deep. And the wind of Divinity, fluttering over the face of the waters.
Septuagintἡ δὲ γῆ ἦν ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος καὶ σκότος ἐπάνω τῆς ἀβύσσου καὶ πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἐπεφέρετο ἐπάνω τοῦ ὕδατος

Tohu and bohu (תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ): 'waste, emptiness'; a stammering pair, kept as a pair. The English gives 'a waste and a void', then 'emptiness, nothing in it'. Tehom (תְּהוֹם), 'the deep', is compared by some to Tiamat; no etymology is established, and the English keeps 'the deep' in lowercase. Merachefet (מְרַחֶפֶת) is the participle of rachaf, used of a bird hovering over its young (Deut 32:11); the English 'fluttering over' keeps the participle without the simile. Ruach (רוּחַ) is wind, breath, or spirit; 'Wind' is chosen. LXX adds 'invisible' (aoratos) and 'unfurnished' (akataskeuastos), neither in the Hebrew, and renders the deep as 'abyss'.

Apparatus
  • tohu wabohu: 'waste and emptiness'. A stammering pair, not two separate nouns.
  • ruach: wind, breath, or spirit. 'Wind' is chosen; the other two stay open.
  • merachefet: fluttering, brooding. The bird over its young is Deut 32:11.

1:6 Divinity said: Let there be רָקִ֖יעַ, in the middle of the waters, and let it מַבְדִּ֔יל water from water.
Septuagintκαὶ εἶπεν ὁ θεός γενηθήτω στερέωμα ἐν μέσῳ τοῦ ὕδατος καὶ ἔστω διαχωρίζον ἀνὰ μέσον ὕδατος καὶ ὕδατος καὶ ἐγένετο οὕτως
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֔ים יְהִ֥י רָקִ֖יעַ בְּת֣וֹךְ הַמָּ֑יִם וִיהִ֣י מַבְדִּ֔יל בֵּ֥ין מַ֖יִם לָמָֽיִם׃

Raqia' (רָקִיעַ), from raqa', 'to beat out, spread thin'. 'Skin' and 'plate' keep the hammering; 'firmament' (Latin, 'support') drops it. Bein mayim lamayim is the ordinary idiom 'between water and water' (cf. Deut 17:8); nothing is odd about the lamed. LXX stereōma, 'firm thing'. LXX ends 1:6 with 'and it was so' (kai egeneto houtōs) and omits it at 1:7, where the Hebrew has it: the formula falls in a different place.

Apparatus
  • raqia': a hammered sheet or the firmament. The root is 'to beat out'.
  • lamayim: 'water to water' or 'to the waters'. Both readings are open.

LXX reads dia tou theou, 'through Divinity', for the Hebrew 'et the Lord: a witness reading that makes the 'et a means, not an apposition.
