TranslationCommentary
1:1 At the head of it, at the first of the first, when God began to hew: heaven, yes; earth, yes.
בְּרֵאשִׁ֖ית בָּרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֑ים אֵ֥ת הַשָּׁמַ֖יִם וְאֵ֥ת הָאָֽרֶץ׃

Re'shit (רֵאשִׁית) is a construct: 'at the head of' or 'in the beginning of'. Read as a temporal clause ('when God began'), the verse becomes subordinate and the making an event rather than a fact. Bara' (בָּרָא) is used in the Qal only of God; its root is disputed, and the sense 'to cut' is one proposal. 'Hew' is my choice and stakes that sense; 'create' is the usual English and hides the physical verb. The particle 'et (אֵת) marks the direct object and has no English counterpart; I render it as the emphatic 'yes'. LXX: 'en archē epoiēsen ho theos' — no article on 'archē', and poiein ('make') replaces bara'. The LXX's 2:3 reads the same verb as 'began to make' (see there), which supports the temporal reading.

1:2 And the ground was a no-shape, a no-hollow, dark on the face of the deep; and the breath of God, quivering, hovering, over the face of the waters.
וְהָאָ֗רֶץ הָיְתָ֥ה תֹ֙הוּ֙ וָבֹ֔הוּ וְחֹ֖שֶׁךְ עַל־פְּנֵ֣י תְה֑וֹם וְר֣וּחַ אֱלֹהִ֔ים מְרַחֶ֖פֶת עַל־פְּנֵ֥י הַמָּֽיִם׃

Tohu and bohu (תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ): a pair of nouns whose sense is 'waste, formlessness, emptiness'; the pair is alliterative and stammering, and I keep both. Tehom (תְּהוֹם), 'the deep', is compared by some to the Babylonian Tiamat; the etymology is not established. Merachefet (מְרַחֶפֶת) is the participle of rachaf, used of a bird hovering over its young (Deut 32:11); 'quivering' takes the brooding sense bodily. Ruach (רוּחַ) is wind, breath, or spirit; I choose breath. LXX adds 'invisible' (aoratos) and 'unfurnished' (akataskeustos), neither in the Hebrew, and renders the deep as 'abyss'. The additions witness an interpretive tradition, not a different Hebrew.

1:3 God spoke: Be-light! And light happened.
וַיֹּ֥אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֖ים יְהִ֣י א֑וֹר וַֽיְהִי־אֽוֹר׃

Yehi or (יְהִי אוֹר) 'let there be light'; vayehi or (וַיְהִי־אוֹר) 'and light happened'. Hayah (הָיָה) means both 'be' and 'become, happen'; 'there was' loses the event. 'Be-light' is my compound, keeping the imperative inside the noun. LXX: 'let it become light' (genēthētō phōs) — the Greek's 'become' is nearer the Hebrew's happening than the English 'let there be'.

1:4 God saw the light: it was good. Then God cut a seam between the light and the dark— a parting, a partition.
וַיַּ֧רְא אֱלֹהִ֛ים אֶת־הָא֖וֹר כִּי־ט֑וֹב וַיַּבְדֵּ֣ל אֱלֹהִ֔ים בֵּ֥ין הָא֖וֹר וּבֵ֥ין הַחֹֽשֶׁךְ׃

Badal (וַיַּבְדֵּל), 'separate, divide'. The 'seam' is my image for the verb's physical edge. Tov (טוֹב) is 'good' in the sense of pleasing, a judgment of fitness rather than of virtue; LXX kalon ('beautiful') is a different register. LXX: diechōrisen, 'separated', with ana meson, 'in the middle'.

1:5 God called the light Day, and the dark he called Night. Dusk came; dawn came. One day.
וַיִּקְרָ֨א אֱלֹהִ֤ים ׀ לָאוֹר֙ י֔וֹם וְלַחֹ֖שֶׁךְ קָ֣רָא לָ֑יְלָה וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם אֶחָֽד׃ {פ}

The first day is 'yom echad' (יוֹם אֶחָד), 'one day', not 'first day'. Elsewhere the count is ordinal (sheni, 'second'; shelishi, 'third'); the first is counted as a unit. 'Erev (עֶרֶב) is evening; boqer (בֹּקֶר) is morning. LXX: 'hēmera mia', the same cardinal.

1:6 God said: let a hammered sheet be in the belly of the waters, a splitter, a parting— water from water.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֔ים יְהִ֥י רָקִ֖יעַ בְּת֣וֹךְ הַמָּ֑יִם וִיהִ֣י מַבְדִּ֔יל בֵּ֥ין מַ֖יִם לָמָֽיִם׃

Raqia' (רָקִיעַ) comes from raqa', 'to beat out, to spread thin' as metal is beaten. 'Firmament' (Latin firmamentum; LXX stereōma, 'firm thing') replaces the image of hammering with one of support. 'Lamayim' (לָמָיִם) is grammatically odd: 'to the waters', or 'water (from) water'. The doubled 'mayim' is a stammer and I keep it.

1:7 God hammered the sheet out, parted the waters— those beneath the sheet, those above the sheet. And so it was: thus.
וַיַּ֣עַשׂ אֱלֹהִים֮ אֶת־הָרָקִ֒יעַ֒ וַיַּבְדֵּ֗ל בֵּ֤ין הַמַּ֙יִם֙ אֲשֶׁר֙ מִתַּ֣חַת לָרָקִ֔יעַ וּבֵ֣ין הַמַּ֔יִם אֲשֶׁ֖ר מֵעַ֣ל לָרָקִ֑יעַ וַֽיְהִי־כֵֽן׃

Vayya'as (וַיַּעַשׂ), 'made'. The verb changes from bara' to 'asah, the ordinary 'make'; the sheet is worked, not created. Wayehi-ken (וַיְהִי־כֵן): 'and it became so'. Ken (כֵּן) is 'thus', a deictic, pointing at what has happened. LXX: 'houtōs', 'thus'.

1:8 God named the sheet: Sky. Dusk; dawn. Day two.
וַיִּקְרָ֧א אֱלֹהִ֛ים לָֽרָקִ֖יעַ שָׁמָ֑יִם וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם שֵׁנִֽי׃ {פ}

Shamayim (שָׁמַיִם), 'the heavens', plural in form. An old folk etymology hears sham mayim, 'there are waters'; this is not an established derivation, and the sound is part of the point. Day two has no 'and God saw that it was good' formula, which commentators have noted. LXX: ouranos.

1:9 God said: let the waters gather into one place— let them pool, let them wait— and let the dry come up. And so it was.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֗ים יִקָּו֨וּ הַמַּ֜יִם מִתַּ֤חַת הַשָּׁמַ֙יִם֙ אֶל־מָק֣וֹם אֶחָ֔ד וְתֵרָאֶ֖ה הַיַּבָּשָׁ֑ה וַֽיְהִי־כֵֽן׃

Yiqqavu (יִקָּווּ), from qavah: 'to wait, to gather into a line, to hope'. The sense spans gathering and waiting; I keep both. Tera'eh (תֵרָאֶה), 'let it be seen': the dry is made visible. Yabbashah (יַבָּשָׁה), 'the dry', is an ordinary noun that stands alone.

1:10 God called the dry: Land. And the pooling of the waters— the waiting-place— he called Seas. God saw: good.
וַיִּקְרָ֨א אֱלֹהִ֤ים ׀ לַיַּבָּשָׁה֙ אֶ֔רֶץ וּלְמִקְוֵ֥ה הַמַּ֖יִם קָרָ֣א יַמִּ֑ים וַיַּ֥רְא אֱלֹהִ֖ים כִּי־טֽוֹב׃

Miqveh (מִקְוֵה), from the same root as qavah: 'gathering-place, pool, reservoir'. The word for 'hope' (tiqvah) shares the root; I keep 'waiting-place' so that the verbs bind. Yamim (יַמִּים), 'seas', plural. LXX: thalassas.

1:11 God said: let the ground green-up a greening: grass that seeds, fruit-trees making fruit after their kind, seed inside them, on the ground. And so it was.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֗ים תַּֽדְשֵׁ֤א הָאָ֙רֶץ֙ דֶּ֗שֶׁא עֵ֚שֶׂב מַזְרִ֣יעַ זֶ֔רַע עֵ֣ץ פְּרִ֞י עֹ֤שֶׂה פְּרִי֙ לְמִינ֔וֹ אֲשֶׁ֥ר זַרְעוֹ־ב֖וֹ עַל־הָאָ֑רֶץ וַֽיְהִי־כֵֽן׃

Tadshe' (תַּדְשֵׁא), from dasha', is a cognate accusative: 'green a greening', 'sprout sprouting'. The verb and its noun (deshe') are one word. Lemino (לְמִינוֹ), 'after its kind' (min, species). The fruit-tree's seed is 'in it' (bo). LXX: 'blastēsatō', 'let it sprout', with 'botanē chortou', 'herb of grass' — the Greek flattens the verb-noun pair.

1:12 The ground brought out greening: grass seeding its kind; tree making fruit, its seed in it, after its kind. God saw: good.
וַתּוֹצֵ֨א הָאָ֜רֶץ דֶּ֠שֶׁא עֵ֣שֶׂב מַזְרִ֤יעַ זֶ֙רַע֙ לְמִינֵ֔הוּ וְעֵ֧ץ עֹֽשֶׂה־פְּרִ֛י אֲשֶׁ֥ר זַרְעוֹ־ב֖וֹ לְמִינֵ֑הוּ וַיַּ֥רְא אֱלֹהִ֖ים כִּי־טֽוֹב׃

Wattotse' (וַתּוֹצֵא): the ground, feminine, brings forth. The earth is the agent, like a mother. The mood shifts from the command of 1:11 to narrated fact. LXX: exēnenken, 'brought forth'.

1:13 Dusk; dawn. Day three.
וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם שְׁלִישִֽׁי׃ {פ}

The day-count is a frame: the formula repeats and carries the narrative. Compare 1:5.

1:14 God said: let there be lights— fire-holders— in the hammered sky, splitting day from night. Let them be signs, appointed meetings, days and years.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֗ים יְהִ֤י מְאֹרֹת֙ בִּרְקִ֣יעַ הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם לְהַבְדִּ֕יל בֵּ֥ין הַיּ֖וֹם וּבֵ֣ין הַלָּ֑יְלָה וְהָי֤וּ לְאֹתֹת֙ וּלְמ֣וֹעֲדִ֔ים וּלְיָמִ֖ים וְשָׁנִֽים׃

Me'orot (מְאֹרֹת), from 'or (light, fire): 'lamps, light-bearers'. The text does not name sun or moon here. Lemo'adim (לְמוֹעֲדִים), 'for appointed times' (from ya'ad, 'to meet at an appointed place'). Otot (אֹתֹת), 'signs, marks'. Shanim (שָׁנִים) is the first use of 'year' as a unit (shanah, 'to repeat, to change'). LXX: phōstēres, sēmeia, kairous, hēmeras, eniautous — a list of nouns.

1:15 Let them be fire-holders in the hammered sky, to give light upon the ground. And so it was.
וְהָי֤וּ לִמְאוֹרֹת֙ בִּרְקִ֣יעַ הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם לְהָאִ֖יר עַל־הָאָ֑רֶץ וַֽיְהִי־כֵֽן׃

Lehe'ir (לְהָאִיר), the causative of 'or: 'to shine, to give light'. The verb ties the lamps to the light of 1:3.

1:16 God made the two great lamps: the great one for the rule of day, the lesser one for the rule of night— and the stars.
וַיַּ֣עַשׂ אֱלֹהִ֔ים אֶת־שְׁנֵ֥י הַמְּאֹרֹ֖ת הַגְּדֹלִ֑ים אֶת־הַמָּא֤וֹר הַגָּדֹל֙ לְמֶמְשֶׁ֣לֶת הַיּ֔וֹם וְאֶת־הַמָּא֤וֹר הַקָּטֹן֙ לְמֶמְשֶׁ֣לֶת הַלַּ֔יְלָה וְאֵ֖ת הַכּוֹכָבִֽים׃

Memshelet (מֶמְשֶׁלֶת), 'dominion, rule', from mashal (to rule). The same root gives mashal, 'proverb, parable': the rule of day is also the day's saying. The text names the two lamps 'great' and 'lesser' and avoids the names shemesh and yarach; commentators have remarked on this. LXX keeps the two-lamp structure: phōstēra ton megan, ton elasson.

1:17 God set them in the hammered sky to give light upon the ground.
וַיִּתֵּ֥ן אֹתָ֛ם אֱלֹהִ֖ים בִּרְקִ֣יעַ הַשָּׁמָ֑יִם לְהָאִ֖יר עַל־הָאָֽרֶץ׃

Wayyiten (וַיִּתֵּן), 'gave, set'. The purpose clause of 1:15 is repeated, now with God as the actor rather than the one commanding.

1:18 to rule the day and the night, to cut the seam between the light and the dark. God saw: good.
וְלִמְשֹׁל֙ בַּיּ֣וֹם וּבַלַּ֔יְלָה וּֽלְהַבְדִּ֔יל בֵּ֥ין הָא֖וֹר וּבֵ֣ין הַחֹ֑שֶׁךְ וַיַּ֥רְא אֱלֹהִ֖ים כִּי־טֽוֹב׃

Lim-shol (לִמְשֹׁל), 'to rule', the same root as mashal (1:16). Lehavdil (לְהַבְדִּיל) repeats the badal of 1:4: the seam is cut twice.

1:19 Dusk; dawn. Day four.
וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם רְבִיעִֽי׃ {פ}

Rebi'i (רְבִיעִי), the ordinal.

1:20 God said: let the waters swarm— a swarm of living breath, and winged things flying over the ground, across the face of the hammered sky.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֔ים יִשְׁרְצ֣וּ הַמַּ֔יִם שֶׁ֖רֶץ נֶ֣פֶשׁ חַיָּ֑ה וְעוֹף֙ יְעוֹפֵ֣ף עַל־הָאָ֔רֶץ עַל־פְּנֵ֖י רְקִ֥יעַ הַשָּׁמָֽיִם׃

Yishretzu (יִשְׁרְצוּ), from sheretz: small creatures that teem and crawl, insects and fish. 'Swarm' carries the teeming. Nefesh chayah (נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה): 'living breath, living creature'. Nefesh is breath, throat, self; LXX psychē zōsa, 'living soul', puts the soul into the animal. Ye'opef (יְעוֹפֵף): a doubled verb of flying — 'fly-flying'.

1:21 God cut the great serpents— tanninim, the sea-monsters— and every breathing swarm the waters teemed with, after its kind, and every winged fowl after its kind. God saw: good.
וַיִּבְרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֔ים אֶת־הַתַּנִּינִ֖ם הַגְּדֹלִ֑ים וְאֵ֣ת כׇּל־נֶ֣פֶשׁ הַֽחַיָּ֣ה ׀ הָֽרֹמֶ֡שֶׂת אֲשֶׁר֩ שָׁרְצ֨וּ הַמַּ֜יִם לְמִֽינֵהֶ֗ם וְאֵ֨ת כׇּל־ע֤וֹף כָּנָף֙ לְמִינֵ֔הוּ וַיַּ֥רְא אֱלֹהִ֖ים כִּי־טֽוֹב׃

Tanninim (תַּנִּינִם), 'sea-serpents, great monsters'. It is the creature-word of the older chaos-myths (Ps 74:13). The verb is bara' again: the great monsters are made, not slain. Commentators have often noted the absence of combat.

1:22 God blessed them, saying: burst, break through, swarm, fill the waters in the seas, let the flyer multiply on the ground.
וַיְבָ֧רֶךְ אֹתָ֛ם אֱלֹהִ֖ים לֵאמֹ֑ר פְּר֣וּ וּרְב֗וּ וּמִלְא֤וּ אֶת־הַמַּ֙יִם֙ בַּיַּמִּ֔ים וְהָע֖וֹף יִ֥רֶב בָּאָֽרֶץ׃

Wayevarekh (וַיְבָרֶךְ): 'blessed'. The root barak is often linked to 'knee' (berekh), though the link is debated. Peru (פְּרוּ), from parah: 'break out, bear fruit'. Rivu (רְבוּ), from rabah: 'become many, a crowd'. Mil'u (מִלְאוּ), 'fill'. Yirev (יִרֶב), 'multiply, swarm'. LXX: auxanesthe, plēthynesthe, plērōsate — three verbs of growth; the Hebrew's 'burst' is lost.

1:23 Dusk; dawn. Day five.
וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם חֲמִישִֽׁי׃ {פ}

Hamishi (חֲמִישִׁי), the ordinal.

1:24 God said: let the ground bring out living breath after its kind— cattle, creeping things, wild beasts of the ground after their kind. And so it was.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֗ים תּוֹצֵ֨א הָאָ֜רֶץ נֶ֤פֶשׁ חַיָּה֙ לְמִינָ֔הּ בְּהֵמָ֥ה וָרֶ֛מֶשׂ וְחַֽיְתוֹ־אֶ֖רֶץ לְמִינָ֑הּ וַֽיְהִי־כֵֽן׃

Totse' (תּוֹצֵא): 'bring out'. The ground brings forth again, as in 1:12. Chayat ha-aretz (חַיְתוֹ־אֶרֶץ), 'beasts of the ground'. Remesh (רֶמֶשׂ), 'crawler'. The animals come in three kinds: cattle, creeper, wild beast.

1:25 God made the wild of the ground after its kind, the cattle after its kind, and every crawler of the red after its kind. God saw: good.
וַיַּ֣עַשׂ אֱלֹהִים֩ אֶת־חַיַּ֨ת הָאָ֜רֶץ לְמִינָ֗הּ וְאֶת־הַבְּהֵמָה֙ לְמִינָ֔הּ וְאֵ֛ת כׇּל־רֶ֥מֶשׂ הָֽאֲדָמָ֖ה לְמִינֵ֑הוּ וַיַּ֥רְא אֱלֹהִ֖ים כִּי־טֽוֹב׃

Adamah (אֲדָמָה) is red soil or ground; the root of adam (human) is the same family. The human will be 'from the red' (2:7). 'After its kind' (lemino) recurs throughout chapter 1; its rhythm is part of the sound of the chapter.

1:26 God said: let us make the human— adam, out of the red— in our image, in our likeness. Let them tread the fish of the sea, the fowl of the sky, cattle, all the ground, and every creeper that creeps on the ground.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֔ים נַֽעֲשֶׂ֥ה אָדָ֛ם בְּצַלְמֵ֖נוּ כִּדְמוּתֵ֑נוּ וְיִרְדּוּ֩ בִדְגַ֨ת הַיָּ֜ם וּבְע֣וֹף הַשָּׁמַ֗יִם וּבַבְּהֵמָה֙ וּבְכׇל־הָאָ֔רֶץ וּבְכׇל־הָרֶ֖מֶשׂ הָֽרֹמֵ֥שׂ עַל־הָאָֽרֶץ׃

Na'aseh (נַעֲשֶׂה), 'let us make': a first-person plural. Readings include a divine council, a royal 'we', and an address to heavenly beings; none is secure. Tselem (צֶלֶם), 'image': from a root some link to 'shade' or 'cut'; 'shadow' is a live reading. Demut (דְּמוּת), 'likeness'. Yirdu (יִרְדּוּ), from radah: 'tread, trample, rule'. LXX: poiēsōmen, 'let us make', with hēmeteran, 'ours'; archetōsan renders 'rule' with a softer verb.

1:27 God cut the human, adam— into its shadow; into God's shadow he cut him. Male and female he cut them.
וַיִּבְרָ֨א אֱלֹהִ֤ים ׀ אֶת־הָֽאָדָם֙ בְּצַלְמ֔וֹ בְּצֶ֥לֶם אֱלֹהִ֖ים בָּרָ֣א אֹת֑וֹ זָכָ֥ר וּנְקֵבָ֖ה בָּרָ֥א אֹתָֽם׃

Bara' (בָּרָא) occurs three times in this verse, a hammer striking three times. LXX epoiēsen ('made') each time: the threefold verb is flattened. Tselem recurs: 'in its image, in the image of God'. The pronoun 'his' (bo, wo) can refer to God or to the human; English must choose, and the Hebrew holds both.

1:28 God blessed them. God said to them: burst, break through, fill— the ground: tread it, trample it as one trample the washed cloth— and tread the fish of the sea, the fowl of the sky, each crawler that creeps on the ground.
וַיְבָ֣רֶךְ אֹתָם֮ אֱלֹהִים֒ וַיֹּ֨אמֶר לָהֶ֜ם אֱלֹהִ֗ים פְּר֥וּ וּרְב֛וּ וּמִלְא֥וּ אֶת־הָאָ֖רֶץ וְכִבְשֻׁ֑הָ וּרְד֞וּ בִּדְגַ֤ת הַיָּם֙ וּבְע֣וֹף הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם וּבְכׇל־חַיָּ֖ה הָֽרֹמֶ֥שֶׂת עַל־הָאָֽרֶץ׃

Kivshuha (כִּבְשֻׁהָ), from kavash: 'to subdue'. The root is shared with kavas, 'to press and beat cloth'. The Hebrew carries the washing-floor image; LXX katakyrieusate, 'lord it over', is a legal and imperial verb. Radu (רְדוּ): 'tread, rule', the verb of 1:26.

1:29 God said: Look— I have given you every seed-spilling herb over the whole face of the ground, and every tree that holds seed-fruit in it. To you it shall be for eating.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֗ים הִנֵּה֩ נָתַ֨תִּי לָכֶ֜ם אֶת־כׇּל־עֵ֣שֶׂב ׀ זֹרֵ֣עַ זֶ֗רַע אֲשֶׁר֙ עַל־פְּנֵ֣י כׇל־הָאָ֔רֶץ וְאֶת־כׇּל־הָעֵ֛ץ אֲשֶׁר־בּ֥וֹ פְרִי־עֵ֖ץ זֹרֵ֣עַ זָ֑רַע לָכֶ֥ם יִֽהְיֶ֖ה לְאׇכְלָֽה׃

Hinneh natati (הִנֵּה נָתַתִּי), 'look, I have given': the perfect with hinneh states a present fact. Lakhem (לָכֶם), 'to you'. The first diet is grain, herb, and fruit, and the animals' food is given in 1:30. LXX: idou dedōka, the same 'look'.

1:30 And to every beast of the ground, every fowl of the sky, every crawler on the ground, each with breath in it: all the green of the grass, for eating. And so it was.
וּֽלְכׇל־חַיַּ֣ת הָ֠אָ֠רֶץ וּלְכׇל־ע֨וֹף הַשָּׁמַ֜יִם וּלְכֹ֣ל ׀ רוֹמֵ֣שׂ עַל־הָאָ֗רֶץ אֲשֶׁר־בּוֹ֙ נֶ֣פֶשׁ חַיָּ֔ה אֶת־כׇּל־יֶ֥רֶק עֵ֖שֶׂב לְאׇכְלָ֑ה וַֽיְהִי־כֵֽן׃

Yereq (יֶרֶק), 'green, greenness'. Every animal is fed on the green. The chapter closes the day's acts with 'and so it was'.

1:31 God saw everything he had made— and look: good, very good. Dusk; dawn: day six.
וַיַּ֤רְא אֱלֹהִים֙ אֶת־כׇּל־אֲשֶׁ֣ר עָשָׂ֔ה וְהִנֵּה־ט֖וֹב מְאֹ֑ד וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם הַשִּׁשִּֽׁי׃ {פ}

Tov me'od (טוֹב מְאֹד), 'good, exceedingly'. LXX kala lian, 'very beautiful'. The Hebrew judges the whole of the work tov; the LXX's kalos is a wider register of beauty.

Chapter 2

TranslationCommentary
2:1 So the heaven and the earth were finished, and all their host.
וַיְכֻלּ֛וּ הַשָּׁמַ֥יִם וְהָאָ֖רֶץ וְכׇל־צְבָאָֽם׃

Vayekhullu (וַיְכֻלּוּ), 'were finished, completed'. Tzeva'am (צְבָאָם), 'their army, host': the stars and the whole muster. LXX: pas ho kosmos autōn, 'all their order' — the LXX substitutes 'cosmos' for 'host'.

2:2 God completed—on the seventh day— the work he had made; and he ceased on the seventh day from all his work he had made.
וַיְכַ֤ל אֱלֹהִים֙ בַּיּ֣וֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִ֔י מְלַאכְתּ֖וֹ אֲשֶׁ֣ר עָשָׂ֑ה וַיִּשְׁבֹּת֙ בַּיּ֣וֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִ֔י מִכׇּל־מְלַאכְתּ֖וֹ אֲשֶׁ֥ר עָשָֽׂה׃

Witness variant. The Hebrew says 'the seventh day' (ha-yom ha-shevi'i) for the completion; the LXX reads 'the sixth day' (en tē hēmera tē hektē) here. Shavat (שָׁבַת), 'cease, stop', is the root from which 'sabbath' derives, but this verse does not yet name a day of rest as an institution. The doubled 'work he had made' sounds the making stopping.

2:3 God knelt in blessing toward the seventh day, cut it apart, set it aside, because in it he stopped from all the work that God cut to make.
וַיְבָ֤רֶךְ אֱלֹהִים֙ אֶת־י֣וֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִ֔י וַיְקַדֵּ֖שׁ אֹת֑וֹ כִּ֣י ב֤וֹ שָׁבַת֙ מִכׇּל־מְלַאכְתּ֔וֹ אֲשֶׁר־בָּרָ֥א אֱלֹהִ֖ים לַעֲשֽׂוֹת׃ {פ}

Vayevarekh (וַיְבָרֶךְ) 'blessed'; vayqaddesh (וַיְקַדֵּשׁ) 'made holy', from qadosh, 'set apart, cut away'. The verb bara' with the infinitive la'asot (לַעֲשׂוֹת) is a doubled verb: 'which God cut to make'. LXX: 'which God began to make' (hōn ērxato ho theos poiēsai). The LXX reads the infinitive as 'began' — the reading that supports the temporal reading of 1:1.

2:4 These are the begettings— toledot, the outcomes— of the heaven and the earth, in their being cut, in the day YHWH God made earth and heaven.
אֵ֣לֶּה תוֹלְד֧וֹת הַשָּׁמַ֛יִם וְהָאָ֖רֶץ בְּהִבָּֽרְאָ֑ם בְּי֗וֹם עֲשׂ֛וֹת יְהֹוָ֥ה אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶ֥רֶץ וְשָׁמָֽיִם׃

Toledot (תּוֹלְדוֹת): 'begettings, generations, histories', from yalad, 'to bear, to beget'. LXX: hautē hē biblos geneseōs, 'this is the book of origin' — the source of the Greek title Genesis. The order inverts 1:1 (heaven-and-earth at the start, earth-and-heaven at the end): a chiasm. The LXX keeps heaven-and-earth both times.

2:5 No shrub of the field yet on the ground, no grass of the field yet sprouted— for YHWH God had not rained on the ground, and there was no human to work the red.
וְכֹ֣ל ׀ שִׂ֣יחַ הַשָּׂדֶ֗ה טֶ֚רֶם יִֽהְיֶ֣ה בָאָ֔רֶץ וְכׇל־עֵ֥שֶׂב הַשָּׂדֶ֖ה טֶ֣רֶם יִצְמָ֑ח כִּי֩ לֹ֨א הִמְטִ֜יר יְהֹוָ֤ה אֱלֹהִים֙ עַל־הָאָ֔רֶץ וְאָדָ֣ם אַ֔יִן לַֽעֲבֹ֖ד אֶת־הָֽאֲדָמָֽה׃

Siach (שִׂיחַ), 'shrub'; the root also gives 'meditation, talk' (Ps 77:13). Sadeh (שָׂדֶה), 'open field, wild land'. Ein (אֵין), 'there is not'. Le-ovod (לַעֲבֹד), 'to work, to serve': the same verb is used of human labour and of service to a master or to God.

2:6 But a seep came up from the ground— ed— and wet the whole face of the red.
וְאֵ֖ד יַֽעֲלֶ֣ה מִן־הָאָ֑רֶץ וְהִשְׁקָ֖ה אֶֽת־כׇּל־פְּנֵ֥י הָֽאֲדָמָֽה׃

Ed (אֵד), 'mist, flow, spring'. The ambiguity is kept: the Hebrew can be a vapour or an underground seep. LXX pēgē, 'spring, fountain': the Greek chooses the spring.

2:7 YHWH God shaped the human from dust of the ground, afar, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of lives; and the human became a living breath.
וַיִּ֩יצֶר֩ יְהֹוָ֨ה אֱלֹהִ֜ים אֶת־הָֽאָדָ֗ם עָפָר֙ מִן־הָ֣אֲדָמָ֔ה וַיִּפַּ֥ח בְּאַפָּ֖יו נִשְׁמַ֣ת חַיִּ֑ים וַיְהִ֥י הָֽאָדָ֖ם לְנֶ֥פֶשׁ חַיָּֽה׃

Yatzar (יָצַר): 'to shape, mould' as a potter does. Afar (עָפָר), 'dust'. Appav (אַפָּיו), 'his nostrils'; the same word is used for anger (aph), so breath and fury share the nose. Nishmat chayim (נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים), 'breath of lives' (plural). Nefesh chayah, 'living breath', is the phrase of 1:20–21 for the animals: the human is not a different kind of breathing thing. LXX: eis psychēn zōsan, 'living soul', and eis to prosōpon, 'into the face'.

2:8 YHWH God planted a garden in Eden— a delight— from the front, from of old, from the east— and set there the human he had shaped.
וַיִּטַּ֞ע יְהֹוָ֧ה אֱלֹהִ֛ים גַּן־בְּעֵ֖דֶן מִקֶּ֑דֶם וַיָּ֣שֶׂם שָׁ֔ם אֶת־הָֽאָדָ֖ם אֲשֶׁ֥ר יָצָֽר׃

Eden (עֵדֶן): 'delight, pleasure' (edan). The etymology is disputed; a Sumerian loan (edin, 'steppe') has been proposed. Miqqedem (מִקֶּדֶם): 'from before, of old' or 'eastward'; the Hebrew leaves both. LXX kata anatolas, 'to the east', chooses one. Gan (גַּן), 'enclosure, garden': a walled place.

2:9 From the red, YHWH God made spring every tree longed for to look at and good to eat; the tree of living in the middle of the garden, and the tree of knowing good and harm.
וַיַּצְמַ֞ח יְהֹוָ֤ה אֱלֹהִים֙ מִן־הָ֣אֲדָמָ֔ה כׇּל־עֵ֛ץ נֶחְמָ֥ד לְמַרְאֶ֖ה וְט֣וֹב לְמַאֲכָ֑ל וְעֵ֤ץ הַֽחַיִּים֙ בְּת֣וֹךְ הַגָּ֔ן וְעֵ֕ץ הַדַּ֖עַת ט֥וֹב וָרָֽע׃

Nekhmad (נֶחְמָד): 'desirable, coveted' (cf. 3:6). Chayyim (חַיִּים): 'lives', plural. Da'at (דַּעַת), 'knowing'; yada' is the verb of intimate knowledge (4:1: 'Adam knew Eve'). Tov va-ra' (טוֹב וָרָע): 'good and harm'; ra' carries ruin as well as evil. LXX: to xylon tou eidenai gnōston kalou kai ponērou — 'the tree of knowing, known of good and evil': the LXX adds 'known' (gnōston), which the Hebrew does not say.

2:10 A river came out of Eden to water the garden; from there it parted and became four heads.
וְנָהָר֙ יֹצֵ֣א מֵעֵ֔דֶן לְהַשְׁק֖וֹת אֶת־הַגָּ֑ן וּמִשָּׁם֙ יִפָּרֵ֔ד וְהָיָ֖ה לְאַרְבָּעָ֥ה רָאשִֽׁים׃

Ro'shim (רָאשִׁים), 'heads, sources, beginnings'. Ro'sh (head) is the root of re'shit (beginning, 1:1). LXX archas, 'beginnings', keeps the echo of archē.

2:11 The first is named Pishon— it circles the whole land of the writhing— where the gold is.
שֵׁ֥ם הָֽאֶחָ֖ד פִּישׁ֑וֹן ה֣וּא הַסֹּבֵ֗ב אֵ֚ת כׇּל־אֶ֣רֶץ הַֽחֲוִילָ֔ה אֲשֶׁר־שָׁ֖ם הַזָּהָֽב׃

Havilah (חֲוִילָה), a place name; the root may be chul, 'to writhe, to whirl, to bring forth in labour'. I keep 'writhing' as a visible echo, not as a claim. Zahav (זָהָב), 'gold'.

2:12 And the gold of that land is good; there is bdolach, and the stone of shoham.
וּֽזְהַ֛ב הָאָ֥רֶץ הַהִ֖וא ט֑וֹב שָׁ֥ם הַבְּדֹ֖לַח וְאֶ֥בֶן הַשֹּֽׁהַם׃

Bedolach (בְּדֹלַח) and shoham (שֹׁהַם) are both of uncertain identity: resin, pearl, or crystal for the first; onyx, beryl, or lapis for the second. LXX gives anthrax (coal, carbuncle) and 'the green stone'. Neither identification is secure.

2:13 The second river: Gihon— it bursts out around all the land of Kush.
וְשֵֽׁם־הַנָּהָ֥ר הַשֵּׁנִ֖י גִּיח֑וֹן ה֣וּא הַסּוֹבֵ֔ב אֵ֖ת כׇּל־אֶ֥רֶץ כּֽוּשׁ׃

Gihon (גִּיחוֹן), from gihah, 'to gush forth': the name hears its own action. Kush (כּוּשׁ) is a southern land, usually located in Nubia or Ethiopia. LXX glosses it Aithiopia.

2:14 The third: Hiddekel— it runs east of Ashur. The fourth: Perat.
וְשֵׁ֨ם הַנָּהָ֤ר הַשְּׁלִישִׁי֙ חִדֶּ֔קֶל ה֥וּא הַֽהֹלֵ֖ךְ קִדְמַ֣ת אַשּׁ֑וּר וְהַנָּהָ֥ר הָֽרְבִיעִ֖י ה֥וּא פְרָֽת׃

Hiddekel (חִדֶּקֶל) is the Tigris; Perat (פְּרָת) the Euphrates. Kidmat (קִדְמַת), 'east of, before', is the word of miqqedem (2:8). LXX: katenanti Assyrion, 'opposite the Assyrians'.

2:15 YHWH God took the human and set him down in the garden of Eden— to serve it, to keep it.
וַיִּקַּ֛ח יְהֹוָ֥ה אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶת־הָֽאָדָ֑ם וַיַּנִּחֵ֣הוּ בְגַן־עֵ֔דֶן לְעׇבְדָ֖הּ וּלְשׇׁמְרָֽהּ׃

La'avdah (לַעֲבְדָהּ), 'to serve, work'; lishmrah (לִשְׁמְרָהּ), 'to keep, guard'. 'Serve' is the verb of slavery and service; LXX ergazesthai, 'work', and phylassein, 'keep', drop the servitude.

2:16 YHWH God commanded the human, saying: Feast, feast on every tree of the garden.
וַיְצַו֙ יְהֹוָ֣ה אֱלֹהִ֔ים עַל־הָֽאָדָ֖ם לֵאמֹ֑ר מִכֹּ֥ל עֵֽץ־הַגָּ֖ן אָכֹ֥ל תֹּאכֵֽל׃

The infinitive absolute: akhol tokhel (אָכֹל תֹּאכֵל), 'eating, eat'. The doubling is emphatic, a permission with weight.

2:17 But from the tree of knowing good and harm you shall not eat— for on the day of your eating from it, dying, you shall die.
וּמֵעֵ֗ץ הַדַּ֙עַת֙ ט֣וֹב וָרָ֔ע לֹ֥א תֹאכַ֖ל מִמֶּ֑נּוּ כִּ֗י בְּי֛וֹם אֲכׇלְךָ֥ מִמֶּ֖נּוּ מ֥וֹת תָּמֽוּת׃

Mot tamut (מוֹת תָּמוּת), 'dying, you shall die': the infinitive absolute again. 'On the day' (be-yom) can name an interval rather than a twenty-four-hour span; the narrative records no death on that day. LXX thanatō apothaneisthe keeps the doubling.

2:18 YHWH God said: not good— the human to be alone. I will make him a helper facing him, a counter-face.
וַיֹּ֙אמֶר֙ יְהֹוָ֣ה אֱלֹהִ֔ים לֹא־ט֛וֹב הֱי֥וֹת הָֽאָדָ֖ם לְבַדּ֑וֹ אֶֽעֱשֶׂה־לּ֥וֹ עֵ֖זֶר כְּנֶגְדּֽוֹ׃

Ezer (עֵזֶר), 'help'. The word is used of God (Ps 33:20) and implies no inferiority in itself. Kenegdo (כְּנֶגְדּוֹ), 'opposite him, before him': a mirror-face. LXX boēthon kat' auton, 'a helper like him': the Greek reads 'like', the Hebrew 'opposite'.

2:19 From the red, YHWH God shaped every beast of the field and every fowl of the sky; he brought them to the human to see what he would call them— and whatever the human called each living breath, that was its name.
וַיִּ֩צֶר֩ יְהֹוָ֨ה אֱלֹהִ֜ים מִן־הָֽאֲדָמָ֗ה כׇּל־חַיַּ֤ת הַשָּׂדֶה֙ וְאֵת֙ כׇּל־ע֣וֹף הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם וַיָּבֵא֙ אֶל־הָ֣אָדָ֔ם לִרְא֖וֹת מַה־יִּקְרָא־ל֑וֹ וְכֹל֩ אֲשֶׁ֨ר יִקְרָא־ל֧וֹ הָֽאָדָ֛ם נֶ֥פֶשׁ חַיָּ֖ה ה֥וּא שְׁמֽוֹ׃

Yatzar again: the verb of 2:7. The animals are shaped from the same red. Mah-yiqra-lo (מַה־יִּקְרָא־לוֹ), 'what he would call them'. Shemo (שְׁמוֹ), 'its name'. LXX: eplasen, 'formed'.

2:20 The human called names— to every cattle, to the fowl of the sky, to every beast of the field. But for the human, no counter-face was found.
וַיִּקְרָ֨א הָֽאָדָ֜ם שֵׁמ֗וֹת לְכׇל־הַבְּהֵמָה֙ וּלְע֣וֹף הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם וּלְכֹ֖ל חַיַּ֣ת הַשָּׂדֶ֑ה וּלְאָדָ֕ם לֹֽא־מָצָ֥א עֵ֖זֶר כְּנֶגְדּֽוֹ׃

Lo matsa (לֹא־מָצָא), 'did not find'. The 'counter-face' of 2:18 is sought and not found. LXX ouch heurethē boēthos homoios, 'no similar helper was found'.

2:21 YHWH God cast a deep trance on the human; he slept; he took one of his sides— tsela— and closed flesh over the place.
וַיַּפֵּל֩ יְהֹוָ֨ה אֱלֹהִ֧ים ׀ תַּרְדֵּמָ֛ה עַל־הָאָדָ֖ם וַיִּישָׁ֑ן וַיִּקַּ֗ח אַחַת֙ מִצַּלְעֹתָ֔יו וַיִּסְגֹּ֥ר בָּשָׂ֖ר תַּחְתֶּֽנָּה׃

Tardemah (תַּרְדֵּמָה): deep sleep, trance (cf. 15:12). Tsela' (צֵלָע): 'side, rib'. Vayisgor (וַיִּסְגֹּר): 'shut, closed'. LXX ekstasin: 'ecstasy', a displacement of the mind.

2:22 YHWH God built the side he had taken from the human into a woman, and brought her to the human.
וַיִּ֩בֶן֩ יְהֹוָ֨ה אֱלֹהִ֧ים ׀ אֶֽת־הַצֵּלָ֛ע אֲשֶׁר־לָקַ֥ח מִן־הָֽאָדָ֖ם לְאִשָּׁ֑ה וַיְבִאֶ֖הָ אֶל־הָֽאָדָֽם׃

Vayiven (וַיִּבֶן): 'built', the verb of building a house (bayit) and of building a family. The rib is built, not bred. LXX ōkodomēsen, 'built'.

2:23 The human said: this time—at last— bone of my bones, flesh of my flesh. This one shall be called Ishah, for from Ish she was taken.
וַיֹּ֘אמֶר֮ הָֽאָדָם֒ זֹ֣את הַפַּ֗עַם עֶ֚צֶם מֵֽעֲצָמַ֔י וּבָשָׂ֖ר מִבְּשָׂרִ֑י לְזֹאת֙ יִקָּרֵ֣א אִשָּׁ֔ה כִּ֥י מֵאִ֖ישׁ לֻֽקְחָה־זֹּֽאת׃

Zot ha-pa'am (זֹאת הַפַּעַם): 'this time', idiomatically 'at last'. Ishah (אִשָּׁה), 'woman'; Ish (אִישׁ), 'man'. The pun works in Hebrew and fails in English, so I keep the transliteration. LXX gynē and andros: the pun is lost, since Greek gynē and anēr do not share a root.

2:24 Therefore: a man leaves his father, his mother— he clings, glues himself, to his woman; and they become one flesh.
עַל־כֵּן֙ יַֽעֲזׇב־אִ֔ישׁ אֶת־אָבִ֖יו וְאֶת־אִמּ֑וֹ וְדָבַ֣ק בְּאִשְׁתּ֔וֹ וְהָי֖וּ לְבָשָׂ֥ר אֶחָֽד׃

Ya'azov (יַעֲזָב), 'leaves, forsakes'. Davaq (דָּבַק), 'cling, weld' (the verb for mortar). 'Therefore' (al-ken) marks the narrator stepping out of the story to explain it: an aetiology.

2:25 Both were bare, the human and his woman, and felt no shame.
וַיִּֽהְי֤וּ שְׁנֵיהֶם֙ עֲרוּמִּ֔ים הָֽאָדָ֖ם וְאִשְׁתּ֑וֹ וְלֹ֖א יִתְבֹּשָֽׁשׁוּ׃

Arumim (עֲרוּמִּים), 'naked, bare'. Arum (עָרוּם), in 3:1, means 'crafty, shrewd'. The same consonants carry both senses; the narrative sets up the pun.

TranslationCommentary
3:1 Now the Hiss was the crafty one— arum: shrewd, and bare— beyond all the beasts of the field YHWH God had made. It said to the woman: Did God truly say— you shall not eat from any tree of the garden?
וְהַנָּחָשׁ֙ הָיָ֣ה עָר֔וּם מִכֹּל֙ חַיַּ֣ת הַשָּׂדֶ֔ה אֲשֶׁ֥ר עָשָׂ֖ה יְהֹוָ֣ה אֱלֹהִ֑ים וַיֹּ֙אמֶר֙ אֶל־הָ֣אִשָּׁ֔ה אַ֚ף כִּֽי־אָמַ֣ר אֱלֹהִ֔ים לֹ֣א תֹֽאכְל֔וּ מִכֹּ֖ל עֵ֥ץ הַגָּֽן׃

Nachash (נָחָשׁ), 'serpent'. The word sits near nechoshet (bronze) and nachash (divination). I render 'the Hiss' for the sibilance. Af ki (אַף כִּי), 'really?', a question of emphasis. LXX asks 'why, that God said' (ti hoti eipen), a different syntax.

3:2 The woman said to the Hiss: From the fruit of the trees of the garden we eat.
וַתֹּ֥אמֶר הָֽאִשָּׁ֖ה אֶל־הַנָּחָ֑שׁ מִפְּרִ֥י עֵֽץ־הַגָּ֖ן נֹאכֵֽל׃

The woman's answer is accurate in part. It omits the single tree, which the next verse names. Nochel (נֹאכֵל): 'we eat'.

3:3 But from the fruit of the tree at the centre of the garden, God said: you shall not eat from it, nor touch it— lest you die.
וּמִפְּרִ֣י הָעֵץ֮ אֲשֶׁ֣ר בְּתוֹךְ־הַגָּן֒ אָמַ֣ר אֱלֹהִ֗ים לֹ֤א תֹֽאכְלוּ֙ מִמֶּ֔נּוּ וְלֹ֥א תִגְּע֖וּ בּ֑וֹ פֶּן־תְּמֻתֽוּן׃

'Nor touch it' is not in 2:17. The woman adds a prohibition. Pen-tmutun (פֶּן־תְּמֻתוּן): 'lest you become dead'. The LXX has the same addition (mēde hapsēsthe), so the addition predates the Greek.

3:4 The Hiss said to the woman: Not death— you shall not die.
וַיֹּ֥אמֶר הַנָּחָ֖שׁ אֶל־הָֽאִשָּׁ֑ה לֹֽא־מ֖וֹת תְּמֻתֽוּן׃

Lo mot temutun (לֹא־מוֹת תְּמֻתוּן): 'not death, you shall die-not'. The Hiss reverses the infinitive of 2:17. LXX ou thanatō apothaneisthe.

3:5 For God knows— the day you eat from it, your eyes will be split open, you will be like gods, knowing good and harm.
כִּ֚י יֹדֵ֣עַ אֱלֹהִ֔ים כִּ֗י בְּיוֹם֙ אֲכׇלְכֶ֣ם מִמֶּ֔נּוּ וְנִפְקְח֖וּ עֵֽינֵיכֶ֑ם וִהְיִיתֶם֙ כֵּֽאלֹהִ֔ים יֹדְעֵ֖י ט֥וֹב וָרָֽע׃

Yodea (יֹדֵעַ), 'knows': the verb of knowing again. Niftechu (נִפְקְחוּ), 'opened, cleared, split', from the root of eye-opening. Kelohim (כֵּאלֹהִים): 'like gods' or 'like God', since elohim is plural in form. LXX hōs theoi, 'like gods'.

3:6 The woman saw: the tree is good for eating; it is craving for the eyes; the tree is coveted for the making-wise. She took from its fruit and ate; she gave also to her man, with her; and he ate.
וַתֵּ֣רֶא הָֽאִשָּׁ֡ה כִּ֣י טוֹב֩ הָעֵ֨ץ לְמַאֲכָ֜ל וְכִ֧י תַֽאֲוָה־ה֣וּא לָעֵינַ֗יִם וְנֶחְמָ֤ד הָעֵץ֙ לְהַשְׂכִּ֔יל וַתִּקַּ֥ח מִפִּרְי֖וֹ וַתֹּאכַ֑ל וַתִּתֵּ֧ן גַּם־לְאִישָׁ֛הּ עִמָּ֖הּ וַיֹּאכַֽל׃

Ta'avah (תַּאֲוָה): 'craving, desire', a strong word. Lehaskil (לְהַשְׂכִּיל): 'to make wise, to see through'; the root skl is insight. The man is 'with her' (immah): the text does not say where he stood. LXX aresta tois ophthalmois, 'pleasing to the eyes': the Hebrew's craving is softened to pleasure.

3:7 Both their eyes were split open, and they knew they were bare; they sewed fig-leaves together and made themselves belts.
וַתִּפָּקַ֙חְנָה֙ עֵינֵ֣י שְׁנֵיהֶ֔ם וַיֵּ֣דְע֔וּ כִּ֥י עֵֽירֻמִּ֖ם הֵ֑ם וַֽיִּתְפְּרוּ֙ עֲלֵ֣ה תְאֵנָ֔ה וַיַּעֲשׂ֥וּ לָהֶ֖ם חֲגֹרֹֽת׃

Vayyidu (וַיֵּדְעוּ), 'they knew': the knowing of 2:9 now plays out. Tafar (תָּפַר), 'to sew, stitch'. Hagorot (חֲגֹרֹת), 'belts, girdles', not 'aprons'. LXX perizōmata, 'wraps'.

3:8 They heard the voice of YHWH God pacing in the garden at the wind of the day; and the human and his woman hid from YHWH God's face among the trees of the garden.
וַֽיִּשְׁמְע֞וּ אֶת־ק֨וֹל יְהֹוָ֧ה אֱלֹהִ֛ים מִתְהַלֵּ֥ךְ בַּגָּ֖ן לְר֣וּחַ הַיּ֑וֹם וַיִּתְחַבֵּ֨א הָֽאָדָ֜ם וְאִשְׁתּ֗וֹ מִפְּנֵי֙ יְהֹוָ֣ה אֱלֹהִ֔ים בְּת֖וֹךְ עֵ֥ץ הַגָּֽן׃

Mithalekh (מִתְהַלֵּךְ): 'walking back and forth, pacing'. Ruach ha-yom (רוּחַ הַיּוֹם): 'wind of the day'. 'Cool of the day' is a gloss; ruach is also 'spirit'. LXX to deilinon, 'the afternoon'.

3:9 YHWH God called to the human: Ayyekka— where are you?
וַיִּקְרָ֛א יְהֹוָ֥ה אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶל־הָֽאָדָ֑ם וַיֹּ֥אמֶר ל֖וֹ אַיֶּֽכָּה׃

Ayyekka (אַיֶּכָּה): 'where?', a rare form of ayeh. The question is God's, put to one who hides. LXX pou ei.

3:10 He said: I heard your voice in the garden, and I shook— for I am bare, and so I hid.
וַיֹּ֕אמֶר אֶת־קֹלְךָ֥ שָׁמַ֖עְתִּי בַּגָּ֑ן וָאִירָ֛א כִּֽי־עֵירֹ֥ם אָנֹ֖כִי וָאֵחָבֵֽא׃

Ira (אִירָא): 'fear, shudder'. The root carries awe as well as dread.

3:11 He said: Who told you you were bare? From the tree— the one I charged you not to eat from— did you eat?
וַיֹּ֕אמֶר מִ֚י הִגִּ֣יד לְךָ֔ כִּ֥י עֵירֹ֖ם אָ֑תָּה הֲמִן־הָעֵ֗ץ אֲשֶׁ֧ר צִוִּיתִ֛יךָ לְבִלְתִּ֥י אֲכׇל־מִמֶּ֖נּוּ אָכָֽלְתָּ׃

Ha-min (הֲמִן): interrogative 'from'. The question turns on the word 'from'. Tsiviti (צִוִּיתִיךָ): 'I charged you'. LXX tis anēngeilen soi, 'who announced to you'.

3:12 The human said: The woman you set beside me— she gave me from the tree, and I ate.
וַיֹּ֖אמֶר הָֽאָדָ֑ם הָֽאִשָּׁה֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר נָתַ֣תָּה עִמָּדִ֔י הִ֛וא נָֽתְנָה־לִּ֥י מִן־הָעֵ֖ץ וָאֹכֵֽל׃

Immadi (עִמָּדִי), 'with me'. The man puts the blame on the gift: the woman is given, and so the giver is implicated.

3:13 YHWH God said to the woman: What is this you have done? The woman said: The Hiss snared me, and I ate.
וַיֹּ֨אמֶר יְהֹוָ֧ה אֱלֹהִ֛ים לָאִשָּׁ֖ה מַה־זֹּ֣את עָשִׂ֑ית וַתֹּ֙אמֶר֙ הָֽאִשָּׁ֔ה הַנָּחָ֥שׁ הִשִּׁיאַ֖נִי וָאֹכֵֽל׃

Hishi'ani (הִשִּׁיאַנִי): from nasha', 'to deceive, to beguile, to cause to forget'. 'Snared' is an attempt at the verb's trap. LXX ēpatēsen, 'deceived'.

3:14 YHWH God said to the Hiss: Because you did this— cursed you are, out of all the cattle, and out of all the beasts of the field; on your belly you shall go, and dust you shall eat all the days of your life.
וַיֹּ֩אמֶר֩ יְהֹוָ֨ה אֱלֹהִ֥ים ׀ אֶֽל־הַנָּחָשׁ֮ כִּ֣י עָשִׂ֣יתָ זֹּאת֒ אָר֤וּר אַתָּה֙ מִכׇּל־הַבְּהֵמָ֔ה וּמִכֹּ֖ל חַיַּ֣ת הַשָּׂדֶ֑ה עַל־גְּחֹנְךָ֣ תֵלֵ֔ךְ וְעָפָ֥ר תֹּאכַ֖ל כׇּל־יְמֵ֥י חַיֶּֽיךָ׃

Gechon (גְּחוֹן), 'belly, underside'. Afar (עָפָר): 'dust'. LXX gēn, 'earth', for dust, and 'chest and belly' (stēthos kai koilia): the Greek adds the chest.

3:15 I will set enmity between you and the woman, between your seed and her seed: he—it—will crush your head; you will bruise its heel.
וְאֵיבָ֣ה ׀ אָשִׁ֗ית בֵּֽינְךָ֙ וּבֵ֣ין הָֽאִשָּׁ֔ה וּבֵ֥ין זַרְעֲךָ֖ וּבֵ֣ין זַרְעָ֑הּ ה֚וּא יְשׁוּפְךָ֣ רֹ֔אשׁ וְאַתָּ֖ה תְּשׁוּפֶ֥נּוּ עָקֵֽב׃ {ס}

Eivah (אֵיבָה), 'enmity, hostility'. Hu (הוּא): 'he, it', masculine. Zera' (זֶרַע), 'seed', is collective, and the pronoun is singular. Yeshufka (יְשׁוּפְךָ): from shuf, 'crush, bruise, lie in wait'; the verb recurs in the second clause. LXX autos tērēsei, 'he will watch': tēreō stands for both verbs, so the Greek changes 'crush' to 'guard'. The verse has been read as a messianic prophecy; the Hebrew names no single victor.

3:16 To the woman he said: I will multiply, multiply your pain and your groaning. In pain you shall bear children. Toward your man your craving, and he shall rule over you.
אֶֽל־הָאִשָּׁ֣ה אָמַ֗ר הַרְבָּ֤ה אַרְבֶּה֙ עִצְּבוֹנֵ֣ךְ וְהֵֽרֹנֵ֔ךְ בְּעֶ֖צֶב תֵּֽלְדִ֣י בָנִ֑ים וְאֶל־אִישֵׁךְ֙ תְּשׁ֣וּקָתֵ֔ךְ וְה֖וּא יִמְשׇׁל־בָּֽךְ׃ {ס}

Itstsavon (עִצָּבוֹן), 'pain, toil', the root of 3:17's 'in pain'. Heronek (הֵרֹנֵךְ): 'your groaning, your conception'. Teshuqah (תְּשׁוּקָה): 'craving, drive'. The same word is used of sin's craving in 4:7. Yimshol (יִמְשֹׁל): 'rule'. LXX apostrophē, 'turning back', for teshuqah: 'your turning back toward your man'.

3:17 To the human he said: Because you listened to your woman's voice and ate from the tree I charged you about— 'you shall not eat from it'— cursed is the red because of you. In pain you shall eat from it all the days of your life.
וּלְאָדָ֣ם אָמַ֗ר כִּֽי־שָׁמַ֘עְתָּ֮ לְק֣וֹל אִשְׁתֶּ֒ךָ֒ וַתֹּ֙אכַל֙ מִן־הָעֵ֔ץ אֲשֶׁ֤ר צִוִּיתִ֙יךָ֙ לֵאמֹ֔ר לֹ֥א תֹאכַ֖ל מִמֶּ֑נּוּ אֲרוּרָ֤ה הָֽאֲדָמָה֙ בַּֽעֲבוּרֶ֔ךָ בְּעִצָּבוֹן֙ תֹּֽאכְלֶ֔נָּה כֹּ֖ל יְמֵ֥י חַיֶּֽיךָ׃

Ha-adamah (הָאֲדָמָה), 'the red ground'. Be-itstsavon (בְּעִצָּבוֹן): 'in pain'; the noun of 3:16 again. LXX adds 'in the works' (en tois ergois): the Greek makes the toil explicit.

3:18 Thorn and thistle it will bring up for you; and you shall eat the grass of the field.
וְק֥וֹץ וְדַרְדַּ֖ר תַּצְמִ֣יחַֽ לָ֑ךְ וְאָכַלְתָּ֖ אֶת־עֵ֥שֶׂב הַשָּׂדֶֽה׃

Qos (קוֹץ): 'thorn'. Dardar (דַּרְדַּר): 'thistle', a rough word in its sound. LXX akanthas kai tribolous, 'thorns and caltrops'. The human's food is now the animal's: grass of the field.

3:19 By the sweat of your face you shall eat bread, until you return to the red— for from it you were taken. Dust you are, and to dust you shall return.
בְּזֵעַ֤ת אַפֶּ֙יךָ֙ תֹּ֣אכַל לֶ֔חֶם עַ֤ד שֽׁוּבְךָ֙ אֶל־הָ֣אֲדָמָ֔ה כִּ֥י מִמֶּ֖נָּה לֻקָּ֑חְתָּ כִּֽי־עָפָ֣ר אַ֔תָּה וְאֶל־עָפָ֖ר תָּשֽׁוּב׃

Appayim (אַפַּיִם): 'face, nostrils, anger', the word of 2:7 again, here 'the sweat of your face'. Lechem (לֶחֶם), 'bread'. Lukkachta (לֻקָּחְתָּ): 'you were taken'. LXX en hidrōti, 'in sweat'.

3:20 The human named his woman Chavah— Living— for she became the mother of all the living.
וַיִּקְרָ֧א הָֽאָדָ֛ם שֵׁ֥ם אִשְׁתּ֖וֹ חַוָּ֑ה כִּ֛י הִ֥וא הָֽיְתָ֖ה אֵ֥ם כׇּל־חָֽי׃

Chavah (חַוָּה), 'Eve', from chayah, 'to live'. The name is a verb in disguise. LXX Zōē, 'life'.

3:21 YHWH God made the human and his woman tunics of skin, and dressed them.
וַיַּ֩עַשׂ֩ יְהֹוָ֨ה אֱלֹהִ֜ים לְאָדָ֧ם וּלְאִשְׁתּ֛וֹ כׇּתְנ֥וֹת ע֖וֹר וַיַּלְבִּשֵֽׁם׃ {פ}

Ketonot or (כָּתְנוֹת עוֹר): 'skin-tunics'. Or is hide or pelt. LXX chitōnas dermatinous, 'leather tunics'.

3:22 YHWH God said: Look—the human has become like one of us, knowing good and harm. And now—lest he stretch his hand and take also from the tree of lives, and eat, and live—forever.
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר ׀ יְהֹוָ֣ה אֱלֹהִ֗ים הֵ֤ן הָֽאָדָם֙ הָיָה֙ כְּאַחַ֣ד מִמֶּ֔נּוּ לָדַ֖עַת ט֣וֹב וָרָ֑ע וְעַתָּ֣ה ׀ פֶּן־יִשְׁלַ֣ח יָד֗וֹ וְלָקַח֙ גַּ֚ם מֵעֵ֣ץ הַֽחַיִּ֔ים וְאָכַ֖ל וָחַ֥י לְעֹלָֽם׃

Ke-echad mi-mennu (כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ): 'like one of us' or 'like one of it'. The 'us' is a plural. Pen yishlach yado (פֶּן־יִשְׁלַח יָדוֹ): 'lest he send out his hand'. 'Forever' (le-olam) names the boundary death keeps. LXX hōs heis ex hēmōn, 'as one of us', agrees.

3:23 YHWH God sent him out from the garden of Eden, to serve the red, from which he was taken.
וַֽיְשַׁלְּחֵ֛הוּ יְהֹוָ֥ה אֱלֹהִ֖ים מִגַּן־עֵ֑דֶן לַֽעֲבֹד֙ אֶת־הָ֣אֲדָמָ֔ה אֲשֶׁ֥ר לֻקַּ֖ח מִשָּֽׁם׃

Vayshalhehu (וַיְשַׁלְּחֵהוּ): 'sent him away'. La-avod (לַעֲבֹד): 'to serve, work': the human's service is now to the ground. LXX ergazesthai, 'work'.

3:24 He drove the human out. He set, at the east of the garden of Eden, the cherubim, and the flaming turning blade, to guard the way to the tree of lives.
וַיְגָ֖רֶשׁ אֶת־הָֽאָדָ֑ם וַיַּשְׁכֵּן֩ מִקֶּ֨דֶם לְגַן־עֵ֜דֶן אֶת־הַכְּרֻבִ֗ים וְאֵ֨ת לַ֤הַט הַחֶ֙רֶב֙ הַמִּתְהַפֶּ֔כֶת לִשְׁמֹ֕ר אֶת־דֶּ֖רֶךְ עֵ֥ץ הַֽחַיִּֽים׃ {ס}

Vayegaresh (וַיְגָרֶשׁ): 'drove out'. Garash is the verb of divorce (Lev 21:7; 22:13): the severance is a divorce. Kerubim (כְּרֻבִים): winged guardians. Lahat ha-chereb (לַהַט הַחֶרֶב): 'flame of the sword'; hammitpakhet (הַמִּתְהַפֶּכֶת): 'turning, whirling, reversing itself'. LXX strephomenēn, 'turning'.

Chapter 4

TranslationCommentary
4:1 Now the human knew Chavah his woman; she conceived and bore Qayin, and said: I have gotten a man— YHWH.
וְהָ֣אָדָ֔ם יָדַ֖ע אֶת־חַוָּ֣ה אִשְׁתּ֑וֹ וַתַּ֙הַר֙ וַתֵּ֣לֶד אֶת־קַ֔יִן וַתֹּ֕אמֶר קָנִ֥יתִי אִ֖ישׁ אֶת־יְהֹוָֽה׃

Yada' (יָדַע): 'knew', the verb of knowing and of intimacy. Qanah (קָנִיתִי): 'I have gotten, acquired'; the root also means 'to create' (Gen 14:19). Qayin (קַיִן): 'spear, smith', a folk etymology. The 'et' of 'et YHWH' is ambiguous: 'with YHWH' (by help) or 'YHWH' in apposition. The line reads 'a man, YHWH' or 'a man with YHWH'.

4:2 She bore again— his brother, Hevel. Hevel: a shepherd of flocks. Qayin: a worker of the red.
וַתֹּ֣סֶף לָלֶ֔דֶת אֶת־אָחִ֖יו אֶת־הָ֑בֶל וַֽיְהִי־הֶ֙בֶל֙ רֹ֣עֵה צֹ֔אן וְקַ֕יִן הָיָ֖ה עֹבֵ֥ד אֲדָמָֽה׃

Hevel (הֶבֶל): 'breath, vapour, vanity', the word of Ecclesiastes. Ro'eh tzon (רֹעֵה צֹאן): 'shepherd of flocks'. Oved adamah (עֹבֵד אֲדָמָה): 'worker of the ground', from the same verb as 'serve' (2:15). The brothers are named for breath and for red. LXX Abel: a transliteration with no sense carried into Greek.

4:3 At the end of days— miqqetz yamim— Qayin brought from the fruit of the red an offering to YHWH.
וַֽיְהִ֖י מִקֵּ֣ץ יָמִ֑ים וַיָּבֵ֨א קַ֜יִן מִפְּרִ֧י הָֽאֲדָמָ֛ה מִנְחָ֖ה לַֽיהֹוָֽה׃

Miqqetz (מִקֵּץ): 'from the end, at the cut-off'. Minchah (מִנְחָה): 'offering, gift'. LXX meth' hēmeras, 'after days': the Greek drops 'end'.

4:4 And Hevel too: he brought from the firstborn of his flock, from their fat— and YHWH fixed his eye on Hevel and his gift.
וְהֶ֨בֶל הֵבִ֥יא גַם־ה֛וּא מִבְּכֹר֥וֹת צֹאנ֖וֹ וּמֵֽחֶלְבֵהֶ֑ן וַיִּ֣שַׁע יְהֹוָ֔ה אֶל־הֶ֖בֶל וְאֶל־מִנְחָתֽוֹ׃

Bekhorot (בְּכֹרוֹת), 'firstborn'. Chelbehen (חֶלְבֵהֶן): 'their fat', the richest and best portion. Vayisha' (וַיִּשַׁע): 'looked, regarded', from a root carrying gaze and sigh. LXX epeiden, 'looked upon', close.

4:5 But to Qayin and his gift he did not look. Qayin burned— exceedingly burned— and his face fell.
וְאֶל־קַ֥יִן וְאֶל־מִנְחָת֖וֹ לֹ֣א שָׁעָ֑ה וַיִּ֤חַר לְקַ֙יִן֙ מְאֹ֔ד וַֽיִּפְּל֖וּ פָּנָֽיו׃

Vayyichar (וַיִּחַר): 'burned, grew hot', of anger. Vayyippelu panav (וַיִּפְּלוּ פָּנָיו): 'his face fell', naphal, 'fall'. The face falls like a body. LXX sun-epesen tō prosōpō, 'fell together upon his face'.

4:6 YHWH said to Qayin: Why do you burn? Why has your face fallen?
וַיֹּ֥אמֶר יְהֹוָ֖ה אֶל־קָ֑יִן לָ֚מָּה חָ֣רָה לָ֔ךְ וְלָ֖מָּה נָפְל֥וּ פָנֶֽיךָ׃

Lamah (לָמָּה), 'why'. This is the first divine speech to a human in the Cain story; it is a question, not yet a judgment.

4:7 Is it not so?— if you do well, there is a lifting of the face. But if you do not do well, at the door a sin crouches— its craving is for you; but you: rule it.
הֲל֤וֹא אִם־תֵּיטִיב֙ שְׂאֵ֔ת וְאִם֙ לֹ֣א תֵיטִ֔יב לַפֶּ֖תַח חַטָּ֣את רֹבֵ֑ץ וְאֵלֶ֙יךָ֙ תְּשׁ֣וּקָת֔וֹ וְאַתָּ֖ה תִּמְשׇׁל־בּֽוֹ׃

Se'et panim (שְׂאֵת פָּנִים): 'lifting of the face', also 'acceptance, forgiveness'. Lapetach (לַפֶּתַח), 'at the door, at the opening'. Chatta't (חַטָּאת): 'sin, sin-offering', the cultic word. Rovets (רֹבֵץ), 'crouching', as a beast lies in wait. Teshuqah: the craving of 3:16, now the sin's. Timshol (תִּמְשׁל): 'you shall rule', the verb that in 3:16 rules the woman. LXX reads hēsychason, 'be still', and adds 'divide rightly' (orthōs dielēs), a cultic gloss the Hebrew does not give.

4:8 Qayin said to Hevel his brother— and it was, in the field, that Qayin rose against Hevel his brother and struck him dead.
וַיֹּ֥אמֶר קַ֖יִן אֶל־הֶ֣בֶל אָחִ֑יו וַֽיְהִי֙ בִּהְיוֹתָ֣ם בַּשָּׂדֶ֔ה וַיָּ֥קׇם קַ֛יִן אֶל־הֶ֥בֶל אָחִ֖יו וַיַּהַרְגֵֽהוּ׃

The Hebrew records Qayin's words with no content: the dash is in the text. The LXX fills the gap: 'let us go out into the field'. Vayyaqom (וַיָּקָם): 'rose up'. Vayahargehu (וַיַּהַרְגֵהוּ): 'killed him', harag, a violent slaying. The gap is part of the text.

4:9 YHWH said to Qayin: Where is Hevel, your brother? He said: I do not know. Am I a keeper of my brother?
וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהֹוָה֙ אֶל־קַ֔יִן אֵ֖י הֶ֣בֶל אָחִ֑יךָ וַיֹּ֙אמֶר֙ לֹ֣א יָדַ֔עְתִּי הֲשֹׁמֵ֥ר אָחִ֖י אָנֹֽכִי׃

Shomer (שֹׁמֵר): 'keeper, guard', from shamar. The same verb is in 2:15, where the human is set to keep the garden. Qayin asks whether he keeps his brother. LXX phylax, 'guard'.

4:10 He said: What have you done? The voice of your brother's bloods cries out to me from the red.
וַיֹּ֖אמֶר מֶ֣ה עָשִׂ֑יתָ ק֚וֹל דְּמֵ֣י אָחִ֔יךָ צֹעֲקִ֥ים אֵלַ֖י מִן־הָֽאֲדָמָֽה׃

Demei (דְּמֵי): 'bloods', the plural of blood, used for violent death. Tso'aqim (צֹעֲקִים): 'crying out'. LXX phōnē haimatos, 'voice of blood', in the singular.

4:11 Now: cursed are you from the red— which opened its mouth to take your brother's bloods from your hand.
וְעַתָּ֖ה אָר֣וּר אָ֑תָּה מִן־הָֽאֲדָמָה֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר פָּצְתָ֣ה אֶת־פִּ֔יהָ לָקַ֛חַת אֶת־דְּמֵ֥י אָחִ֖יךָ מִיָּדֶֽךָ׃

Patzta (פָּצְתָה): 'opened, gaped'. The earth is given a mouth (peh). LXX echanen, 'gaped', from chainō: the earth opens.

4:12 When you work the red, it will not add its strength to you. A staggerer, a drifter, you will be on the earth.
כִּ֤י תַֽעֲבֹד֙ אֶת־הָ֣אֲדָמָ֔ה לֹֽא־תֹסֵ֥ף תֵּת־כֹּחָ֖הּ לָ֑ךְ נָ֥ע וָנָ֖ד תִּֽהְיֶ֥ה בָאָֽרֶץ׃

Na' va-nad (נָע וָנָד): 'staggering and wandering', two sounds of unsteady motion. Ko'ach (כֹּחַ): 'strength, power'. LXX stenōn kai tremōn, 'groaning and trembling': a different image.

4:13 Qayin said to YHWH: My guilt—too great to carry, too great to be lifted.
וַיֹּ֥אמֶר קַ֖יִן אֶל־יְהֹוָ֑ה גָּד֥וֹל עֲוֺנִ֖י מִנְּשֹֽׂא׃

Avon (עָוֹן): 'guilt, iniquity, punishment': the word runs from fault to penalty. Minnesso (מִנְּשֹׂא): 'from lifting'; nasa' both carries and forgives. The line reads 'too big to bear' and 'too big to be forgiven'. LXX aitia, 'charge, cause': the Greek turns guilt into a case.

4:14 Look—you drive me out today from the face of the red. From your face I shall be hidden; I shall be a staggerer and drifter in the earth, and whoever finds me will kill me.
הֵן֩ גֵּרַ֨שְׁתָּ אֹתִ֜י הַיּ֗וֹם מֵעַל֙ פְּנֵ֣י הָֽאֲדָמָ֔ה וּמִפָּנֶ֖יךָ אֶסָּתֵ֑ר וְהָיִ֜יתִי נָ֤ע וָנָד֙ בָּאָ֔רֶץ וְהָיָ֥ה כׇל־מֹצְאִ֖י יַֽהַרְגֵֽנִי׃

Garashta (גֵּרַשְׁתָּ): 'you drove out', garash again (see 3:24). Mopnei (מִפָּנֶיךָ): 'from your face'. Yahargeni (יַהַרְגֵנִי): 'will kill me', harag again.

4:15 YHWH said to him: Not-so—therefore— whoever kills Qayin seven-fold shall be avenged. And YHWH set a mark on Qayin, lest anyone who found him strike him.
וַיֹּ֧אמֶר ל֣וֹ יְהֹוָ֗ה לָכֵן֙ כׇּל־הֹרֵ֣ג קַ֔יִן שִׁבְעָתַ֖יִם יֻקָּ֑ם וַיָּ֨שֶׂם יְהֹוָ֤ה לְקַ֙יִן֙ א֔וֹת לְבִלְתִּ֥י הַכּוֹת־אֹת֖וֹ כׇּל־מֹצְאֽוֹ׃

Lakhen (לָכֵן): 'therefore' or 'not so' (lo ken). The Hebrew allows either reading; the LXX takes 'not so' (ouch houtōs). Shiv'atayim (שִׁבְעָתַיִם): 'seven-fold'. Ot (אוֹת): 'sign, mark'; its form is not described. LXX ekdikoumena, 'avenged'.

4:16 Qayin went out from YHWH's face and settled in the land of Nod— Wandering— east of Eden.
וַיֵּ֥צֵא קַ֖יִן מִלִּפְנֵ֣י יְהֹוָ֑ה וַיֵּ֥שֶׁב בְּאֶֽרֶץ־נ֖וֹד קִדְמַת־עֵֽדֶן׃

Nod (נוֹד): 'wandering', the root of na' and nad (4:12, 4:14). He settles in Wander-land. LXX Naid: a transliteration that hides the pun.

4:17 Qayin knew his woman; she conceived and bore Chanokh. He was building a city, and named the city after his son: Chanokh.
וַיֵּ֤דַע קַ֙יִן֙ אֶת־אִשְׁתּ֔וֹ וַתַּ֖הַר וַתֵּ֣לֶד אֶת־חֲנ֑וֹךְ וַֽיְהִי֙ בֹּ֣נֶה עִ֔יר וַיִּקְרָא֙ שֵׁ֣ם הָעִ֔יר כְּשֵׁ֖ם בְּנ֥וֹ חֲנֽוֹךְ׃

Bonneh ir (בֹּנֶה עִיר): 'building a city'. This is the first city in the text, named for a child. Chanokh (חֲנוֹךְ), 'dedicated, initiated', from chanakh, 'to train, to dedicate'. LXX matches the Hebrew.

4:18 And to Chanokh was born Irad; and Irad fathered Mechuyael; and Mechuyael fathered Methushael; and Methushael fathered Lamech.
וַיִּוָּלֵ֤ד לַֽחֲנוֹךְ֙ אֶת־עִירָ֔ד וְעִירָ֕ד יָלַ֖ד אֶת־מְחֽוּיָאֵ֑ל וּמְחִיָּיאֵ֗ל יָלַד֙ אֶת־מְת֣וּשָׁאֵ֔ל וּמְתוּשָׁאֵ֖ל יָלַ֥ד אֶת־לָֽמֶךְ׃

Yillad (יִוָּלֵד): 'was born', passive. Each name fathers the next. Irad (עִירָד): unclear, perhaps a wild ass or a place. Mechuyael (מְחוּיָאֵל): 'smitten by El' or 'given life by El'; the root is contested. Methushael (מְתוּשָׁאֵל): 'man of El' or 'death of El' (mot); disputed. LXX Gaidad for Irad, and the doubled 'Gaidad and Gaidad' renders the Hebrew's repeated 'and Irad fathered'; Maiel for Mechuyael; Mathousala for Methushael, the spelling of Methuselah in 5:21. The chain ends on Lamech, who takes two wives in 4:19.
