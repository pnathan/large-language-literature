Bereshit

Genesis 1:1 – 4:18 · Hebrew → English

An original translation, made from the Hebrew text with the Septuagint beside it, of the first verses of the book. The English is deliberately strange and primal: the aim is a text the reader does not recognise, not one they half remember. The middle column gives the Greek. The right-hand column carries the commentary: root sense, the readings the Septuagint takes instead, and every place the register bends the Hebrew.

No published English translation was consulted. Rashi and Ibn Ezra are the normative traditional interpreters: where both read the Hebrew against the English, the English follows them; where they differ, both readings are recorded.

Conventions. אֱלֹהִים: Divinity. יְהוָה: the Lord. אֲדָמָה: the red earth. אֶרֶץ: the ground (land only for place names). נָחָשׁ: the serpent. אִשָּׁה and אִישׁ are kept as Ishah and Ish for the pun. Names are given in an English form in the translation. The commentary writes Hebrew in Hebrew script and Greek in Greek script. Under each verse: a note, an apparatus of the readings held open, the readings of Rashi and Ibn Ezra, and a word-by-word table.

1:1 At the start of Divinity's cutting the heavens and the ground—
Septuagintἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆν
בְּרֵאשִׁ֖ית בָּרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֑ים אֵ֥ת הַשָּׁמַ֖יִם וְאֵ֥ת הָאָֽרֶץ׃

בְּרֵאשִׁית is a construct form, 'at the start of', with no article: it needs a following word. Here it stands before the verb בָּרָא, so the clause reads 'at the start of (Divinity's) cutting'. The verse is then a time clause, and the main clause comes in 1:2 or in 1:3. This is the reading of Rashi and of Ibn Ezra, and the English follows it. בָּרָא is used only with Divinity as subject; the English gives 'cut', the sense Ibn Ezra gives: to cut, and set a cut boundary. אֵת marks the object and is not rendered. אֶרֶץ is 'the ground' in this translation; אֲדָמָה is 'the red earth'. The LXX reads a main clause: ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεός, 'in a beginning Divinity made', with no article on ἀρχῇ and ἐποίησεν, 'made', for בָּרָא.

Apparatus
  • בְּרֵאשִׁית: 'at the start of', a time clause (Rashi, Ibn Ezra); or 'at the start', a main clause (the LXX).
  • בָּרָא: cut, shape, or create. The root is disputed.
Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: The verse does not teach the order of creation. Every רֵאשִׁית in Scripture is bound to the word after it, so read בְּרֵאשִׁית בְּרֹא: 'at the start of the creating of heavens and earth, when the earth was waste and void and dark, Divinity said: let there be light'. The main clause is 1:3.
  • Ibn Ezra: בְּרֵאשִׁית is a construct (ולפי דעתי שהוא סמוך), like 'at the start of the reign of Yehoyakim'; the main clause is 1:2. בָּרָא is not making out of nothing: its sense is 'to cut, and set a cut boundary' (לגזור ולשום גבול נגזר). 'The heavens and the ground' here are the firmament and the dry land. אֱלֹהִים is a plural of honour, and so the verb is singular.

1:2 the ground was waste and void, and dark was on the face of the deep, and the wind of Divinity was fluttering over the face of the waters—
Septuagintἡ δὲ γῆ ἦν ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος καὶ σκότος ἐπάνω τῆς ἀβύσσου καὶ πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἐπεφέρετο ἐπάνω τοῦ ὕδατος
וְהָאָ֗רֶץ הָיְתָ֥ה תֹ֙הוּ֙ וָבֹ֔הוּ וְחֹ֖שֶׁךְ עַל־פְּנֵ֣י תְה֑וֹם וְר֣וּחַ אֱלֹהִ֔ים מְרַחֶ֖פֶת עַל־פְּנֵ֥י הַמָּֽיִם׃

תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ, 'waste and void', a rhyming pair. תְּהוֹם, 'the deep', has no article: it is used as a name. מְרַחֶפֶת, 'fluttering, hovering', is the verb of an eagle over its young in Deut 32:11. רוּחַ is wind, breath, or spirit; the English gives 'wind', with Ibn Ezra. The verse is three circumstantial clauses: if 1:3 is the main clause (Rashi), all of 1:2 is background; if 1:2 is the main clause (Ibn Ezra), it begins here. The dashes allow both. The LXX has ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος, 'unseen and unformed', for תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ, ἄβυσσος for the deep, and πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἐπεφέρετο, 'a breath of Divinity was borne over'.

Apparatus
  • וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים: wind of Divinity (Ibn Ezra), breath of Divinity (Rashi), or spirit of Divinity.
  • מְרַחֶפֶת: fluttering, hovering, or blowing.
Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: תֹהוּ is astonishment and desolation: a person is amazed at the emptiness of it; בֹהוּ is emptiness. The deep is the water on the earth. The throne of glory stood in the air and hovered over the waters by the breath of the mouth of the Holy One, as a dove hovers over the nest.
  • Ibn Ezra: תֹהוּ is that which has no substance; בֹהוּ is its brother. The ו of וְהָאָרֶץ marks the main clause (as the Arabic fa'). The wind is the agent of the Name, sent to dry the waters; מְרַחֶפֶת is blowing over the waters, as in 'over its young it hovers'.

1:6 Divinity said: Let there be a hammered plate, in the middle of the waters, and let it split water to water.
Septuagintκαὶ εἶπεν ὁ θεός γενηθήτω στερέωμα ἐν μέσῳ τοῦ ὕδατος καὶ ἔστω διαχωρίζον ἀνὰ μέσον ὕδατος καὶ ὕδατος καὶ ἐγένετο οὕτως
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֔ים יְהִ֥י רָקִ֖יעַ בְּת֣וֹךְ הַמָּ֑יִם וִיהִ֣י מַבְדִּ֔יל בֵּ֥ין מַ֖יִם לָמָֽיִם׃

רָקִיעַ, from רָקַע, 'to beat out, to spread thin' (as metal is beaten into plates, Exod 39:3). בֵּין מַיִם לָמָיִם, 'between water and water': the second member takes לְ, 'to', and the English gives it literally. The LXX has στερέωμα, 'firm thing', and adds καὶ ἐγένετο οὕτως, 'and it was so', here; it lacks the formula at 1:7, where the Hebrew has it.

Apparatus
  • רָקִיעַ: a beaten plate, a firm vault (Rashi), or a spread-out expanse, the air (Ibn Ezra).
Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: 'Let the firmament be strong': the heavens made on the first day were still soft, and they set firm on the second at the rebuke of the Holy One.
  • Ibn Ezra: רָקִיעַ is something spread out, as in 'they beat out' (וירקעו); the firmament is the air.

יִקָּווּ, 'let them be gathered', a passive of קָוָה. The noun מִקְוֶה, 'gathering', in 1:10 is from it. The LXX adds a report that the command was done: καὶ συνήχθη τὸ ὕδωρ … καὶ ὤφθη ἡ ξηρά, 'and the water was gathered … and the dry appeared'.

Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: The waters were spread over the face of the whole earth, and he gathered them into the Ocean, the great sea.
  • Ibn Ezra: יִקָּווּ means 'let them be joined together' (יתחברו). He reads the passage as a pluperfect: Divinity had already said, 'let the waters be gathered'.

1:26 Divinity said: let us make a human, in our image, as our likeness. Let them oppress the fish of the sea, the bird of the heavens, the cattle, the whole ground, every crawler that crawls on the ground.
Septuagintκαὶ εἶπεν ὁ θεός ποιήσωμεν ἄνθρωπον κατ εἰκόνα ἡμετέραν καὶ καθ ὁμοίωσιν καὶ ἀρχέτωσαν τῶν ἰχθύων τῆς θαλάσσης καὶ τῶν πετεινῶν τοῦ οὐρανοῦ καὶ τῶν κτηνῶν καὶ πάσης τῆς γῆς καὶ πάντων τῶν ἑρπετῶν τῶν ἑρπόντων ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς
וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֱלֹהִ֔ים נַֽעֲשֶׂ֥ה אָדָ֛ם בְּצַלְמֵ֖נוּ כִּדְמוּתֵ֑נוּ וְיִרְדּוּ֩ בִדְגַ֨ת הַיָּ֜ם וּבְע֣וֹף הַשָּׁמַ֗יִם וּבַבְּהֵמָה֙ וּבְכׇל־הָאָ֔רֶץ וּבְכׇל־הָרֶ֖מֶשׂ הָֽרֹמֵ֥שׂ עַל־הָאָֽרֶץ׃

נַעֲשֶׂה, 'let us make', first person plural. אָדָם has no article: 'a human', a kind, not yet a name. צֶלֶם, 'image'; דְּמוּת, 'likeness'. וְיִרְדּוּ, from רָדָה, 'rule over, tread down'; the plural takes אָדָם as a collective. The English gives 'oppress', the harsh edge of the verb. The LXX has ποιήσωμεν, 'let us make', κατ' εἰκόνα ἡμετέραν καὶ καθ' ὁμοίωσιν, 'after our image and after likeness', with a καί the Hebrew lacks, and ἀρχέτωσαν, 'let them rule'.

Apparatus
  • נַעֲשֶׂה: Divinity with the heavenly court (Rashi, Ibn Ezra), or a plural of majesty.
  • וְיִרְדּוּ: rule over, or tread down.
Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: Divinity took counsel with his court of angels (נִמְלַךְ בְּפָמַלְיָא שֶׁלּוֹ), to teach humility, though the plural could mislead. וְיִרְדּוּ holds both 'ruling' and 'going down': if he is worthy he rules the beasts; if not, he goes down before them.
  • Ibn Ezra: The Name said to the angels, 'let us make'. He rejects the reading of נַעֲשֶׂה as a passive participle.

The English follows the Hebrew. וַיִּשְׁבֹּת, 'ceased', the verb from which שַׁבָּת is named; the verse does not yet name an institution. מְלַאכְתּוֹ, 'his work', twice. The English gives the ceasing two verbs, 'quit, dropped away'.

Apparatus
  • בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי: on the seventh day (Rashi), or by the seventh day, with the work done on the sixth (Ibn Ezra).
Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: The Holy One knows his times to the hair's breadth: he entered the seventh day exactly, and it seemed as if he finished on it. Another reading: the world lacked rest; the sabbath came, rest came, and the work was finished.
  • Ibn Ezra: He rejects the reading that the day itself was a created work. The ב here means 'before': his work, which he did on the sixth day before the sabbath.

וַיֹּ֧אמֶר ל֣וֹ יְהֹוָ֗ה לָכֵן֙ כׇּל־הֹרֵ֣ג קַ֔יִן שִׁבְעָתַ֖יִם יֻקָּ֑ם וַיָּ֨שֶׂם יְהֹוָ֤ה לְקַ֙יִן֙ א֔וֹת לְבִלְתִּ֥י הַכּוֹת־אֹת֖וֹ כׇּל־מֹצְאֽוֹ׃

לָכֵן, 'therefore'. The threat against the killer is not finished: the sentence breaks after 'whoever kills Qayin'. שִׁבְעָתַיִם יֻקָּם, 'sevenfold it will be avenged'; the verb is passive and names no one. Rashi and Ibn Ezra both take the vengeance to fall on Qayin himself after seven generations, not on his killer; the English is written so as not to name the killer as its object. אוֹת, 'sign'; its form is not told. The LXX reads οὐχ οὕτως, 'not so', for לָכֵן (as if לֹא כֵן), and ἑπτὰ ἐκδικούμενα παραλύσει, 'he will release seven things avenged'.

Apparatus
  • לָכֵן: therefore, or 'not so' (the LXX).
  • שִׁבְעָתַיִם: sevenfold, or after seven generations.
Rashi and Ibn Ezra
  • Rashi: The first half is a rebuke with its penalty left unsaid: whoever kills Qayin, such and such will be done to him. 'Sevenfold it will be avenged': I will not take vengeance on Qayin now; after seven generations Lamekh his descendant will kill him (לְסוֹף שִׁבְעָה דוֹרוֹת). The sign: he engraved a letter of his name on Qayin's forehead.
  • Ibn Ezra: שִׁבְעָתַיִם means 'until seven generations' (עד שבעה דורות), not a multiple. יֻקָּם: his vengeance will be taken; the Name delayed his anger with Qayin for seven generations. The text does not reveal the sign (והכתוב לא גלה האות).

Names, and their roots

אָדָםthe humanfrom אֲדָמָה, 'ground'. The link is the text's own wordplay (2:7), not an established etymology.
חַוָּהChavahfrom חַי, 'living'. The link is the text's own (3:20); the form is unusual.
קַיִןQayinthe text links it to קָנִיתִי, 'I have acquired' (4:1). A proposed sense 'smith, spear' is not settled.
הֶבֶלHevel'breath, vapour': the word of Ecclesiastes for what does not last.
