# The Guest-House — Source Research & Translation Project

## Source Information

**Poem**: "The Guest House" — a modern composite title (Coleman Barks's); Rumi's own conceit-word is مهمان‌خانه (*mehmān-khāneh*, "guest-house"), and the passages carry only editorial rubrics, not titles
**Author**: Jalāl al-Dīn Muḥammad Rūmī (1207–1273 CE)
**Location**: *Masnavi*, Book V — two passages: lines 3644–3646 (§155) and 3676–3707 (§157), separated by the story of §156 (lines 3647–3675)
**Language**: Classical New Persian (13th century), with two Arabic couplets (V.3694–3695). *Note: not "Middle Persian" — Middle Persian (Pahlavi) is the Sasanian-era language, ca. 3rd–9th centuries CE; an earlier draft of this project used the term in error.*

## Manuscript Witnesses & Critical Edition

### Primary Source
**Reynold A. Nicholson's Critical Edition** (1925–1940)
- Standard scholarly edition, Gibb Memorial Series
- Based on the oldest manuscript witnesses (including the Konya manuscript)
- 8-volume set: 3 volumes Persian text, 3 volumes English translation, 2 volumes commentary
- **This is the authoritative source for the Persian text and the line numbering used in this project**

### Online Witnesses Used for Collation
- **Ganjoor** (ganjoor.net): received critical text of Masnavi Book V, §§155–157
- **masnavi.net**: Persian text with Nicholson's English translation, by line number
- Both reproduce the received critical text; final publication should still be collated against Nicholson's printed volumes directly

## The Text's True Shape

The popular English "Guest House" is a composite: §155's three couplets + selected couplets from §157 (including V.3693, lifted from mid-section). Between them sits §156 — the story of the householder's wife who resents the guest when rain sets in ("مهمان در گردن ما ماند," the very idiom of line 3645) — which popular versions silently omit.

Opening couplet, as in the received text:

```
هست مهمان‌خانه این تن ای جوان
هر صباحی ضیف نو آید دوان
```

"This body, O youth, is a guest-house: every morning a new guest comes running."

## Translation Challenge

**The bowdlerization problem**: most popular English versions (especially Coleman Barks) have:
- Removed Islamic terminology and the explicitly religious lines (Job as host of "God's guest," the Arabic prayers of V.3694–3695, "the command and wisdom of the Maker" at V.3702)
- Stripped the passage's structure (the composite conceals the story and reorders couplets)
- Genericized the spiritual instruction into self-help

**Equal and opposite risk**: over-correction — importing technical vocabulary (qadr, fanā, tawḥīd, tajallī) that the passage does not contain. The completed translation claims only what the text says, and marks interpretation as interpretation.

**The task**: a complete, original translation that
1. Presents both passages in full, in true order, with real line numbers
2. Preserves the Persian idioms that worldview gaps distort (rāh zadan "to waylay," mā-warā "the Beyond," the astrological conceit, ghayr "all that is other")
3. Keeps the Islamic theological content the text actually carries
4. Documents choices and ambiguities in notes
