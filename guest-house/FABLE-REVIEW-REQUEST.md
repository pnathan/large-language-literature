## The Specific Passages I'm Most Uncertain About

### Passage 1: Line 2
Persian: "که هم اکنون باز پرد در عدم"
Literal: "for right now it opens and flies back into non-being"

I interpret "عدم" (adum/non-being) as the Sufi concept of fanā (ego-annihilation). But could it simply mean the guest "disappears" or "departs"? Is the mystical reading justified?

### Passage 2: Line 6-7
Persian: "زانک شخص از فکر دارد قدر و جان / فکرِ غم گر راه شادی میزند"
Literal: "The self draws its worth and life from thought / Should a sorrowful thought open the way for joy"

This suggests that:
- The self has no independent reality (it's entirely dependent on what thinks through it)
- Sorrow is not just tolerated—it actively *serves* joy by opening the path

Is this actually what Rumi is saying? Or am I projecting modern psychology onto a medieval text?

### Passage 3: Source of Joy
The poem says joy comes "from its true source" (اصل خیر). I interpret "اصل" as "divine origin."

Is this correct? Or is it just "good source" without theological content?
