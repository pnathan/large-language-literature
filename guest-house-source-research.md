# The Guest House (Tavazon) — Source Research & Translation Project

## Source Information

**Poem**: "The Guest House" (خانه مهمان — *Khāneh-ye Mehmān*)  
**Author**: Jalāl ad-Dīn Muḥammad ar-Rūmī (1207-1273 CE)  
**Location**: Book 5 of the *Masnavi* (Mathnawī), lines 3644-3680 (approximately)  
**Language**: Middle Persian (13th century)

## Manuscript Witnesses & Critical Edition

### Primary Source
**Reynold A. Nicholson's Critical Edition** (1925-1940)
- Standard scholarly edition used worldwide
- Based on oldest manuscript witnesses
- Gibb Memorial Trust publication
- 8-volume set: 3 volumes Persian text, 3 volumes English translation, 2 volumes commentary
- **This is the authoritative source for accurate Persian text**

### Online Resources
- **masnavi.net**: Full Persian text (Mahdî Âzâr Yazdî's edition) + Nicholson's English translation
- Available: Both Persian and English versions of all 6 books

## Persian Text (Reconstructed from sources)

Opening couplet:
```
این تن خانه مهمان است ای جوان
هر صباحی ضیفِ نو آید دوان
```

"This body is a guest house, O youth—
Every morning a new guest comes running."

(Complete text to be derived from Nicholson edition via masnavi.net)

## Translation Challenge

**The bowdlerization problem**: Most popular English versions (especially Coleman Barks) have:
- Removed Islamic terminology and Sufi technical vocabulary
- Stripped out specific references to God/divine reality
- Genericized the spiritual instruction
- Made it sound like generic self-help

**Our task**: Create a fresh translation that:
1. Preserves Sufi theological content
2. Maintains precise word meanings from Middle Persian
3. Captures the rhetorical and metaphorical layers
4. Shows where ambiguity or multiple meanings exist
5. Documents translation choices with Islamic mystical context
