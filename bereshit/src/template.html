Bereshit

Genesis 1:1 – 4:18 · Hebrew → English

An original translation, made from the Hebrew text with the Septuagint beside it, of the first verses of the book. The English is deliberately strange and primal: the aim is a text the reader does not recognise, not one they half remember. The middle column gives the Greek. The right-hand column carries the commentary: root sense, the readings the Septuagint takes instead, and every place the register bends the Hebrew.

No published English translation was consulted. Rashi and Ibn Ezra are the normative traditional interpreters: where both read the Hebrew against the English, the English follows them; where they differ, both readings are recorded. Review: an independent Opus-model pass on the first draft; a Fable-model pass on this revision and on every Rashi and Ibn Ezra entry, run on the final text.

Conventions. אֱלֹהִים: Divinity. יְהוָה: the Lord. אָדָם: the earthling. אֲדָמָה: the red earth. אֶרֶץ: the ground (land only for place names). נָחָשׁ: the serpent. אִשָּׁה and אִישׁ are kept as Ishah and Ish for the pun. Names are given in an English form in the translation. The commentary writes Hebrew in Hebrew script and Greek in Greek script. The page opens with the translation as continuous text, in the paragraphs of the Masoretic text; the verse-by-verse section follows. Under each verse: a note, an apparatus of the readings held open, the readings of Rashi and Ibn Ezra, and a word-by-word table. A critical apparatus at the end collates the Samaritan Pentateuch, the Qumran reports and the ancient versions, and records how the tradition reads the cruxes.

Hebrew witness
Miqra according to the Masorah (MAM), a Ben-Asher text built on the Aleppo and Leningrad codices, from the Sefaria API (fetched 2026-10-14). 98 verses; Hebrew and English verse numbers coincide in this span. MAM's documentation notes on the manuscripts are in section IV of the apparatus.
Traditional interpreters
Rashi (Rosenbaum–Silbermann text) and Ibn Ezra (Piotrkow text), Hebrew, from Sefaria (fetched 2026-10-14). Each entry gives the commentator's plain-sense reading with a short quotation.
Greek witness
Septuagint, Blue Letter Bible interlinear for Genesis 1–4 (fetched 2026-10-14). The edition is not stated on the page and is not confirmed here as Rahlfs.
