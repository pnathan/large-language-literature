# Bereshit, chapter 3. Format and rules: bereshit/CLAUDE.md, section 'Text files'.

@ 3:1
> The serpent was craftier than any beast of the field the Lord God had made.
> It said to the woman:
> Is it so: Divinity said, you shall not eat from any tree of the garden?
note: נָחָשׁ, 'serpent'; the consonants also spell נַחַשׁ, 'divination', and sit near נְחֹשֶׁת,
  'bronze'. עָרוּם, 'crafty', sounds like עֲרוּמִּים, 'bare', of 2:25: the two words come from
  different roots and share their written form. אַף כִּי opens a question that presumes earlier
  talk: 'is it so that'. The serpent says אֱלֹהִים, Divinity, and not the Name. The LXX reads
  τῶν θηρίων τῶν ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς, 'the beasts on the earth', for 'the beasts of the field', and
  τί ὅτι, 'why is it that', for אַף כִּי.
app: עָרוּם: crafty, or shrewd. A sound link with עֲרוּמִּים (2:25), not a shared root.
rashi: אַף כִּי means 'perhaps he said to you' (שֶׁמָּא אָמַר לָכֶם): the serpent starts a talk with a
  leading question. Rashi links the verse to 2:25: the serpent saw them bare and desired the
  woman.
ibn-ezra: עָרוּם is 'wise, acting with cunning' (חכם שיעשה דבריו בערמה). אַף כִּי shows that other
  talk came first (יורה כי דבר דברים אחרים). The serpent does not say the Name because he did not
  know it (לא הזכיר הנחש השם הנכבד … כי לא ידעו).
= 0 1 2 | The serpent was craftier than | and the serpent was crafty; מִן of comparison: 'craftier than'
= 3 4 5 | any beast of the field | 'from every living thing of the field'
= 6 7 8 9 | the Lord God had made. | 'which the Lord God had made'
= 10 11 12 | It said to the woman: | 'and he said to the woman'; the serpent is masculine in Hebrew, 'it' in the English
= 13 14 15 16 | Is it so: Divinity said, | 'even that Divinity said': a leading question
= 17 18 19 20 21 | you shall not eat from any tree of the garden? | 'you (plural) shall not eat from every tree of the garden'

@ 3:5
> For Divinity knows:
> the day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like the divine ones,
> knowing good and harm.
note: יֹדֵעַ, 'knows', a participle. וְנִפְקְחוּ, 'will be opened', from פָּקַח, the verb used only of
  eyes and ears. כֵּאלֹהִים: אֱלֹהִים is plural in form, and the participle that follows, יֹדְעֵי,
  is plural; the English gives 'the divine ones'. The LXX has ὡς θεοί, 'like gods'.
app: כֵּאלֹהִים: like divine beings, like gods, or like Divinity. The plural participle יֹדְעֵי
  favours a plural.
rashi: 'Like Divinity' means 'makers of worlds' (יוֹצְרֵי עוֹלָמוֹת), from Bereshit Rabbah.
ibn-ezra: 'Like angels' (כמלאכים).

@ 3:6

@ 3:7

ibn-ezra: The fig is not the tree of knowledge; if it were, the text would say they sewed leaves of

@ 3:8
> They heard the voice of the Lord God pacing in the garden at the wind of the day;
> the human and his woman hid from the face of the Lord God
> among the trees of the garden.
note: מִתְהַלֵּךְ, 'walking to and fro', a reflexive participle; grammatically it can attach to the
  voice or to the Lord God. לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם, 'at the wind of the day'. The LXX has τὸ δειλινόν,
  'the afternoon', and puts the walking in the genitive, περιπατοῦντος, so that the Lord walks.
app: מִתְהַלֵּךְ: the voice moving, or the Lord God walking.
app: לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם: at the windy time of the day, toward evening; or toward the quarter of the wind.
rashi: The walking is that of the Holy One (קוֹל הַקָּבָּ"ה שֶׁהָיָה מִתְהַלֵּךְ בַּגָּן). 'The wind of
  the day' is the direction to which the sun goes, the west: toward evening.
ibn-ezra: A voice can walk (קולה כנחש ילך). The time is near evening, when the wind of the day
  blows (סמוך לערב בעת התנופף רוח היום).

@ 3:15
> Hatred I will set between you and the woman, between your seed and her seed:
> he will crush your head;
> you will snap at his heel.
note: אֵיבָה, 'enmity'. זֶרַע, 'seed', is collective; הוּא, 'he', agrees with it. The verb שׁוּף occurs
  twice: of the head the English gives 'crush', of the heel 'snap at', following Rashi, who reads
  the two uses differently; Ibn Ezra glosses the first as 'strike'. The LXX has τηρήσει, 'will watch', for both. Later
  Christian tradition reads the verse as a prophecy of one victor; the Hebrew names none.
app: הוּא: he or it, referring to the collective seed.
app: יְשׁוּפְךָ … תְּשׁוּפֶנּוּ: one verb in two senses, crush and bite; or one sense, strike, in both.
rashi: יְשׁוּפְךָ is 'he will crush you' (יְכַתֶּתְךָ). Of the heel, the serpent has no height and will
  bite (תִּשְּׁכֶנּוּ) at the heel; the verb is the hiss of a serpent about to bite (נָשַׁף).
ibn-ezra: Her seed will strike you on the head (זרעה יככה בראש). The heel is the foot: the head is
  first, the heel last.

@ 3:16

  toil'. הֵרֹן, 'pregnancy'. תְּשׁוּקָה, 'longing, desire'; the same word is sin's desire in 4:7.
  The English gives 'thirst' for both. The LXX has στεναγμόν, 'groaning', for 'pregnancy', and

rashi: עִצְּבוֹנֵךְ is the pain of raising children; הֵרֹנֵךְ is the pain of pregnancy (צַעַר הָעִבּוּר);

ibn-ezra: Pregnancy is heavy on the woman (ההריון כבד על האשה). תְּשׁוּקָה means 'your obedience'

@ 3:22
> The Lord God said:
> Look— the human has become like one of us, to know good and harm.
> Now lest he reach out his hand, take also from the tree of lives,

note: הֵן, 'look'. כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ: מִמֶּנּוּ is 'of us' or 'of him'. לְעֹלָם, 'for ever', or 'for a
  long age'. The sentence breaks off after 'live for ever': the consequence is not said. The LXX
  has ὁ θεός alone for 'the Lord God', and ὡς εἷς ἐξ ἡμῶν, 'as one of us'.
app: כְּאַחַד מִמֶּנּוּ: like one of us, or unique, as one from himself.
app: לְעֹלָם: for ever, or for a long time.
rashi: The human is unique among the lower beings as I am unique among the upper (יָחִיד בַּתַּחְתּוֹנִים

@ 3:23

@ 3:24
> He drove the human out.
> At the east of the garden of Eden he settled the cherubim and the flame of the turning blade, to guard the road to the tree of lives.
note: וַיְגָרֶשׁ, 'drove out'. וַיַּשְׁכֵּן, 'caused to dwell': the verb of settling. מִקֶּדֶם, 'east of'.
  כְּרֻבִים, winged guardians. הַמִּתְהַפֶּכֶת, 'turning itself about'. The LXX makes the human the
  object of the settling: κατῴκισεν αὐτὸν ἀπέναντι τοῦ παραδείσου, 'he settled him opposite the
  garden'; it then stations the cherubim with ἔταξεν and has no word for 'east'.
app: לַהַט הַחֶרֶב: the flame that is the blade, or a flame beside a blade.
rashi: 'East of the garden of Eden' is outside the garden. The cherubim are angels of destruction
  (מַלְאֲכֵי חַבָּלָה). לַהַט is the edge of the blade.
ibn-ezra: מִקֶּדֶם means from the east (ממזרח). The cherubim are the known angels. The blade has two
  edges.
