@ 2:1
> Heavens and ground were finished, and all their army.
note: וַיְכֻלּוּ, 'were finished, were completed', a passive. צְבָאָם, 'their host': the word for an army
  mustered for war, here the full array of what fills heavens and ground. The LXX has πᾶς ὁ κόσμος
  αὐτῶν, 'all their order', for the host.
ibn-ezra: וַיְכֻלּוּ means 'were completed' (נשלמו). The sea is included in the ground.

= 3 4 | and all their army. | 'and all their host'

@ 2:2
> Divinity finished on the seventh day his work that he had made;
> and on the seventh day he quit, dropped away from all his work that he had made.
note: Witness variant: the Hebrew has 'on the seventh day' for the finishing; the LXX has ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ
  τῇ ἕκτῃ, 'on the sixth day'. The English follows the Hebrew. וַיִּשְׁבֹּת, 'ceased', the verb from
  which שַׁבָּת is named; the verse does not yet name an institution. מְלַאכְתּוֹ, 'his work', twice.
  The English gives the ceasing two verbs, 'quit, dropped away'.
app: בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי: he finished at the entry of the seventh day (Rashi); or 'finishing' is not an
  act, and the work was done on the sixth (Ibn Ezra).
rashi: The Holy One knows his times to the hair's breadth: he entered the seventh day exactly, and
  it seemed as if he finished on it. Another reading: the world lacked rest; the sabbath came, rest
  came, and the work was finished.
ibn-ezra: He rejects the reading that the day itself was a created work, and he reports and sets
  aside the reading of ב as 'before'. Completing is not a deed (וכלוי מעשה איננה מעשה): 'he finished
  on the seventh' means he did no work on it. The work was done on the sixth day, before the
  sabbath.

= 7 8 9 | and on the seventh day he quit, | 'and he ceased on the seventh day'
= 10 11 12 13 | dropped away from all his work that he had made. | 'from all his work which he had made'; 'dropped away' adds a second verb

@ 2:3
> Divinity blessed the seventh day, and set it apart,
> because on it he quit from all his work that Divinity carved out, to make.
note: וַיְקַדֵּשׁ, 'made holy, set apart'. אֲשֶׁר בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים לַעֲשׂוֹת, 'which Divinity carved out, to make':
  the infinitive after בָּרָא is not explained in the verse, and the English keeps it bare. The LXX
  has ὧν ἤρξατο ὁ θεὸς ποιῆσαι, 'which Divinity began to make'.
app: לַעֲשׂוֹת: work meant for the seventh day and done on the sixth (Rashi); the power given to the
  kinds to make their like (Ibn Ezra); or a mere complement, 'in making'.
rashi: 'Which Divinity carved out, to make': the work fit to be done on the sabbath he doubled and did on
  the sixth day.
ibn-ezra: Blessing is an increase of good; holiness is that no work was done on it as on the other
  days. לַעֲשׂוֹת is the roots in all kinds, given the power to make their likeness; he rejects
  reading it as a mere synonym of בָּרָא.

@ 2:4
> This is the table of heavens and ground, the tale of how they came to be, when they were carved out, in the day the Lord God made
> ground and heavens.
note: תּוֹלְדוֹת, from יָלַד, 'to bear': 'births, generations, what came of'. The English renders it
  twice, 'the table' and 'the tale of how they came to be'. בְּהִבָּרְאָם, 'in their being carved out', the
  passive of בָּרָא. Here the Name, יְהוָה, first appears, with אֱלֹהִים: 'the Lord God'. 'Ground and
  heavens' reverses the order of 1:1. The LXX has αὕτη ἡ βίβλος γενέσεως, 'this is the book of
  origin', the source of the Greek title, ὅτε ἐγένετο, 'when they came to be', for בְּהִבָּרְאָם, and
  ὁ θεός alone, without κύριος.
app: אֵלֶּה: these that follow, or these that were told above (Rashi).
rashi: 'These' are those told above. 'In the day' teaches that all were created on the first day.
ibn-ezra: 'These are the generations': these that they brought forth. בְּהִבָּרְאָם: when the firmament
  was and the ground appeared. עֲשׂוֹת here is fitting-out: carving out, then forming, then making.

@ 2:5
> No bush of the field yet on the ground, no plant of the field, not yet sprouted.
> For the Lord God had not rained on the ground, and no earthling to work the red earth.
note: טֶרֶם, 'not yet'. שִׂיחַ, 'shrub'; עֵשֶׂב, 'plant'. לַעֲבֹד, 'to work, to serve'. The verse
  explains why nothing grew: no rain, and no worker for the אֲדָמָה. The LXX has πρὸ τοῦ γενέσθαι … πρὸ τοῦ
  ἀνατεῖλαι, 'before it came to be … before it sprang up': it reads טֶרֶם as 'before', the sense Rashi
  rules out; and ὁ θεός alone for the Lord God.
rashi: Every טֶרֶם in Scripture means 'not yet', not 'before'. The plants had not come out; they stood
  at the opening of the ground until the sixth day. He had not made it rain because there was no
  earthling to work the red earth and to know the good of rain; when the earthling came and prayed, rain fell.
  'The Lord God': the Lord is his name; Divinity is he who rules and judges all.
ibn-ezra: שִׂיחַ is a tree, in his view a fruit tree.

@ 2:6
> A vapour went up out of the ground and watered the whole face of the red earth.
note: אֵד, 'vapour, mist'; a rare word. Rashi and Ibn Ezra both read it as a rising vapour or cloud,
  and the English follows them. יַעֲלֶה, 'goes up', is an imperfect; after the circumstantial
  clauses of 2:5 it can describe one past event, as both commentators take it. The LXX has πηγή,
  'a spring', and ἀνέβαινεν, 'was going up'.
app: אֵד: vapour or cloud (Rashi, Ibn Ezra); or an underground flow, a spring (the LXX).
rashi: For the creation of the earthling: he raised the deep and watered the clouds, to soak the dust,
  and the earthling was formed, as a baker pours water and then kneads the dough.
ibn-ezra: אֵד is like smoke (כמו עשן). A vapour went up from the ground by the power of the lights,
  watered the red earth, and made it sprout. He records the Gaon's reading 'and no vapour went up' (ולא אד יעלה).

@ 2:7
> The Lord God potted the earthling out of the dust of the red earth,
> and blew into his nostrils the blast of lives;
> and the earthling became a living breath.
note: וַיִּיצֶר, 'formed', as a potter forms clay; the English gives 'potted'. The word is written
  with two י. עָפָר מִן הָאֲדָמָה, 'dust from the red earth': הָאָדָם, the earthling, from הָאֲדָמָה, the
  red earth, is the text's own sound link. נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים, 'breath of lives'; the English gives
  'blast'. נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה, 'living breath', the phrase used of the animals in 1:20–24. The LXX has
  ὁ θεός alone, εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον, 'into the face', for 'into his nostrils', and ψυχὴν ζῶσαν,
  'a living soul'.
app: נֶפֶשׁ: breath, throat, life, or self.
rashi: The two י mark two formings: for this world and for the resurrection. He made the earthling from
  the lower and the upper: body from below, breath from above. Beasts are also called נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה,
  but the earthling's is the most living of all: knowledge and speech were added to it.
ibn-ezra: The nostrils are where life is: they let out the warm air of the heart and bring in other
  air. 'A living breath': he walked at once, like the animals, and not like an infant.

@ 2:21

  hill, of a building. Rashi and Ibn Ezra both read it here as a side, and the English follows them;
  BDB lists these two verses under 'rib of man'.
  וַיִּסְגֹּר, 'closed'. בָּשָׂר, 'flesh, meat'; the English gives 'meat' throughout. תַּחְתֶּנָּה, 'in its
  place'. The LXX has ἔκστασιν, 'trance', τῶν πλευρῶν, 'of the ribs, sides', and ἀνεπλήρωσεν σάρκα,
  'filled up flesh', for 'closed'.
app: מִצַּלְעֹתָיו: from his sides (Rashi, Ibn Ezra), or from his ribs.
rashi: מִצַּלְעֹתָיו means 'from his sides' (מִסְּטָרָיו), as in 'the side of the tabernacle': the
  earthling was created with two faces. 'Closed' is the place of the cut. He slept so as not to see the

@ 2:23
> The earthling said:
> This one, this time, is bone out of my bones,
> meat out of my meat!
> This one shall be called Ishah,
> for from Ish she was taken.
note: זֹאת הַפַּעַם, 'this one, this time': at last. אִשָּׁה, 'woman', and אִישׁ, 'man': the text joins
  them by sound; whether they are one root is disputed (Ibn Ezra takes them as one family). The English keeps the Hebrew words so that the
  pun survives. The LXX has γυνή and ἀνδρὸς αὐτῆς, 'her man', and loses it.
app: אִשָּׁה / אִישׁ: a play on sound (Rashi), or one word family (Ibn Ezra).
rashi: 'This time': he had come upon every beast and was not content with them. אִשָּׁה from אִישׁ is a
  play of word on word, from which the world was created in the holy tongue.
ibn-ezra: 'This time I have found a help as opposite me, like me, because she came from me'. לְזֹאת
  means 'because of this'. The דגש in the שׁ of אִשָּׁה stands for the hidden י of אִישׁ (תמורת
  היו"ד הנח הנעלם שהוא באיש): he takes the two as one family; נָשִׁים is from אֱנוֹשׁ.

= 8 9 10 | This one shall be called Ishah, | 'this one shall be called אִשָּׁה'
= 11 12 13 14 | for from Ish she was taken. | 'for from אִישׁ was taken this one'

@ 2:24
> Therefore: a man leaves father and mother, clings to his woman;
> and they become one meat.
note: עַל כֵּן, 'therefore': the narrator speaks outside the story. יַעֲזָב, 'leaves, forsakes'.
  דָּבַק, 'cling, stick fast'. לְבָשָׂר אֶחָד, 'one flesh'; the English gives 'meat' for בָּשָׂר. The
  LXX adds οἱ δύο, 'the two'.
app: לְבָשָׂר אֶחָד: one body as a pair, or one flesh in the child (Rashi; Ibn Ezra rejects this).
rashi: The holy spirit says this, to forbid forbidden unions to all humankind. One flesh: the child
  is formed by both, and there their flesh becomes one.
ibn-ezra: 'One flesh': as if they were one. He calls the reading that they beget one child
  far-fetched.

@ 2:25

rashi: They did not know the way of modesty, to tell good from evil; they had knowledge to name the

ibn-ezra: עֲרוּמִּים is a plain adjective, 'bare'.
