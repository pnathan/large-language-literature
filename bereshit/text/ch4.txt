# Bereshit, chapter 4. Format and rules: bereshit/CLAUDE.md, section 'Text files'.

@ 4:1
> Now the earthling knew Chavah his woman;
> she conceived and bore Qayin, and said:
> I have acquired a man, with the Lord.
note: יָדַע, 'knew', the verb of knowing, here of intimacy. The subject stands before the verb, a
  word order that can mark a pluperfect, 'had known'. קָנִיתִי, 'I have acquired'; the name קַיִן
  is heard in it. אֶת יְהוָה: the particle אֶת is either 'with' or the mark of an object in
  apposition ('a man, the Lord'); the English takes 'with'. The LXX has διὰ τοῦ θεοῦ, 'through
  Divinity'.
app: קָנִיתִי: I have acquired, or I have made. The name קַיִן is the text's own wordplay.
app: אֶת יְהוָה: with the Lord, or the Lord in apposition to 'a man'.
rashi: 'The earthling had known' already before the events of chapter 3 (כְּבָר קֹדֶם), and so the
  conception and birth came before the expulsion. אֶת יְהוָה is 'with the Lord' (כְּמוֹ עִם ה'):
  in making this child we are partners with him. קַיִן is named for קָנִיתִי.

= 10 11 12 13 | I have acquired a man, with the Lord. | 'I have acquired a man with the Lord'

@ 4:3
> When the days ran out, Qayin brought from the fruit of the red earth a gift to the Lord.
note: מִקֵּץ יָמִים, 'at the end of days': after a span of time. מִנְחָה, 'gift, tribute'; later the word
  of the grain offering. The LXX has μεθ' ἡμέρας, 'after days', and θυσίαν, 'a sacrifice'.
rashi: 'From the fruit of the ground': from the worst of it (מִן הַגָּרוּעַ).
ibn-ezra: Qayin brought the gift to the place he had set for prayer, not to his father. The text
  hints that he did not bring from the first fruits.

@ 4:7
> If you do well, is there not a lifting?
> If you do not do well, at the door a sin crouches— its thirst is toward you; but you will rule it.
note: שְׂאֵת, 'lifting', an infinitive with no object: the face lifted (against the fallen face of 4:5),
  or the sin lifted, that is, forgiven. חַטָּאת, 'sin', is feminine; רֹבֵץ, 'crouching', is
  masculine, as a beast lies in wait. תְּשׁוּקָתוֹ, 'its desire', the word of 3:16. תִּמְשָׁל בּוֹ,
  'you will rule over it', or 'you may rule over it'. The LXX reads a different text: οὐκ ἐὰν ὀρθῶς
  προσενέγκῃς, ὀρθῶς δὲ μὴ διέλῃς, ἥμαρτες; ἡσύχασον, 'if you offer rightly but do not divide
  rightly, have you not sinned? Be still'.
app: שְׂאֵת: lifting of the face, or lifting of the sin (forgiveness).
app: לַפֶּתַח: at the door of the house, at the door of the grave, or at the opening of the mouth.
rashi: For שְׂאֵת he refers to the Aramaic Targum (כְּתַרְגּוּמוֹ פֵּרוּשׁוֹ), whose rendering,
  יִשְׁתְּבֵיק לָךְ, 'it will be forgiven you', is in the apparatus (III, 4:7). 'At the door' is the door of your
  grave, where your sin is kept. The desire is that of the evil inclination (יֵצֶר הָרָע), which
  always longs to make you fall. 'You will rule it': if you wish, you will overcome it.
ibn-ezra: שְׂאֵת is the lifting of the face (שאת פנים), against 'his face fell'. He reports the door
  of the house, of the grave and of the mouth, and holds that the inclination of the human heart
  crouches with him (ויצר לב האדם הוא רובץ עמו). The inclination will return to your obedience, and
  you have the power to rule it.
= 0 1 2 3 | If you do well, is there not a lifting? | 'is it not: if you do well, lifting'
= 4 5 6 7 8 9 | If you do not do well, at the door a sin crouches— | 'and if you do not do well, at the door sin is crouching'
= 10 11 12 13 14 | its thirst is toward you; but you will rule it. | 'and toward you its desire, and you will rule over it'

rashi: He entered into words of quarrel with him, to find a pretext to kill him.
ibn-ezra: Most likely he told Hevel all the rebukes the Name had spoken to him.

@ 4:9

@ 4:10
> He said: What have you done?

  agrees with the bloods, not with קוֹל, 'voice'; קוֹל stands alone as an exclamation, 'listen!'.

rashi: 'Your brother's bloods': his blood and the blood of his descendants.
ibn-ezra: 'Crying' does not attach to 'voice' (איננו דבק עם קול), as in 'the voice of my beloved,

@ 4:13
> Qayin said to the Lord: My guilt is too great to bear.
note: עָוֹן is both the guilt and its punishment. מִנְּשֹׂא, 'than bearing'; נָשָׂא, 'bear, lift',
  also means 'forgive' with עָוֹן as object. Rashi and Ibn Ezra both read 'bear', not 'forgive':
  the English follows them. The LXX takes the forgiveness sense: τοῦ ἀφεθῆναί με, 'than that I
  be forgiven'.
app: עֲוֹנִי: my guilt, or my punishment.
app: The sentence: a statement, or a question in wonder (Rashi).
rashi: A question (בִּתְמִיהָ): you bear the upper and lower worlds, and my guilt you cannot bear?
ibn-ezra: Others read נְשֹׂא as 'forgive'; he reads עָוֹן as the punishment: this punishment is great,
  I cannot bear it (זה העונש גדול לא אוכל לסבלו). The next verse confirms it.

@ 4:15
> The Lord said to him: Therefore, whoever kills Qayin—
> in seven generations vengeance will be taken.
> The Lord set a sign for Qayin, so no one who found him would strike him.
note: לָכֵן, 'therefore'. The threat against the killer is not finished: the sentence breaks after 'whoever
  kills Qayin'. שִׁבְעָתַיִם יֻקָּם, 'sevenfold it will be avenged'; the verb is passive and names
  no one. Rashi and Ibn Ezra both read שִׁבְעָתַיִם as 'at seven generations', not a multiple, and
  the vengeance as falling on Qayin himself, not on his killer; the English follows them. אוֹת, 'sign'; its form is not told. The LXX reads οὐχ οὕτως, 'not so', for לָכֵן (as if
  לֹא כֵן), ἑπτὰ ἐκδικούμενα παραλύσει, 'he will release seven things avenged', and κύριος ὁ θεός,
  'the Lord God', in both places where the Hebrew has the Name alone.
app: לָכֵן: therefore, or 'not so' (the LXX).
app: שִׁבְעָתַיִם: after seven generations (Rashi, Ibn Ezra; Sforno: seven doubled, death in the seventh
  generation), or sevenfold on the killer (Radak, Chizkuni; the LXX).
rashi: The first half is a rebuke with its penalty left unsaid: whoever kills Qayin, such and such
  will be done to him. 'Sevenfold it will be avenged': I will not take vengeance on Qayin now; after
  seven generations Lamekh his descendant will kill him (לְסוֹף שִׁבְעָה דוֹרוֹת). The sign: he
  engraved a letter of his name on Qayin's forehead.
ibn-ezra: שִׁבְעָתַיִם means 'until seven generations' (עד שבעה דורות), not a multiple. יֻקָּם: his
  vengeance will be taken; the Name delayed his anger with Qayin for seven generations. The text
  does not reveal the sign (והכתוב לא גלה האות).

= 7 8 | in seven generations vengeance will be taken. | 'sevenfold it will be avenged'; read as seven generations, with Rashi and Ibn Ezra
= 9 10 11 12 13 14 15 16 17 | The Lord set a sign for Qayin, so no one who found him would strike him. | 'and the Lord set for קַיִן a sign, that none who finds him should strike him'

@ 4:18

> Irad fathered Mechuyael;

rashi: יָלַד, 'fathered', speaks of the man's seed.
ibn-ezra: Lamekh was the sixth from Qayin and the seventh from the earthling.
