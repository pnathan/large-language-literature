# Bereshit, chapter 1. Format and rules: bereshit/CLAUDE.md, section 'Text files'.

@ 1:1
> In the headwaters of time, when Divinity carved out
> the heavens and the ground—
note: בְּרֵאשִׁית is a construct form, 'at the start of', with no article: it needs a following word.
  Here it stands before the verb בָּרָא, so the clause reads 'at the start of (Divinity's) carving out'.
  רֵאשִׁית is formed from רֹאשׁ, 'head'; the English gives 'headwaters', the head of a river, as
  רָאשִׁים is at 2:10. Aquila reads ἐν κεφαλαίῳ, 'in the head'. 'Of time' is the English's addition:
  it names what the headwaters are of, the time sense that the versions read (ἐν ἀρχῇ, בְּקַדְמִין).
  In the Hebrew the construct binds to the verb, not to a noun; 'when' keeps that binding.
  The verse is then a time clause, and the main clause comes in 1:2 or in 1:3. This is the reading
  of Rashi and of Ibn Ezra, and the English follows it. Ramban and Radak read the word absolutely
  (Radak: בַּתְּחִלָּה, 'at first'); Sforno reads 'at the start of time', the first instant, with no time before it. בָּרָא is used only with Divinity as
  subject, and never names a material, unlike יָצַר, 'potted' (2:7). The English gives 'carved
  out', after Ibn Ezra: to cut, and set a cut boundary. His evidence is the intensive form בֵּרֵא:
  the forest cleared to make room in Josh 17:15, and the sword in Ezek 23:47. Whether the simple
  form shares that root is disputed. 'Out' carries the boundary: the form freed, the space marked
  off.
  אֵת marks the object and is not rendered. אֶרֶץ is 'the ground' in this translation; אֲדָמָה is
  'the red earth'. The LXX reads a main clause: ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεός, 'in a beginning Divinity
  made', with no article on ἀρχῇ and ἐποίησεν, 'made', for בָּרָא.
app: בְּרֵאשִׁית: 'at the start of', a time clause (Rashi, Ibn Ezra); or 'at the start', a main clause
  (the LXX, Ramban, Radak).
app: בָּרָא: carve out (Ibn Ezra), or create from nothing (Ramban, Radak). The root is disputed.
rashi: The verse does not teach the order of creation. Every רֵאשִׁית in Scripture is bound to the
  word after it, so read בְּרֵאשִׁית בְּרֹא: 'at the start of the creating of heavens and earth, when
  the earth was waste and void and dark, Divinity said: let there be light'. The main clause is
  1:3.
ibn-ezra: בְּרֵאשִׁית is a construct (ולפי דעתי שהוא סמוך), like 'at the start of the reign of
  Yehoyakim'; the main clause is 1:2. בָּרָא is not making out of nothing: its sense is 'to cut,
  and set a cut boundary' (לגזור ולשום גבול נגזר). 'The heavens and the ground' here are the
  firmament and the dry land. אֱלֹהִים is a plural of honour, and so the verb is singular.
= 0 | In the headwaters | 'at the start of', a construct; רֵאשִׁית from רֹאשׁ, 'head'
= - | of time, | added: what the headwaters are of; the Hebrew has no noun here
= 1 2 | when Divinity carved out | 'carved out Divinity': the verb bound to the construct, given as a clause
= 3 4 | the heavens | אֵת marks the object; 'the heavens'
= 5 6 | and the ground— | 'and the ground'

@ 1:2
> the ground was waste and void,
> and dark was on the face of the deep,
> and the wind of Divinity was fluttering over the face of the waters—
note: תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ, 'waste and void', a rhyming pair. תְּהוֹם, 'the deep', has no article: it is used
  as a name. מְרַחֶפֶת, 'fluttering, hovering', is the verb of an eagle over its young in Deut 32:11.
  רוּחַ is wind, breath, or spirit; the English gives 'wind', with Ibn Ezra. The verse is three
  circumstantial clauses: if 1:3 is the main clause (Rashi), all of 1:2 is background; if 1:2 is
  the main clause (Ibn Ezra), it begins here. The dashes allow both. The LXX has ἀόρατος καὶ
  ἀκατασκεύαστος, 'unseen and unformed', for תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ, ἄβυσσος for the deep, and πνεῦμα θεοῦ
  ἐπεφέρετο, 'a breath of Divinity was borne over'.
app: וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים: wind of Divinity (Ibn Ezra), breath of Divinity (Rashi), or spirit of Divinity.
app: מְרַחֶפֶת: fluttering, hovering, or blowing.
rashi: תֹהוּ is astonishment and desolation: a person is amazed at the emptiness of it; בֹהוּ is
  emptiness. The deep is the water on the earth. The throne of glory stood in the air and hovered
  over the waters by the breath of the mouth of the Holy One, as a dove hovers over the nest.
ibn-ezra: תֹהוּ is that which has no substance; בֹהוּ is its brother. The ו of וְהָאָרֶץ marks the main
  clause (as the Arabic fa'). The wind is the agent of the Name, sent to dry the waters; מְרַחֶפֶת is
  blowing over the waters, as in 'over its young it hovers'.

@ 1:6
> Divinity said:
> Let there be a hammered plate, in the middle of the waters, and let it split
> water to water.
note: רָקִיעַ, from רָקַע, 'to beat out, to spread thin' (as metal is beaten into plates, Exod 39:3).
  בֵּין מַיִם לָמָיִם, 'between water and water': the second member takes לְ, 'to', and the
  English gives it literally. The LXX has στερέωμα, 'firm thing', and adds καὶ ἐγένετο οὕτως, 'and
  it was so', here; it lacks the formula at 1:7, where the Hebrew has it.
app: רָקִיעַ: a beaten plate, a firm vault (Rashi), or a spread-out expanse, the air (Ibn Ezra).
rashi: 'Let the firmament be strong': the heavens made on the first day were still soft, and they
  set firm on the second at the rebuke of the Holy One.
ibn-ezra: רָקִיעַ is something spread out, as in 'they beat out' (וירקעו); the firmament is the air.

@ 1:9
> Divinity said: let the waters under the heavens be gathered into one place,
> and let the dry show itself.
> So.
note: יִקָּווּ, 'let them be gathered', a passive of קָוָה. The noun מִקְוֶה, 'gathering', in 1:10
  is from it. A homonymous קָוָה means 'to hope'; Rashi and Ibn Ezra read only 'gather' here, and
  the English follows them. וְתֵרָאֶה, 'let it be seen'. The LXX adds a report that the command
  was done: καὶ συνήχθη τὸ ὕδωρ … καὶ ὤφθη ἡ ξηρά, 'and the water was gathered … and the dry
  appeared'.
rashi: The waters were spread over the face of the whole earth, and he gathered them into the
  Ocean, the great sea.
ibn-ezra: יִקָּווּ means 'let them be joined together' (יתחברו). He reads the passage as a pluperfect:
  Divinity had already said, 'let the waters be gathered'.

@ 1:14
> Divinity said: let there be lamps in the hammered plate of the heavens, splitting day from night.
> Let them be for marks and meeting-times, and for days and years.
note: מְאֹרֹת, 'light-holders', from אוֹר, 'light'; written without the vowel letter ו. The text names
  neither sun nor moon. מוֹעֲדִים, 'appointed times', from יָעַד, 'to appoint a meeting'. Each
  noun takes לְ, 'for'. The LXX has φωστῆρες, 'lights', and adds εἰς φαῦσιν τῆς γῆς, 'to give
  light to the earth'.
app: מוֹעֲדִים: seasons, or appointed times and festivals.
rashi: The signs are eclipses, a bad sign for the world. מוֹעֲדִים names the festivals still to come
  (עַל שֵׁם הֶעָתִיד). מְאֹרֹת is written short, and is read as מְאֵרָה, 'curse': the fourth day
  was a day of sickness for children.
ibn-ezra: אֹתֹת are moments (רגעים), מוֹעֲדִים are hours (שעות); the signs may also be eclipses of moon
  and sun (ויתכן).

@ 1:26
> Divinity said:
> let us make an earthling, in our image,
> as our likeness.
> Let them hold sway over the fish of the sea, the bird of the heavens,
> the cattle, the whole ground, every crawler that crawls on the ground.
note: נַעֲשֶׂה, 'let us make', first person plural. אָדָם has no article: 'an earthling', a kind, not yet
  a name. The English gives 'earthling' for אָדָם throughout: the Hebrew word carries
  אֲדָמָה, 'ground', and the English word carries 'earth'. צֶלֶם, 'image'; דְּמוּת, 'likeness'. וְיִרְדּוּ, from רָדָה, 'rule over, tread down'; the
  plural takes אָדָם as a collective. The English gives 'hold sway over': the rule, without the softening of 'have charge'. Rashi
  reads both 'rule' and 'go down'. The
  LXX has ποιήσωμεν, 'let us make', κατ' εἰκόνα ἡμετέραν καὶ καθ' ὁμοίωσιν, 'after our image and
  after likeness', with a καί the Hebrew lacks, and ἀρχέτωσαν, 'let them rule'.
app: נַעֲשֶׂה: Divinity with the heavenly court (Rashi, Ibn Ezra), or a plural of majesty.
app: וְיִרְדּוּ: rule over, or tread down.
rashi: Divinity took counsel with his court of angels (נִמְלַךְ בְּפָמַלְיָא שֶׁלּוֹ), to teach humility,
  though the plural could mislead. וְיִרְדּוּ holds both 'ruling' and 'going down': if he is worthy
  he rules the beasts; if not, he goes down before them.
ibn-ezra: The Name said to the angels, 'let us make'. He rejects the reading of נַעֲשֶׂה as a passive
  participle.
