## Sigla
p: The base text is the Masoretic text as served by Sefaria (MT). Readings are cited in the
  script of their witness. The apparatus is positive: the witnesses for the base reading are
  named where they are known.
sig: MT | Masoretic text, Sefaria (src/witness-hebrew.json); the base text.
sig: SP | Samaritan Pentateuch, consonantal text as transcribed on tanakh.info (fetched 2026-10-14); collated against MT by machine, every reading checked by hand.
sig: 4QGenᵇ | Qumran Cave 4, 4Q2, Genesis 1:1–27 and fragments of 2 and 4. Readings reported in secondary sources (Wikipedia variant tables after van de Giessen; the Cave 4 inventory); DJD XII not consulted.
sig: 4QGenᵍ | Qumran Cave 4, 4Q7, Genesis 1:1–11, 13–22; 2:6–7. Reported as for 4QGenᵇ.

## III. Readings of the tradition at the cruxes
p: How the versions and the early interpreters read the words on which the English turns. Rashi
  and Ibn Ezra are the normative interpreters of this edition (Methodology, item 6); the others
  are recorded for their age and for their plain-sense readings.
@ 1:1
= בְּרֵאשִׁית ] ἐν ἀρχῇ {G σ′ θ′ Jos} ; ἐν κεφαλαίῳ, 'in the head' {α′} ; בְּקַדְמִין, 'at first' {TO} ;
  מלקדמין בחכמה, 'from the beginning, with wisdom' {TN} ; מִן אַוְולָא, 'from the first' {TPsJ} ;
  בקמאותא {TSam} ; ܒܪܫܝܬ {Syr} ; 'by the רֵאשִׁית', the Torah as the architect's plan {BerR} ;
  the first of the works of the first day {Jub Chag} ; construct, 'at the start of creating', main clause
  at 1:3 {Rashi} ; construct, main clause at 1:2 {IE Rashbam}
~ Every ancient witness reads the word absolutely, as a time; the construct reading is the
  medieval plain-sense reading. Rashbam rejects both 'first of all' and 'before he created'.
= בָּרָא ] ἐποίησεν, 'made' {G} ; ἔκτισεν, 'founded' {α′ Jos} ; ברא … ושכלל, 'created … and
  perfected' {TN} ; טלמס {TSam} ; 'to cut, and set a cut boundary' {IE}
~ The English 'carve out' follows Ibn Ezra.
@ 1:2
= תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ ] ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος, 'unseen and unfurnished' {G} ; κένωμα καὶ οὐθέν,
  'emptiness and nothing' {α′} ; ἀργὸν καὶ ἀδιάκριτον {σ′} ; צָדְיָא וְרֵיקַנְיָא, 'desolate and empty'
  {TO} ; תהיא ובהיא, desolate of humans and beasts, empty of plants and trees {TN} ;
  תַּהֲיָיא וּבַהֲיָיא, desolate of humans, empty of all beasts {TPsJ} ; ܬܘܗ ܘܒܘܗܿ
  {Syr} ; 'not coming into sight, hidden in deep darkness' {Jos} ; תֹּהוּ a green line round the world,
  בֹהוּ slimy stones in the deep {Chag} ; astonishment and emptiness {Rashi BerR} ; that which has no
  substance {IE} ; without inhabitant {Rashbam}
= וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים ] πνεῦμα θεοῦ {G α′ θ′ σ′} ; πνεύματος … ἐπιθέοντος, 'a breath running over' {Jos} ;
  רוּחָא … מְנַשְּׁבָא, 'a wind blowing' {TO} ; ריח אלהה מנשבה {TSam} ; רוח דרחמין … מנשבא, 'a wind of
  mercy blowing' {TN TPsJ} ; the spirits that serve before him, created on the first day {Jub} ;
  the spirit of the Messiah {BerR} ; the blowing wind {Saadia Rashbam IE} ; the throne of glory
  hovering by the breath of his mouth {Rashi}
~ The English 'wind' stands with the Targums, Josephus and the medieval plain-sense readers.
@ 1:3
= וַיֹּאמֶר ] γενέσθαι φῶς ἐκέλευσεν ὁ θεός, 'God commanded light to come to be', with 1:2 as background

@ 2:6

## Limits
p: The Samaritan text, the Targums Onkelos and Samaritan, the Peshitta, the Septuagint as printed
  there, and the three later Greek translators are taken from one comparison site; their editions
  are not named on it. The Qumran readings are reported, not collated: DJD XII was not consulted.
  Philo, De opificio mundi 26–28, and the Qumran Hebrew of Jubilees (4Q216) were not reachable
  and are not cited. Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion survive only in later quotation.
