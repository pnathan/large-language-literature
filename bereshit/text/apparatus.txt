## Sigla

  script of their witness. The apparatus is positive: the witnesses for the base reading are
  named where they are known.

## III. Readings of the tradition at the cruxes
p: How the versions and the early interpreters read the words on which the English turns. Rashi
  and Ibn Ezra are the normative interpreters of this edition (Methodology, item 6); the others
  are recorded for their age and for their plain-sense readings. Radak, Ramban, Chizkuni and Sforno are
  cited at the cruxes from the full commentaries on 1:1–4:18.
@ 1:1
= בְּרֵאשִׁית ] ἐν ἀρχῇ {G σ′ θ′ Jos} ; ἐν κεφαλαίῳ, 'in the head' {α′} ; in principio {Vg VL} ; בְּקַדְמִין, 'at first' {TO} ;
  מלקדמין בחכמה, 'from the beginning, with wisdom' {TN} ; מִן אַוְולָא, 'from the first' {TPsJ} ;
  בקמאותא {TSam} ; ܒܪܫܝܬ {Syr} ; 'by the רֵאשִׁית', the Torah as the architect's plan {BerR} ;
  heaven and earth counted among the works of the first day {Jub Chag} ; construct, 'at the start of creating', main clause
  at 1:3 {Rashi} ; construct, main clause at 1:2 {IE Rashbam} ; absolute, 'at first' {Radak} ; absolute:
  רֵאשִׁית need not be bound, as מֵרֵאשִׁית אַחֲרִית (Isa 46:10) {Ramban} ; 'before all' {Chizk} ; 'at the start of
  time, the first indivisible instant, with no time before it' {Sforno}
~ The English 'headwaters' takes the 'head' of Aquila's κεφάλαιον; 'of time' takes the time sense of
  G, the Targums and Syr. Every ancient version reads the word absolutely, as a time (TN adds 'with wisdom'); BerR reads it
  as the Torah. The construct reading is that of Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Rashbam; Ramban and Radak read the word
  absolutely, Ramban against Rashi's argument. Rashbam rejects both 'first of all' and 'before he created'.
  Sforno's 'start of time' is the time sense of 'headwaters of time'.
= בָּרָא ] ἐποίησεν, 'made' {G} ; ἔκτισεν, 'founded' {α′ Jos} ; ברא … ושכלל, 'created … and
  perfected' {TN} ; טלמס {TSam} ; creavit {Vg} ; fecit {VL} ; 'to cut, and set a cut boundary' {IE} ; to bring out
  being from absolute nothing, the only Hebrew word for it {Ramban Radak} ; 'made what was not' {Sforno}
~ The English 'carve out' follows Ibn Ezra, against Ramban and Radak.

  רוּחָא … מְנַשְּׁבָא, 'a wind blowing' {TO} ; ריח אלהה מנשבה {TSam} ; רוח דרחמין … מנשבא, 'a wind of

## IV. The Masoretic text
p: The Masoretic manuscripts differ from each other only in letter size, in vowel points, in
  accents and in one spelling. The readings are from MAM's documentation notes
  (src/mam-notes.json, fetched 2026-10-15), with MAM's sigla resolved by its own decoder; they
  were not checked against facsimiles here. None of them changes the sense.
p: Not entered below: L adds a right-hand געיה at 1:15, 1:27, 4:3 (twice) and 4:14; at 2:7 L, L17
  and C18 put a געיה in וַיְהִי, where MT follows P8, PC1 and the Aleppo system; Sapir's record
  confirms the MT accent or vowel at 1:11 (תַּדְשֵׁא), 2:22, 3:19, 4:4 and 4:16; at 2:10 a dot in
  the ר of וְנָהָר in L is a stain in the parchment, not a point.

## Limits
p: The Samaritan text, the Targums Onkelos and Samaritan, the Peshitta, the Septuagint as printed
  there, and the three later Greek translators are taken from one comparison site; their editions
  are not named on it. The Qumran readings are reported, not collated: DJD XII was not consulted.
  Philo, De opificio mundi 26–28, was not reachable and is not cited. The Qumran Hebrew of
  Jubilees 2 (4Q216, cols. V–VII; ed. VanderKam and Milik, DJD XIII, 1–22) could not be fetched:
  the transcription in the Academy of the Hebrew Language's database (Ma'agarim, text 11000) is
  behind a bot check, and DJD XIII is not online. Jub is therefore cited from a retroversion. Section IV
  reports the Masoretic manuscripts through MAM's notes; no facsimile was consulted. Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion survive only in later quotation. Vg is cited from the Clementine text (1592), not
  the critical edition of Weber and Gryson.
