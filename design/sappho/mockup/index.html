Fr. 58 · The Tithonus poem

‹ 55 63 ›

The 'Tithonus poem,' on old age and the myth of Tithonus and Dawn; the older Lobel-Page text (from Athenaeus, lines 11-22) substantially completed by the 2004 Cologne papyrus, a landmark modern discovery.

Π¹P.Köln 21351 + 21376Π²P.Oxy. 1787Ath.Athenaeus 15.687b

Greek · AeolicEnglish

    ὔμμες πεδὰ Μοίσαν ἰ]ο̣κ[ό]λ̣πων κάλα δῶρα, παῖδες,

    You, girls, [busy yourselves] with the lovely gifts of the violet-robed Muses,

Translator’s note

Translated from TEXT A (Digital Sappho / Dickinson College Commentaries reading). Where sources diverge substantively: line 4 follows ἐπέλλαβε "seized" (vs. Wikipedia's κατέσκεθε "held fast/gripped," similar sense); line 10 follows the "seized/smitten by love" (πάσχω-type) reading over the rival "having learned love" (δα-/δαῆναι) reading, since the exact papyrus letters are damaged and disputed. Lines 1-2's opening verb is only a scrap in the papyrus (three incompatible scholarly supplements exist); I render the sense common to the "busy yourselves with/pursue" family of restorations rather than committing to one editor's exact Greek supplement. Lines 13-14 are too fragmentary (isolated verb-forms only) to translate beyond their bare gapped sense. The final couplet (here lines 15-16) is preserved whole only via Athenaeus's separate quotation, used to patch the otherwise-damaged papyrus (P.Oxy. 1787); its last word/clause is elliptical in Greek ("has obtained/fallen to my lot"), so I supply "for me" implicitly rather than inventing an explicit object beyond what the source gives.
