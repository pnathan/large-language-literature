Commentarynote · 2 traditional

נָחָשׁ, 'serpent'; the consonants also spell נַחַשׁ, 'divination', and sit near נְחֹשֶׁת, 'bronze'. עָרוּם, 'crafty', sounds like עֲרוּמִּים, 'bare', of 2:25: the two words come from different roots and share their written form. אַף כִּי opens a question that presumes earlier talk: 'is it so that'. The serpent says אֱלֹהִים, Divinity, and not the Name. The LXX reads τῶν θηρίων τῶν ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς, 'the beasts on the earth', for 'the beasts of the field', and τί ὅτι, 'why is it that', for אַף כִּי.

Readings held open

  • עָרוּם: crafty, or shrewd. A sound link with עֲרוּמִּים (2:25), not a shared root.

Rashi רש״י

אַף כִּי means 'perhaps he said to you' (שֶׁמָּא אָמַר לָכֶם): the serpent starts a talk with a leading question. Rashi links the verse to 2:25: the serpent saw them bare and desired the woman.

Ibn Ezra אבן עזרא

עָרוּם is 'wise, acting with cunning' (חכם שיעשה דבריו בערמה). אַף כִּי shows that other talk came first (יורה כי דבר דברים אחרים). The serpent does not say the Name because he did not know it (לא הזכיר הנחש השם הנכבד … כי לא ידעו).

Commentarynote · 2 traditional

יֹדֵעַ, 'knows', a participle. וְנִפְקְחוּ, 'will be opened', from פָּקַח, the verb used only of eyes and ears. כֵּאלֹהִים: אֱלֹהִים is plural in form, and the participle that follows, יֹדְעֵי, is plural; the English gives 'the divine ones'. The LXX has ὡς θεοί, 'like gods'.

Readings held open

  • כֵּאלֹהִים: like divine beings, like gods, or like Divinity. The plural participle יֹדְעֵי favours a plural.

Rashi רש״י

'Like Divinity' means 'makers of worlds' (יוֹצְרֵי עוֹלָמוֹת), from Bereshit Rabbah.

Ibn Ezra אבן עזרא

'Like angels' (כמלאכים).

Commentarynote · 2 traditional

Readings held open

  • מִתְהַלֵּךְ: the voice moving, or the Lord God walking.
  • לְרוּחַ הַיּוֹם: at the windy time of the day, toward evening; or toward the quarter of the wind.

Rashi רש״י

The walking is that of the Holy One (קוֹל הַקָּבָּ"ה שֶׁהָיָה מִתְהַלֵּךְ בַּגָּן). 'The wind of the day' is the direction to which the sun goes, the west: toward evening.

Ibn Ezra אבן עזרא

A voice can walk (קולה כנחש ילך). The time is near evening, when the wind of the day blows (סמוך לערב בעת התנופף רוח היום).
