English · this edition

This is the root of the ancient word, here — Quiche is its name.

The first two lines of the manuscript, as its two columns give them, with this edition’s English.

Note on the text

This translation was made directly from the K'iche' text of Ayer MS 1515, Father Francisco Ximénez’s early-eighteenth-century transcription of an older K'iche' document, now lost. The manuscript (Newberry Library, Chicago; digitized by the Library of Congress) sets K'iche' and Ximénez’s own contemporary Spanish in parallel columns across fifty-six folios. Working text and paragraph citations come from the Multepal Project’s diplomatic transcription.

Ximénez’s Spanish is consulted only as an aid for obscure vocabulary, never as the object of translation. No modern published English translation was consulted. Gaps in the manuscript are marked rather than bridged.

Prologue and the divine makers

§ 01 · Multepal p01–p05 · Ayer MS 1515, vol. 2, fol. 1r–2v

Translator’s note on § 01

Round-6 revision responding to reviewer feedback on two points. First, the opening sentence and the tinamit/nation clause echoed Dennis Tedlock's distinctive published wording ("here in this place called Quiche" and "citadel of Quiche, in the nation of the Quiche people"); these are reworded independently from the K'iche' "varal Quiche vbi" (literally "here, Quiche its name") and "tinamit" (rendered plainly as "town," as Ximenez glosses it "pueblo," rather than the Tedlock-specific "citadel"), giving "here — Quiche is its name" and "in the town of Quiche, among the Quiche people." Second, the K'iche' clause "ilbal zac petenac chaca palo" ("the instrument of sight, come from across the sea") had been dropped when the ancient book's disappearance was compressed; it is now restored in the second paragraph alongside the existing "no longer a place to see it" clause, translated directly from the K'iche' with the Spanish ("dela otra parte de el mar es venido donde se a visto") consulted as an aid. The restored earth-creation ending from the prior revision (Earth spoken into being, mist/cloud forming, crab simile, mountains, cypresses and pines, Gucumatz's rejoicing, water-courses dividing, Heart of Sky/Heart of Earth closing) and the plain, non-Recinos/Goetz-Morley rendering of the water-clearing command ("not be an obstacle to the creatures") are unchanged from the previous round, having drawn no further objection.

English

This is the root of the ancient word, here — Quiche is its name. Here we shall write, here we shall begin the ancient word, the root and the start of everything that was done in the town of Quiche, among the Quiche people. And here we shall bring out its being shown, its being made plain, its being told — the hiding, the making clear — by the one who builds, the one who gives shape, Mother and Father, whose names are Hun Ahpu Vuch, Hun Ahpu Vtiu, Great White Coati, Tepeu, Gucumatz, the Heart of the Lake, the Heart of the Sea, He of the Green Plate, He of the Green Gourd, so they are called; and named along with them, spoken of along with them, the grandmother and the grandfather, whose names are Xpiyacoc and Xmucane, protector and shelterer, twice a midwife, twice a grandfather, as they are named in the Quiche word — when they told, when they gave account of all that they did together, within the clarity that had already come to be, within the words that had already come to be told.

This we shall write now within the word of God, within Christianity; we bring it out because there is no longer a place to see it — the instrument of sight, come from across the sea, the ancient book, the original writing whose sight is hidden from the one who looks at it, from the one who ponders it. Great is its telling and its account of when the sky and the earth were finished being formed, squared, divided into four parts, measured out, when the cord was stretched across the sky and across the earth, its four corners, its four sides, as it is told — by the one who builds, the one who gives shape, mother and father of life and of what was made, who gives breath, who gives thought, who watches over the good walk of the people, of humankind, born into clarity, begotten into clarity, one who worries over, one who knows all that lies within the sky, the earth, the lake, the sea.

English

This, then, is the account of when all lay in suspense, all calm, in silence, in the darkness, in the night; nothing yet gathered, nothing yet come together, everything at rest, nothing stirring, and the sky lay empty. This is the first word, the first speech. There was not yet one person, one animal, bird, fish, crab, tree, stone, hollow, canyon, meadow, forest. Only the sky existed. The face of the earth had not yet appeared. Only the sea lay penned in, alone, and all that was of the sky. Nothing whatever was joined together, nothing went ‘mal,’ nothing went ‘cotz’ — these being sounds — in the sky. Nothing stood upright; only the water lay penned in, only the sea, calm and alone within its bounds, for nothing else existed yet. Only stillness and silence in the darkness, in the night. Only the one who builds, the one who gives shape, Tepeu, Gucumatz, Mother, Father, lay in the water, surrounded by clarity, hidden under green and blue feathers — and so they are called Gucumatz. Great sages, great thinkers were they in their being; and so it was that the sky existed, and the Heart of the Sky existed too, for that is the name of this god, as it is told.

English

Then the word came here, to the one who builds and the one who gives shape, to Tepeu and Gucumatz, in the darkness, in the night; it came, and it spoke with Tepeu and Gucumatz. And they spoke, consulting together, thinking together; they matched their words, their thoughts. Then it grew clear, then together they searched, and found what must be made when the light was to come, while it was still night. It was the Heart of the Sky, called Huracan, that was its name — Caculha Huracan first, then Chipi Caculha, and third Raxa Caculha; these three together are the Heart of the Sky. They came together, then, with Tepeu and Gucumatz, and there the light and the life were thought upon: how the sowing would be done, how the dawn would be brought forth, who would provide, who would sustain.

“Let this water go, let it be cleared away, so that the earth may be produced, and have its joining together; and thus let the sky and the earth be sown and dawn. And so it will not be an obstacle to the creatures, to what we make, when men are made,” they said, before the earth was created by them.

“Earth,” they said, and at once it was made. Like a mist, like a cloud was its forming, taking shape, when the hills appeared out of the water like a crab rising up; and in an instant the great mountains came to be. Only by a wonder, only by an act of power, was this done, this forming of the cypresses and the pines upon its face. And so Gucumatz rejoiced: “Good is your coming, Heart of Sky, you Huracan, and you Chipi Caculha, Raxa Caculha; our work, our making, will be brought to completion,” they said. First the earth was made, the hills and the flatlands; the courses of the water were divided, and the streams went their way among the hills, and the water stood apart when the great mountains appeared. Thus was the making of the earth, when it was made by the Heart of Sky, the Heart of Earth, as they are called who first made fruitful the sky and made fruitful also the earth within the water, when they gave it thought, when they gave it consideration, and brought their work to its completion.
